# Afterlight action verification

PASS in system Chromium with the actual Phaser 3.90.0 action scene. Run `node qa/afterlight/action.e2e.mjs`; run the short settings/loss check with `AFTERLIGHT_ACTION_FOCUS=1 node qa/afterlight/action.e2e.mjs`.

The browser campaign used only legal movement, autoaim fire and dodge inputs through engine updates: eighteen survivors escorted, six beacon defenses completed, 104 enemies cleared and all six guardians defeated and the final win reached. Each browser region was observed with a live undefeated guardian while still playing, then completed only after the guardian died; pure tests additionally verify that an expired timer cannot bypass that gate. Final region health ranged from 78 to 100. No player position, inventory, health or completion flags were assigned. Fourteen independent pure action tests also pass, including legal campaigns with three seeds (engine coordinator evidence).

Actual native inputs pass: Space firing, pointer aiming in world coordinates, held Fire, Shift and Dodge, arrows and phone directional controls. Pause freezes simulation and, after the source worker settles, sampled canvas pixels exactly. Page-hide and game-switch pause pass; deliberate resume works. A legal no-fire loss followed by one native retry click starts the current world with full health. Sound on/off updates without browser errors; audibility was not tested. Manual reduced-motion mode leaves essential simulation running.

The scene fills 1440×900, 390×844 and 844×390 exactly, with no horizontal or vertical document overflow. Portrait and landscape use touch-enabled browser contexts. Opening and active screenshots show accessible native buttons, visible rescue progress and physical HELP/YOU/BEACON cues. Interrupted saves resume deliberately; won saves preserve six completed regions; a new campaign resets progress. Malformed and blocked storage both permit fresh play. Action saves leave the Classic storage key intact. `/afterlight/classic.html` starts its original Phaser journey; its archived browser suites now target that route.

Normal-motion canvas comparisons sampled RGB every eight pixels, excluding all DOM HUD content. The six-region guardian run changed 22–43% of sampled pixels over two frames about 550 ms apart, across all 48 spatial tiles, with saturated color occupying 21–26% of the picture. After the final rendering optimization, fresh forest/city comparisons changed 40.1%/42.7%, occupied all 48 tiles and retained saturated color over 23.5%/23.0%. Actual Phaser image bounds and fresh screenshots confirm full-picture coverage at desktop and both phone sizes.

Final normal runtime performance checks over three seconds passed the 30-fps median requirement: forest rendered 148 frames with RAF median 16.7 ms and p95 33.4 ms; city rendered 175 frames with median 16.7 ms and p95 16.8 ms. The earlier slow/cropped rendering regression was detected visually, fixed by root and rechecked. These are isolated headless Chromium measurements, not a guarantee on physical phones.

Opening, active landscape, normal city, moon ending and latest optimized forest images were inspected: regional source scenery remains recognizable under emerald, cyan, gold and violet light; the action sprites share the dot texture; compact overlaid instruments leave the picture as the playfield. The guardian screenshot shows a prominent purple threat, health bar and explicit defeat/dodge instructions.

Evidence lives in `/tmp/afterlight-action/`: `report.json`, `focus-report.json`, `campaign-report.json`, `performance-report.json`, `opening-{1440x900,390x844,844x390}.png`, `active-{1440x900,390x844,844x390}.png`, `latest-normal-{forest,city,390x844,844x390}.png`, and `{forest,city,coast,fjord,desert,moon}-{frame-a,frame-b,guardian,restored}.png`. No uncaught page errors or local asset HTTP failures occurred.

Physical devices and browsers other than Chromium were not tested. The source texture is computed in a worker and broad decorative motion is reduced on request; no claim of a constant 60 frames per second is made.
