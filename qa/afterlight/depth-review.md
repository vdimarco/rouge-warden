# Afterlight and Battle Tanks depth review

Gameplay, perspective depth, responsive layouts and recoverable WebGL startup pass in Chromium with SwiftShader. Software rendering misses the 30-fps target; visual fidelity to the concept is partial. Run `node qa/afterlight/depth.e2e.mjs` and `node qa/battle-tanks/depth.e2e.mjs`; use `AFTERLIGHT_DEPTH_FOCUS=1` for the short appearance/performance/startup check. The old action command now imports the depth suite; Classic discovery checks the Three runtime before opening its original Phaser journey.

Afterlight's browser campaign legally broke all tethers, escorted eighteen survivors, defended six beacons and defeated six guardians. Pulses interrupted nearby warned attacks during the campaign; native E/Shift/fire responded. A sustained-fire probe overheated the weapon, rejected another shot and recovered after cooling. Final regional health ranged from 61 to 100. Eighteen pure action tests cover tactical mechanics and safe progress. No player coordinates, resources or completion flags were assigned by the browser solver.

Battle Tanks' final ranged solver legally drove the perimeter to flank relays, used shell fire, smoke and artillery, then entered extraction in three missions. Relay destruction alone left the mission playing; extraction completed it. Final health was 10, 1 and 55, and the won save reloaded correctly. Seven pure tests cover acceleration, independent turret aiming, cover blocking movement/shells, smoke, ranged artillery, repairs, saves and the requirement to leave spawn. Native driving, pointer turret aiming without hull rotation, Space, smoke/strike and pause/page-hide/switch passed.

Both games fill 1440×900, 390×844 and 844×390 without document overflow. Actual Three geometry, a PerspectiveCamera and WebGL draw calls are present. Afterlight projected a near object at 13.37 pixels per world unit and a far object at 7.29; ground projection/raycast returned the original point within floating-point tolerance. Scenery raycasts occluded 149 of 414 visible sampled points. Native traversal changed the camera position and rendered view. Disabling WebGL produced explicit startup messages and accessible Classic links; Afterlight disabled Begin and Tanks offered Retry graphics.

The final bounded-renderer SwiftShader spot check produced 56 frames in 3.05 seconds: RAF median 50 ms, p95 83.4 ms (about 18 fps). The backend identifies SwiftShader software rendering at 233×146 internal pixels; hardware GPUs retain a separate 640-pixel quality bound. This improves the initial 3.7-fps result but remains below target. Measured renderer submission was about 2.8 ms, so this result includes software GPU/compositor costs. It does not establish hardware-GPU or physical-phone performance. No uncaught page errors or local asset failures occurred during the gameplay suites.

The generated reference and actual desktop/phone images were inspected. Five comparisons matter: perspective terrain and foreground occlusion are now real; the centered golden beacon and warm/cool palette remain; fog, dark pines and cyan ground ribbons provide multiple planes; characters and threats have 3D silhouettes with projected labels; full-screen HUD and reachable circular action buttons retain the reference's composition. The result still uses simpler polygonal trees, block-shaped beacon surfaces and fewer luminous ASCII details than the reference's intricate forest, cliffs and waterfalls. The reduced internal render resolution is visibly coarse. These are material differences; the review does not claim a visual match or prove that the game is fun/balanced. Tank YOU/relay/extraction labels and radar improve identification, and the initially opaque deployed smoke cloud was corrected: fresh desktop/portrait smoke screenshots show the player hull and turret through translucent smoke.

Cabinet/switcher discovery has a separate lightweight check with `BATTLE_TANKS_DISCOVERY_ONLY=1`. Physical devices, hardware acceleration and other browsers were not tested.
