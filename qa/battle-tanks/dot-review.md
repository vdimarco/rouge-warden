# Battle Tanks luminous dot review

PASS for dot presentation, real perspective, controls and campaign mechanics in Chromium with SwiftShader. Run `node qa/battle-tanks/dot.e2e.mjs`; the short final appearance/input/stress check uses `BATTLE_TANKS_DOT_FOCUS=1`. Software performance remains limited; no constant 30-fps claim is made.

The legal browser campaign completed all three missions through driving, aiming, fire, smoke and ranged artillery, followed by required extraction. Final health was 52/32/52 and score 7375. No player coordinates, resources or completion flags were assigned. Stationary updates generated zero charge; actual travel generated 100 charge, and the next shot consumed it for double damage and one penetration. Relay destruction produced a repair pickup, five-second overdrive, a restoration pulse and persistent wrecks. Destroyed IDs were unique, relay destruction alone did not complete a mission, and the won save reloaded. Eleven pure engine tests independently cover these consequences, enemy roles, cover/range limits and safe saves. The existing depth suite's legal flank bot remains compatible with the additive schema.

Desktop 1440×900, portrait 390×844 and landscape 844×390 fit without document overflow. Actual W movement, camera tracking, independent pointer turret aiming, Space, pause and page-hide pass. The final focused run also passes a ground projection/raycast round trip, trusted touch driving/cancellation/firing and sound-button state changes without errors; sound audibility was not tested. Canvas-only native driving/turning changed 17.3% of sampled pixels across 38 of 48 tiles. Paused samples changed zero pixels.

The renderer uses three real Three.js point batches and zero solid geometry. The final scene contains about 16.8k points in three draw calls. A render-only stress fixture with the stock desert mission, all live gun warnings, forty shells and twelve impacts retained all critical geometry: 6069 actor points and zero dropped/essential-dropped points. This fixture tests rendering capacity, not campaign play. Final reward imagery had luminous pixels over 4.84% of the internal picture, distributed across 47 of 48 tiles; 89.9% of these bright pixels had a high-contrast immediate edge. These measurements accompany image inspection rather than substitute for it.

The original generated reference and actual desktop, reward and portrait captures were inspected. Genuine dotted tank hulls/turrets, trees, terrain and cover replace black solid slabs. Gold player marks contrast with rose/violet enemies; cyan mist streams, bright branches, horizon particles and foreground ferns provide depth layers. Relay wrecks and local restoration rings visibly change the battlefield, with readable overdrive feedback. Native labels, radar and touch controls remain legible. The result is materially closer to the requested luminous dot aesthetic, while simpler and sparser than the reference's elaborate cliffs, waterfalls and dramatic atmosphere; it is not an exact match. Afterlight's renderer and tests were not changed.

Final isolated software rendering recorded 55 frames over 3.02 seconds, RAF median 50 ms and p95 100.1 ms (about 18 fps). Renderer submission was about 0.6 ms at 640×400 internal resolution. Hardware GPUs use a separate 960-pixel bound; hardware acceleration, physical phones and other browsers were not tested. No uncaught page errors or local asset HTTP failures occurred.
