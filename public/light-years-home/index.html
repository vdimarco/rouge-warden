How it works

Play

  • Each stop gives you a clue. Three rings, A, B and C, mark what you might be looking for. Open a ring's card to read about it, then set course.
  • Right on the first lock scores the most. A wrong lock tells you what you found and warms your shield for the next flight.
  • In flight, hold to push toward light and let go to ease off. The orange notch is the speed limit of the space around you. Above it the shield heats up. At full heat a pane cracks. Lose all three panes and the journey ends.
  • Earth's clock races while you fly. Each chapter you finish gives back one pane and saves your place.

Real

  • Every named object, where it is and how far away it is.
  • The length of the route: 1,215,266,893 light-years.
  • Both clocks, yours and Earth's.
  • How far short of light you fly, down to light − 2.5 nanometres per second.
  • The gas in each zone of space, each with a source.
  • The two things that heat the shield: gas atoms striking it, and the cosmic background bunched up ahead of you.
  • How the sky bends forward, turns blue and brightens ahead as you speed up.
  • The years radio takes to reach you from Earth.
  • The dates of Earth's history on the "On Earth" cards.

Made up

  • The shield's rating: 1 terawatt per square metre.
  • The drive starts and stops at once and needs no fuel.
  • On the long legs between clusters, the autopilot threads unnamed galaxies to keep its lock. A real straight path would almost never meet one.
  • The time-lapse rate on screen.
  • The picture shows the sky as it would look at up to 0.986 of light speed. The ship goes far faster: at its real speed the whole sky would fit inside a dot far smaller than a pixel.
  • The sky between named objects is generated.
  • Time spent choosing a landmark does not count on either clock.
  • The dust in the asteroid belt is an estimate.
  • The expansion of the universe is left out. That changes the numbers by less than 4 %.
  • Everything is drawn where it is now.
