    How to play

    1 of 6

    Twelve turns carry the world from 2026 to 2100. A step can be 6 years or 10. The longer step moves the climate more.

    Each turn, play one breakthrough, invest in one technology pathway, or hold. Three cards are dealt, plus the Idea Lab when it is open. A pathway takes the whole turn and spends the same limited resources.

    Warming chases a target set by emissions and the land. It does not jump to the target. You always see the heat now and the heat projected for 2100.

    The path draws the heat so far and the line if you hold. The shade is seven quiet policies: hold, cut, grow, restore, power, trust, or the first card you can pay. Future news is not in that band. Pathways you already started stay in the line. The mark is 2.0°, where heat starts to drag prosperity.

    The energy race runs every step. Demand grows with the economy, electrification, and new loads such as AI. Clean supply is solar, wind, nuclear, storage, and grids. Efficiency in buildings, vehicles, and industry lowers how much new generation you need. Rules, a political surplus, and cheaper clean power retire fossil plants. You are ahead when clean additions plus efficiency exceed demand and fossil generation is falling. Grids and long-duration storage both need to be through pilot before more of that clean supply counts. Otherwise some of it is curtailed.

    Aviation fuels and shipping fuels cut hard-to-abate emissions. Heavy industry cuts industrial stacks. Carbon removal raises removal and the land sink. Each pathway moves through research, pilot, and scale. Some tries stall, and you still pay. Effects stay bounded. The heat is still a stock with lag.

    Costly energy drags prosperity and stirs resistance. Growth pushes emissions unless you break that link. A living landscape stores carbon. Low trust softens a law and raises its political price.

    The Idea Lab is one wild card every three turns. There is no reroll. A card you cannot pay for stays dim.

    Real-world context, not a score from this game. Under current policies, the UNEP Emissions Gap report estimates roughly 2.8°C of warming this century. That implies materially higher risks from heat, flooding, food disruption, migration, infrastructure damage, and other stresses that can pile up. The IPCC AR6 WGII finds that climate change can increase conditions associated with violent conflict, particularly where poverty, weak government, food insecurity, and existing tensions are already present. It rates that link medium confidence. It does not say climate change automatically causes wars. The outcome is not predetermined.

    Ember reports that in 2025 electricity demand rose by 849 TWh while low-carbon generation rose by 887 TWh, so fossil generation edged down. That is context for the race, not a figure this game produces.

    The run ends in an alternate history, with the timeline of what you chose. A hard ending is interacting stress, not a scripted war.
