# Plateau replica streetscape

Not yet imported or verified on the host. Everything here was generated and checked on the CPU, without Unreal and without a GPU. The host import, the fresh-load verify, the curb-parking, car-collision and traffic checks, the captures and the frame-time profile are all still open.

This folder holds the layers that make the La Fontaine map read as the Plateau at street level. Every layer sits in its own folder with a `manifest.json` that uses one schema: `layer`, `generator`, `frame`, `batches[]` (`file`, `mesh`, `material`, `collision`, `triangles`, `bounds_cm`, `sha256`), `materials{}` (`base_color_srgb`, `roughness`, `metallic`, optional `texture` and `emissive_*`), `counts` and `source_sha256`. The frame is the existing map frame: x east, y north, z up, centimetres (`prepare_lafontaine.xy`). The world is mirrored (see `openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/design.md`), and these layers keep that frame so they line up with gameplay.

| Layer | Generator | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Streets/` | `Scripts/plateau_replica.py` | Carriageways cut to curb lines measured from the mapped sidewalks, Québec paint, grey concrete sidewalks with an 18 cm curb, parterres to the building line with low iron fences, tree pits, curb extensions round the mapped street trees the carriageway covers (`street_trees.json`, with the trees left for the host), ruelles paved from fence to fence with a weedy edge, bike-track bollards |
| `Furniture/` | `Scripts/plateau_replica.py` | `placements.json` only: lamps, hydrants, ARRÊT signs, white street-name signs, one-way and parking signs, BIXI docks with bikes, benches, bins, mail boxes |
| `Soul/` | `Scripts/plateau_replica.py` | Seeded wear and life: asphalt patches, sealed cracks, potholes, leaf drifts, weeds, rust streaks, original colour-block murals, ruelle wires; the lane life (gallery laundry, gallery props, gate props, ruelles vertes, tags, see *Lane life*); `placements.json` for cones, the chantier fence, utility poles, curb and gate bins, flower boxes and locked bikes, and the gallery props (patio chairs, potted plants, barbecues, bikes, bins, window AC units, rail planters, satellite dishes); `lane_life.json` with every lane-life record; and the committed `ruelles_vertes_clip.json` (the city's ruelles vertes over the district) |
| `Plex/` | `Scripts/plateau_plex.py` | Plex fronts on the plain buildings (see `Plex/` files) |
| `Props/` | `Scripts/blender/plateau_props.py` | The prop meshes that Furniture and Soul instance (see `Props/README.md`) |

The OBJ files are generated and git-ignored (`ReplicaGeometry/**/*.obj` in `games/nord-unreal/.gitignore`). The manifests, placements and READMEs are committed. Generation is deterministic: two builds are byte-identical, and every OBJ hash is in its manifest.

## Regenerate and check (any machine, stdlib Python)

From `games/nord-unreal`:

```bash
python3 Scripts/plateau_replica.py         # about 30 s: Streets, Furniture, Soul
python3 Scripts/test_plateau_replica.py    # about 2.5 min: builds twice into temp dirs, then checks
python3 Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py --dry-run   # the import plan, from the manifests only
```

Run `plateau_plex.py` and the Blender props build first if those layers are missing; the replica layers read `Props/manifest.json` for the BIXI slot positions, the utility-pole wire anchors, the locked-bike lean point and the prop bounds, and fall back to the agreed defaults when it is absent. The Soul layer's lane life reads the Plex yard records in `Plex/fronts.json` (galleries and their props floor, guards, flights, fences, gates, sheds, pads, yard trees), the Plex layer's `Glass` batches (through `Plex/manifest.json`, for the rear windows the AC units sit in) and `Soul/ruelles_vertes_clip.json`; without `fronts.json` it is skipped, and without the clip a seeded 30% of the ruelles is green. Rerun `plateau_replica.py` whenever `plateau_plex.py` rewrites `fronts.json` or its batches, or the props build rewrites `Props/manifest.json`: the laundry anchors, gallery props, gate props, beds and tags follow their records, and the manifests hash all three. Rebuild the clip from the city's file with `python3 Scripts/plateau_replica.py --clip-ruelles ruelles-vertes.geojson`; the build reads only the clip. A build takes about 30 s, the test about 2.5 minutes.

`test_plateau_replica.py` checks:
- **Output:** byte-identical across two builds; the manifest schema and every OBJ hash, triangle count and bound; no triangle that collapses in float32.
- **Heights:** asphalt at z 7.0, paint 0.1–0.2 cm above it; sidewalk tops at 18 cm, ramps and dropped curbs aside.
- **Wall orientation:** every sidewalk top faces up; vertical faces point out of their slab and curb faces toward the road.
- **Traffic band:** no collision within 300 cm of any of the 663 traffic segments. This covers each colliding prop's MAP-PROPS collider boxes, not just its base point. Traffic curbs are at least 540 cm from their centreline.
- **Widths:** curb-to-curb per street; the plex streets stay inside the spec's 10–12.5 m.
- **OSM parity:** one stop bar and one ARRÊT sign per mapped stop on the network; painted crosswalks against the styled OSM crossings; lamps and hydrants one-to-one with the OSM nodes; street-name text from OSM names.
- **Legacy dashes:** the old `OSM Marking` dashes that no replica asphalt covers are re-authored.
- **Props:** BIXI bikes sit exactly in the MAP-PROPS slots.
- **Apply plan:** emissive materials, asphalt duplicating `M_Asphalt`, tints within range, colliding instances only on meshes that get box collision, colour variants, the always-cook entry and the native instancing class.
- **Soul:** every ingredient is present, and no two blocks repeat a bin arrangement.
- **Lane life:** every laundry line starts within 6 cm of the gallery guard it names, at its cap rail, runs over the lot's own yard (never past the fence, over the ruelle or over a shed), keeps 60 cm from every wall along its length, rises at least 30 cm to its clothesline pole, ends at least 2.2 m over the lane fence (the washing hangs up to 1.1 m under it), and its pole stands at least 1 m from every utility pole and outside the traffic band, 85% or more of the poles within 70 cm of the back fence; from the `ruelle_mentana` preview camera at least one line is in sight (a quarter of its points, over 60 px), by a ray cast through the Plex, Streets and Soul triangles, the props' boxes and the yard trees' species meshes, every masked card counted as solid; every gallery prop stands on its deck's props floor (`decks[].free`), or hangs from a drawn rear window (AC units), on the outside of the outer guard (planters) or on a guard cap facing the satellite (dishes), never in front of a gallery door or at a flight's head, hung props clear of the flights and the laundry pulleys, no two overlapping, colliders on the deck; every gate prop clears the 300 cm band, every gate's swing zone, the buildings and the other props; the ruelles vertes match the clip, with beds, plants, arches whose posts clear the buildings, two planters per arch and a mural on at least 70% of them; tags lie on a lane fence (clear of the gate) or a shed or garage door, facing the ruelle. Fixtures check that murals and tags face out of their wall, card UVs, interval cuts and the seeded fallback share; that a pole stands at the fence of a synthetic yard (or beside its shed, the line never over the roof) with the line rising to it; and that 300 seeded decks of a synthetic gallery with a door, two windows, a flight beside the outer guard and a pulley keep every prop in its place and turn up every kind.
- **Street trees:** no mapped trunk (the 4,571 `plateau_rendering.tree_positions`, checked one to one) stands on carriageway asphalt in the Streets OBJs: each of the 648 the carriageway covers within 3 m of its curb has a curb extension over it, and the other 16 are exactly the `host_review` rows of `street_trees.json`, each more than 3 m out. Extensions merge only where no parked car fits between them, two separate ones on a curb leave at least 5.4 m, and they take less than 15% of the parking curb (12.4% now).
- **Ruelles:** sampled every 2 m along every ruelle, nothing is bare 2.55 m off the centreline (6,396 samples outside buildings), the paving reaches the 2.9 m fence line wherever no wall stands nearer (5,866 samples), no paving or edge piece runs more than 20 m along its lane, the weedy edge lines at least half the ruelle length per side, and lane-side gate props stand on the paving.
- **Curb to building line:** on Mentana, Fabre, Rachel, Papineau, Sherbrooke and Napoléon, every metre of curb, every 20 cm out to a building up to 15 m behind the sidewalk, lies on sidewalk, parterre, walk or ruelle paving; at most 0.1% of the samples per street and 30 in all may miss (4 of 135,123 now, see *Limits*).
- **Planting:** the parterres use the shared `Yard_Grass` and `Yard_Soil` keys, the grass spill, verge and pit soil take the lawn and soil sets through their texture path, and every face of the ruelle-verte perennials and shrubs is a vertical card or a flat leaf card on the creeper set.
- **Open steps:** every outer edge of a raised sidewalk, curb extension, apron, parterre, walk or pit top that stands more than 2.5 cm over the ground just in front of it (a dropped apron, the foot of a ramp, the carriageway or bare floor; buildings and the park aside) has a wall under it. At most 50 of about 184,000 outer edges may miss (41 now, see *Limits*), none 2 m long, and none 1 m long within 25 m of a capture street's centreline.
- **Budgets:** triangle and batch limits.

## Import on the host (editor Python)

`Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py` is additive and refuses to run twice: it stops if any actor label starts with `Plateau replica ` or if `/Game/Plateau/Replica` exists. Run it in a fresh `-NullRHI` editor with the saved map loaded and no unsaved packages:

```bash
ue_root=/home/von/.local/share/Epic/UE_5.8.3
cd games/nord-unreal
python3 Scripts/plateau_replica.py && python3 Scripts/test_plateau_replica.py
backup="$(pwd)/Saved/Recovery/plateau-replica-$(date +%Y%m%d-%H%M%S)"
"$ue_root/Engine/Binaries/Linux/UnrealEditor-Cmd" "$(pwd)/Quebec.uproject" /Game/Maps/LaFontaine \
  -NullRHI -Unattended -NoP4 -NoSound -ExecutePythonScript="$(pwd)/Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py" \
  -NordPlateauReplicaBackup="$backup"
# Verify in a new process with the same backup path:
"$ue_root/Engine/Binaries/Linux/UnrealEditor-Cmd" "$(pwd)/Quebec.uproject" /Game/Maps/LaFontaine \
  -NullRHI -Unattended -NoP4 -NoSound -ExecutePythonScript="$(pwd)/Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py" \
  -NordPlateauReplicaBackup="$backup" -NordPlateauReplicaVerify
```

Add `-NordPlateauReplicaDryRun` (no backup needed) to log the plan without changing anything. The log lines to look for are `PLATEAU_REPLICA_OK` and `PLATEAU_REPLICA_PLAN`.

What the apply step does:

- **Preflight.** Checks the OBJ hashes against the manifests, the OSM source hash, the base materials, the required editor APIs, the native class `/Script/Quebec.QuebecReplicaInstances` and the fresh backup path inside `Saved/Recovery`. The Quebec module must be rebuilt with `Source/Quebec/QuebecReplicaInstances.h/.cpp` before the apply step.
- **Backup.** Copies `Content/Maps/LaFontaine.umap` and its sidecars into the backup folder, with `original-actors.json` and `backup-manifest.json`.
- **Materials.** It never rebuilds an existing `M_*`; rebuilding strips the NordSky wet-street and night wrappers. Each manifest material becomes `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Materials/M_Replica_<Layer>_<key>`, a duplicate of an existing parent with its tint set. The tint is the parent's only `Constant3Vector` that is not a NordSky wrapper node; the rain pass adds a flat-normal one to `M_Asphalt`. Tints may reach 8× the texture mean, enough for buff brick `#CCB07F`.
  - Asphalt duplicates `M_Asphalt`, keeping the world-space texture and the wet wrapper. Its tint is the target colour divided by the texture mean.
  - Ground surfaces (sidewalk, curb, yard, patches) duplicate `M_ConcreteDark`, which keeps the wet wrapper.
  - Brick duplicates `M_BrickRed`.
  - Emissive prop materials (`emissive_srgb`, `emissive_strength` in `Props/manifest.json`: the 3000 K lamp lens, the park globe, the BIXI screen, the amber flasher and the light-bar lenses) duplicate the emissive-capable `M_Glow`. The duplicate's emissive becomes colour × an `EmissiveStrength` scalar parameter. That product replaces the tint on the NordSky `WindowGlow` multiply, so night glow is kept. A parent without that wrapper gets `plateau_atmosphere.night_window`.
  - Everything else duplicates `M_Trim`.
  - If a parent does not have exactly one tint node, a fresh constant material with the same colour, roughness and metallic is created instead.
- **Meshes.** Each batch is imported with the `upgrade_plateau.import_mesh` options into `/Game/Plateau/Replica/<Layer>/SM_<batch>`, with Nanite on (exact fallback), 64 Lumen cards, and complex-as-simple collision on collision batches. Each prop's main part (pole, post, body...) gets the `colliders_cm` boxes from `Props/manifest.json` as simple box collision. Props land in `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Props`, where `QuebecLife.cpp` looks for the companions. That folder is in `DirectoriesToAlwaysCook` (`Config/DefaultGame.ini`), so the packaged game contains the dogs, strollers and bags.
- **Actors.** One `StaticMeshActor` per batch, labelled `Plateau replica <Layer> <batch>`, in the `Replica/<Layer>` folder, at identity. Collision batches get `BlockAll`; the others get `NoCollision`. Paint, decals and flats have shadows off.
- **Instances.** Furniture and Soul placements become one `AQuebecReplicaInstances` actor per prop part, colour variant and collision class. The class is new in `Source/Quebec/QuebecReplicaInstances.h/.cpp`; its root is a static `HierarchicalInstancedStaticMeshComponent`, like `AQuebecParkInstances`, which the `upgrade_plateau.py` guard rejects on the live map. Cull distance is 90–120 m. The variant material is picked per placement from its seed. Only the main part of a colliding placement gets `BlockAll`.
- **Street names.** Black `TextRenderActor`s on both faces of each blade, at the `text_slots` of `Props/manifest.json`, with the small generic over the large specific. If a text renders mirrored, turn it 180° about Z (see `Props/README.md`).
- **One existing actor changes.** `OSM Marking` is hidden in game, because its old centre dashes would poke through the new asphalt. Its collision and transform are unchanged, and the change is recorded in `apply-report.json`. The Streets layer re-authors the 41 dashes that no replica asphalt covers (the Panet/Visitation route roads), so those roads keep their centre line.
- **Save and report.** Saves the level and writes `apply-report.json` to the backup folder. `verify` reloads in a fresh process and writes `verify-report.json`. It checks:
  - every pre-existing actor against `original-actors.json`: only the hidden flag of `OSM Marking` may change, and every new actor carries the replica prefix;
  - every actor's mesh path, triangle count (within 0.1 %), Nanite, collision profile and material;
  - every instanced actor's count, variant material and collision profile, and the box collision on colliding meshes;
  - asphalt and ground duplicates still carry the texture and the `Wetness` wrapper, and emissive duplicates their `EmissiveStrength` and `WindowGlow`;
  - the street-name text count and the hidden `OSM Marking`.

### Recovery

1. Close the editor.
2. Copy `<backup>/Content/Maps/LaFontaine.*` back over `Content/Maps/`.
3. Delete `Content/Plateau/Replica/`.
4. Confirm with `Scripts/verify_plateau.py` on a fresh load.

A failed apply leaves its partial state and `apply-report.json` with the error in the backup folder. Restore before retrying, because the script refuses to run over existing replica actors or assets.

## Rules the Streets layer keeps (runtime couplings)

- **Road meshes.** Road surfaces are `SM_Asphalt_Replica_*` (carriageways) and `SM_Asphalt_Replica_Alley_*` (ruelles), with no collision. `QuebecGameplay.cpp` promotes `SM_Asphalt*` to Visibility-only at BeginPlay, so cars and people still stand on the z 0 floor. The asphalt is at z 7.0, 1 cm over `OSM Road`; ruelles are at z 6.8.
- **Sidewalks.** Sidewalks, corner rings and the raised curb extensions are `SM_Concrete_Collision_Replica_*` with complex collision and the top at 18.0 cm, inside the 17–25 cm curb-parking window. Curb ramps drop to 9 cm within 1.1 m of each crossing. Where a building stands inside the sidewalk band (narrow streets such as Napoléon), the sidewalk runs from the curb to its wall instead of stopping.
- **Curb extensions and parking.** An extension's raised edge is a curb like any other, so runtime curb parking (`CurbParking` samples the curb at −235, 0 and +235 cm along the car) does not park on one, and `CanFitAt` keeps cars off its collision. Extensions closer than 5.4 m of clear curb merge, so the curb between separate ones still takes a car.
- **Curb distance.** The nearest curb of every traffic segment is at least 550 cm from its centreline; runtime parking needs 540 cm. Measured offsets are clamped up to that, so Mentana is 11.0 m from curb to curb instead of about 10 m. Residential offsets are capped at 625 cm, so plex streets stay within the spec's 12.5 m: Fabre is 12.3 m and Garnier 12.5 m.
- **Traffic band.** No collision lies within 300 cm of any traffic segment. Sidewalk pieces that would intrude, for example at driveways or at ruelles in the traffic graph, become visual-only dropped-curb aprons at z 9, and so does any part of a curb extension in the band (18 of them) or across a crossing ramp (3); a parterre slab in the band is laid in the non-colliding lawn, soil or curb key. Props whose base point or collider boxes reach into the band keep their position but lose collision; the ones that sat in a carriageway are moved to the curb first.
- **Curb faces face the street.** Curb faces, back walls and end walls are wound outward from each slab. With one-sided materials, the 18 cm curb is therefore visible from the road.
- **Visual-only geometry.** Paint, bollards (flexible, as on the real street), fences, tree pits, the ruelle paving and its weedy edge, and every Soul mesh have no collision. Parterre yards are collision slabs at 18 cm, so feet do not sink into the lawn. The mapped trees keep no collision, as before; the curb extension round a trunk is what a car meets.

## Method and sources

- **Curb lines.** Every drive way inside the district is clipped to the boundary. Junctions are kept, and the polylines between them are simplified, because OSM splits ways at every crossing node. Every 6 m a perpendicular ray finds the parallel `footway=sidewalk` and the nearest façade. The curb offset per way side is the median sidewalk-centre offset minus half the sidewalk width: 1.9 m residential, 2.6–3.2 m arterial. The offset is capped by class, so a park promenade is not taken for roadway. Unmeasured sides use class defaults. Results: Rachel 14.0 m, Papineau 17.1, Sherbrooke 21.0, Fabre 12.3, Garnier 12.5, Mentana 11.0.
- **Junctions.** Arms are sorted by angle. Each corner gets a curb fillet: 3.6 m on residential corners and 5.2 m on arterials, shortened on short arms. The sidewalk fills to the sharp property corner. Near-straight joins use a mitre. Junction pavement is a star polygon around the node.
- **Paint** (Québec, *Marques sur la chaussée*):
  - Yellow centre line on two-way streets, double where there are 4 or more lanes; white lane lines.
  - White parking edge lines where OSM tags parking lanes and on arterials.
  - White 40 cm stop bars at the mapped `highway=stop` nodes and on signalised approaches.
  - Crosswalks: white blocks or two white lines where signals or stops control the crossing, yellow blocks on uncontrolled marked crossings. A two-line crosswalk is generated at a mapped stop with no crossing mapped near it.
  - Green conflict zones and white bike symbols on on-street cycle tracks. 1.16 m green `VC-BA-1` bollards every 4 m on the traffic side of the Rachel, Brébeuf and Cherrier tracks.
  - Dash lengths and widths are estimates, because MTQ Tome V was not retrieved.
- **Ground detail.** Sidewalks have 1.5 m saw-cut joints and a light 15 cm curb band. Manholes and storm drains are placed per block. Parterres are cut per 7.6 m lot. The building line is sampled every 60 cm along the lot, so a parterre reaches a stepped or set-back façade (up to 15 m behind the sidewalk; 2.5 m where no building is that close) and stops 3 cm short of it, at a street, another sidewalk, the park or a ruelle's paving; past a ruelle met at a slant it fills the ground to the next wall. Each lot gets lawn or a soil bed (the shared `Yard_Grass` and `Yard_Soil` keys, textured as the Plex yards are) and a walkway; plex streets get low picket fences with a gate gap, along the parterre only (never across a ruelle mouth). Lawn edges spill jittered grass over the sidewalk edge, so the seam is not a ruler line, but not where the sidewalk below drops to an apron or a ramp, where a tuft would float. Beside a ruelle mouth met at a slant the parterre fills the wedge up to the paving. Tree pits sit at the OSM tree nodes that fall on a sidewalk, with an iron frame and a low hooped fence on some; a pit that would hang over the carriageway where a short strip's curb and back lines converge at a junction is left out (4), and its trunk stands on the sidewalk.
- **Side passages.** Where the building line steps back at least 1.5 m for at most 9 m between two façades (the passage between two plexes that leads to the yards), a concrete walk runs from the parterre to where the passage opens out, a Plex yard, a ruelle, a street or a building, at most 30 m back. It is laid in 25 cm rows across the passage from a seed that follows a slanted passage, merged where the rows run straight, and a lead-in quad, found to 1 cm at both ends of the first row, closes the gap to the parterre's far edge (118 passages, about 5,700 m²). No outside technique was adapted.
- **Risers.** A raised sidewalk piece gets an end wall wherever the next piece is a dropped apron, a gap or the strip's end, and so do corner rings; a parterre or walk gets one along an edge over lower ground (an apron, the foot of a ramp, a sliver of bare floor); the part of a curb extension behind the curb gets one where no sidewalk lies under it; and a dropped apron's edge over bare floor gets a 9 cm one. This closes the gap where the old `OSM Path` showed between a sidewalk and the dropped apron at a ruelle mouth on Fabre. A sidewalk piece that a building corner cuts across is laid in six parts, so the part clear of the wall stays. The walls are the skirts that terrain renderers hang under tile edges to hide cracks (Ulrich's chunked level of detail, as the TU München terrain-rendering tutorial describes it, https://www.cs.cit.tum.de/fileadmin/w00cfj/cg/Research/Tutorials/Terrain.pdf).
- **Street trees on the carriageway.** The measured curbs, clamped out to 550 cm on traffic streets, left 664 of the 4,571 mapped trees (`plateau_rendering.tree_positions`, which the saved map instances) on the new asphalt. The trees are not moved. Each trunk within 3 m of its curb (a sidewalk strip's curb, a corner arc, or the carriageway edge of a side with no sidewalk) gets a curb extension (saillie): a trapezoid out from the curb holding the 110 x 100 cm pit and 25 cm of concrete, 90 cm deep at least, with chamfered tapers of 0.8 cm per cm of depth (at most 90 cm), an 18 cm curb face and the light curb band, which the strip's own band gives way to. On a side with no sidewalk it is a raised lawn verge reaching 40 cm behind the edge. Extensions with less than 5.4 m of clear curb between them merge, so a parked car still fits between separate ones. Parts in the 300 cm traffic band and extensions across a crossing ramp are laid flush at 9 cm without collision. The 132 trunks mapped on the sidewalk within 45 cm of the curb with no pit of their own lean over the gutter, so they get one too. A pit slides up to 35 cm along the curb, its trunk staying inside it, to stay on an extension cut short at a strip's end, and a verge reaches far enough back for the pit of a trunk at its edge. 586 extensions carry 780 trees (648 on the carriageway, 132 at the curb); 114 merge two or more, 7 are flush and 14 partly flush. `Streets/street_trees.json` lists every extension and the 16 trunks more than 3 m out (`host_review`). Sources: the City of Minneapolis *Street Design Guide*, "Curb extensions" (mid-block extensions for greening in the parking lane; 5:1 or 3:1 tapers where they guide traffic, so these short ones in the parking lane only chamfer; https://sdg.minneapolismn.gov/design-guidance/intersections/pedestrian-crossings/curb-extensions) and Ville de Montréal, *Saillies de trottoir* (mid-block and planted saillies; https://montreal.ca/articles/saillies-de-trottoir-pour-traverser-en-toute-securite-14429). No code was taken.
- **Ruelles.** Each ruelle is paved from its centreline out to the yard fence line, 290 cm (`plateau_plex.FENCE_FROM_ALLEY`), or to a wall where one stands nearer, and past the fence line out to a wall up to 6 m off the centreline where no Plex yard, fence, shed or ground, sidewalk or carriageway lies before it (a side or rear wall on the lane, a garage), sampled every metre and bracketed to 1 cm where a wall begins or ends (a wall's end keeps the wider reach for one sample, the rest lying under the building) and laid in pieces of 20 m or less, with the concrete centre gutter, a wedge on the outside of each bend and the old round cap at free ends. A thin weedy edge, 12–32 cm of crushed stone (`Yard_Gravel`) or grass, runs along the fence foot, mostly grass on the ruelles vertes, with weed tufts every few metres in the Soul layer; it stops at walls, carriageways and crossing ruelles. Ruelle-verte grass now reaches the fence. The city's ruelles vertes data lists asphalt, concrete, gravel, planting, pavers or sod as their surfaces; no outside technique was adapted for the edge, which reuses the parterres' jittered soft edge.
- **Seeds.** Every choice is seeded by SHA-256 of stable OSM ids through SplitMix64, so neighbouring blocks differ and every run repeats exactly.
- **Soul layer.** It follows the owner's reference, Alex Lingris, "City building games have a Soul Problem pt.2" (Radical Elements, 2026): wear, clutter and traces of life produced by procedural rules. One chantier zone sits in a parking lane. The murals are original compositions (split colour grids, a disc, triangles), one per 150 m at most, on exposed side walls; no existing artwork is copied. A street mural now goes only on a blank wall: where the Plex layer skins the wall with windows or a detailed front stands on it, the wall is skipped, and on a blank Plex skin the mural is painted on the skin's face (16.9 cm out) instead of 6 cm out behind it. The first murals were wound inward, so one-sided materials culled them from the street; every mural face now faces out. Rust streaks hang below the balcony line on street-facing walls; the plex stair positions were not known when they were generated.
- **Lane life** (`lane_life.json` lists every record):
  - *Laundry.* A pulley clothesline (corde à linge) hangs from a pulley on a gallery's cap rail, 1 m over the deck, near the end of the module away from its stair (or 0.45 or 0.9 m in from it), and runs out over the lot's own yard to a clothesline pole at the back fence, one pulley per floor on the pole, the loop drawn as two strands with the garments pegged on the lower one. The pole stands 22 cm in from the fence line (40 or 70 cm where the line must clear a shed's corner; 1.1 or 1.8 m only where a shed, pad, gate or tree holds the whole fence line) and is taller than the gallery it serves: each line rises 35 to 130 cm from its pulley to the pole (ending 3.3 m up at least), so the poles, the lines and the washing stand over the fences, seen from the ruelle, as they are on the Plateau (the National Film Board's stock shots of Plateau Mont-Royal back lanes, a clothesline over a lane bordered by sheds, https://archives.nfb.ca/stockshot/1590/). The old poles stood up to 1.8 m in from the fence with the lines falling to them, and no line was in sight of the ruelle camera. A line may run up to 45 degrees off square to its gallery, round a shed or past a gate. 60% of the gallery modules have a pole (30% on the steel blocks), 75% of their decks a line, and about 60% of the lines carry sheets, towels, shirts, trousers or socks (it is late October): 478 lines on 323 poles (317 at the fence line), 2,034 garments. Montréal allows a line only in the rear yard, on private land, never on a Hydro-Québec, city or other utility pole, and some boroughs forbid one across a ruelle (Ville de Montréal, *Installer une corde à linge*, https://montreal.ca/demarches/installer-une-corde-linge; the Plateau-Mont-Royal entry asks only for private land), so lines no longer hang from the utility poles or end at the masses behind the galleries. A pole stands outside the traffic band, a metre from every utility pole, clear of gates, sheds, pads, stairs and yard-tree trunks.
  - *Galleries.* A gallery is an outdoor room. On 82% of the 1,510 decks with a props floor (`decks[].free` and `ground_decks[].free` in `Plex/fronts.json`, which Plex keeps clear of stair openings, flight heads, the door and the way between them, and walks its player capsule over with the floor filled), seeded per deck: patio chairs, one or a pair, set down a little askew against the wall facing the yard (or at the rail); potted geraniums at the guard or the wall; a propane barbecue against the guard, its knobs to the house (30% of the ground and first-floor decks, 18% higher up); a bike leaning on the outer guard's cap rail by its handlebar, wheels on the props floor (20% low, 6% high); the blue and brown bins against the wall or the guard; a window AC unit sitting on the sill of a rear window over the deck, its wall plane on the glass (the windows are read from the Plex layer's `Glass` triangles, 22% of them); planters hung on the outside of the outer guard, rim at the rail; and, on 6% of the decks, a satellite dish clamped to the cap of a guard that faces the satellite (Bell 91.1 W at azimuth 204, elevation 35, three in four; Shaw Direct 107.3 W at 223 and 28; the satsig.net pointing formula, https://www.satsig.net/maps/satellite-tv-dish-pointing-canada.htm) with a clear line to it for 30 m past buildings, gallery stacks and yard-tree crowns (OMHM asks its tenants to take dishes off the guards, so they stay few). Floor props stay 2 cm inside the props floor and 6 cm from each other; hung props keep 60 cm from a straight flight's centre line and 85 cm from a spiral's, and planters 96 cm from a laundry pulley. Chairs, barbecues and bins collide (their boxes on the deck, 3 m or more from traffic); the bike, the pots and the hung props do not. Blocks of five storeys or more (steel fire escapes) get none. 3,778 props: 977 chairs, 1,570 pots, 266 barbecues, 112 bikes, 278 blue and 231 brown bins, 154 AC units, 176 planters and 14 dishes.

  - *Ruelles vertes.* The ruelles that the city's open data maps as ruelles vertes (Ville de Montréal, *Ruelles vertes*, CC BY 4.0, https://donnees.montreal.ca/dataset/ruelles-vertes; 96 Plateau-Mont-Royal ruelles in the file, 15 of them within 20 m of the district) are matched to the OSM ruelles by sampling every 2 m: 27 OSM ways, 14 ruelles.   - *Tags.* Original tags on the lane face of 16% of the lane fences (between two posts, clear of the gate and the gate props), of 25% of the shed doors and 70% of the garage doors on a lane, half as often on a ruelle verte and never on its fences; a quarter are two- or three-letter throw-ups (bubble letters, silver or white on a black outline).
- **Technique sources.** OSM2World's tag-driven road markings and Streets GL's procedural roads were adapted as ideas only; no code was taken. Junction fillets are standard curb-return geometry. The existing `plateau_streets.py` route kit was the model for the slabs, ramps and alley treatment. Lane life: the tags follow the Graffiti Markup Language's model of a tag as strokes of points with no width (F.A.T. Lab, 2010, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graffiti_Markup_Language), drawn as ribbons after one pass of Chaikin's corner cutting (Chaikin 1974; the UNC notes at https://www.cs.unc.edu/~dm/UNC/COMP258/LECTURES/Chaikins-Algorithm.pdf); the letters are original skeletons, not real tags. The creeper is the foliage-card patch the Plex layer uses (the CG Cookie foliage-card thread, https://cgcookie.com/community/11178-collab2021-wip-raisonhomme), and the perennials and shrubs are crossed foliage cards, the grass-object idea of Pelzer, *Rendering Countless Blades of Waving Grass* (GPU Gems, chapter 7, https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-7-rendering-countless-blades-waving-grass). Cut-out cards are masked and two-sided (Epic, *Material Blend Modes*). The beds, planters, arches and lane closures follow the borough's *Guide d'aménagement d'une ruelle verte* (https://portail-m4s.s3.montreal.ca/pdf/guide_ruelles_vertes_2022_final.pdf) and the dataset's own description (beds, furniture, ground marking, full or partial closure). The gallery props are laid out the way Germer and Schwarz's furniture agents furnish a room (*Procedural Arrangement of Furniture for Real-Time Walkthroughs*, Computer Graphics Forum 28(8), 2009, https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.2312/CGF.v28i8pp2068-2078): each kind seeks its parent surface (the wall, the guard, a window, the rail, a guard cap) and keeps its clearance, on the free floor Plex leaves; here one seeded pass per deck rather than agents moving over time. The test's sight check walks a uniform grid along each sight line (Amanatides and Woo, *A Fast Voxel Traversal Algorithm for Ray Tracing*, Eurographics 1987, https://www.eecs.yorku.ca/~amana/research/grid.pdf) and tests triangles with Möller and Trumbore's ray-triangle intersection (*Journal of Graphics Tools* 2(1), 1997). No code was taken from any of them.
- **Data.** `Data/LaFontaine/osm.xml` (© OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0) and the read-only traffic graph in `Data/LaFontaine/layout.json`. Ruelles vertes: Ville de Montréal, CC BY 4.0, clipped into `Soul/ruelles_vertes_clip.json` with the source, licence and the file's SHA-256. Dimensions and palette come from `research-plateau.md` (Plateau heritage sheets, the Ville de Montréal sign inventory, Québec marking rules).

## Limits

- **Mapped stops only.** OSM maps only 26 stop nodes inside the district (21 on the replica network), not the 77 of the whole snapshot. Most Plateau junctions are all-way stops in reality, but only mapped stops get ARRÊT signs and bars.
- **Missing props.** There is no traffic-signal prop yet, so the 236 signal nodes have no poles. Parking-sign text is part of the prop mesh; it is not per pole.
- **The Panet/Visitation route is skipped.** The route within 64 m of `prepare_plateau.ROUTE` keeps its own detailed streets.
- **Avenue du Parc-La Fontaine.** It has few mapped sidewalk samples, so most of its sides use the clamped default. Its park side has no sidewalk, so the 28 trees there stand in lawn verges.
- **Trees for the host** (`street_trees.json`, `host_review`): 16 mapped trunks stand more than 3 m out from the curb, so they got no extension and stay on the asphalt: four on Rue Saint-Christophe and one on Roy Est 3.0–3.3 m out and 2.6–3.0 m from the traffic centreline (the clamped curb sits 1.5–2 m past the real one), seven on Rue De Champlain about 3.5 m out (its mapped sidewalk lies far from the road), one on Marie-Anne Est, and three within 70 cm of a traffic centreline (one on Rachel near the park, two on Avenue du Parc-La Fontaine), which look like a median or a mapping error. Check each against the street and move the tree node or narrow the curb.
- **Coverage residue.** On the capture streets, 4 of 135,123 curb-to-building samples miss, all within 35 cm of a building line: three on Papineau, in slivers a few cm wide where a ruelle's weedy edge or a façade corner meets the parterre at a slant, and one in a 2 cm wedge at a building corner 14.5 m back on Rachel.
- **Open steps.** 41 of about 184,000 raised edges still have no wall over lower ground: eight edges of four tree pits on extensions cut short at a junction, where the trunk stands at or past the strip's end, so the pit cannot slide onto the extension; parterre slabs in the traffic band beside an apron; curb-band ends over a ramp; and slivers a few cm wide by a parterre, a ruelle or an apron. The longest is a 1.7 m parterre edge over the foot of a ramp. Five lie within 25 m of a capture street, none longer than 60 cm.
- **Parking.** Curb extensions take 12.4% of the parking curb's length.
- **No terrain.** The ground is flat; curb faces drop to z 0.
- **Mirror fix.** When the mirror fix lands, negate y in every batch and placement. For prop instances, negate only position y and yaw, never scale (see `Props/README.md`).
- **Text only in the editor.** The street-name `TextRender`s are not visible in the top-down CPU previews.
- **Lane life.** Laundry hangs only from Plex galleries over Plex yards: 191 modules drawn to have a pole find no room (a yard under 2.6 m past the gallery, or a shed, pad, gate, tree or wall in the way). On the Mentana and Saint-André ruelle the nearest lots have yards too shallow or a shed at the fence, so the nearest line in sight of the preview camera is 29.5 m away (an empty one; a line with six garments is in sight at 42 m); a 0.9 cm rope that far is under a pixel wide, so it is the poles and the washing that read. The preview script still draws the yard trees on the old dense shared tree mesh, which hides more of these lines than the game's species meshes do. 221 drawn gate props find no room, most on ruelles in the traffic graph, where the lane side is inside the 300 cm band. Garments are flat double-faced cards in the plane of the line, so from along the line they read edge-on. The plants are crossed foliage cards and grass tufts, and the ruelle-verte beds stop at gates, sheds and gate props. Gate props, plants, planters, arches, laundry and the gallery bikes, pots, AC units, planters and dishes have no collision. AC units sit in the windows Plex draws flat on the wall (the wall is not cut), so the unit's 4 cm inside part ends in the skin, and its side panels (92 cm across) stand a few cm past the 80 cm window frame. A small cedar the Plex layer may plant is not in its records, so yard-side gate props keep clear of everywhere one can stand.
