# Textures: replica surface sets

`python3 Scripts/plateau_textures.py` writes this folder: 24 texture sets of three PNG maps each (23 tileable, one garment atlas), one macro-variation mask (`T_Replica_Macro_mask.png`), and `textures.json` (sizes, tile sizes, UV tiling, mean linear colour, mean roughness, the material each set needs, the shared material keys that take it, import settings, and two SHA-256 hashes per map). `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_textures.py` checks it, and `python3 Scripts/plateau_textures.py --check` compares the folder with a fresh build. The generator uses only the standard library and writes the same pixels on every run and platform: sines and cosines come from a fixed polynomial, not the C library. The sets build in parallel worker processes (about 30 s on four cores, about two minutes on one); the output does not depend on it. The folder is about 51 MB. The compressed PNG bytes can differ with the zlib build, so the apply step checks `raw_sha256`, the hash of the decompressed image stream.

## Macro variation

`T_Replica_Macro_mask.png` (512², linear) holds three independent tileable noises, each stretched to the full range and decorrelated from the ones before it. The sets with a `macro` entry in `textures.json` (membrane, ballast, tar, crushed stone, buff brick, lane concrete, leaf litter) get it in the apply step: the tint is multiplied by `lerp(1 - s, 1 + s, R)` with R sampled at a 17.3 m tile and by `lerp(1 - 0.6 s, 1 + 0.6 s, G)` with G at 5.3 m, where s is the set's strength (0.05 for buff brick to 0.16 for tar). Neither tile is a whole multiple of any set's tile, so the pattern never lines up with the detail and a 49 m roof shows no grid. This is the macro-variation approach of Epic's landscape materials (the Starter Content `T_MacroVariation` texture sampled at large, unrelated scales), on a generated mask.

## Maps and UVs

- `diff` is sRGB base colour. The material multiplies it by the target colour divided by `mean_linear`, capped at 8. The sets made to be painted (siding, parging, sheet metal, balusters, lattice, fence boards, pavers) are near-neutral, so any paint colour works. The others carry their own colour; `base_color_srgb` in `textures.json` is the texture's mean, and a manifest colour near it gives a tint of about 1. A set with `own_colour` (tar) keeps a white tint whatever the manifest colour, and a set with `metallic` (sheet metal) caps the manifest's metallic at it (`textured_tint` and `textured_metallic` in the apply step).
- The cut-out sets (`balusters`, `lattice`, `fence_boards`, `creeper`, `leaf_litter` and the other masked sets) have an RGBA `diff`: A is the opacity mask, anti-aliased about the 0.5 clip, and `coverage` in `textures.json` is the share of texels over it. Their means count only those texels. Under the holes the colour is the surface's own, so filtering never pulls a dark halo into an edge.
- `nor_dx` is a tangent-space normal map in the DirectX convention (green down), like the Poly Haven `_nor_dx` maps in `Data/Art`.
- `rough` is linear: R is roughness, the channel the brick-style material reads. G is height and B is cavity (1 in joints, gaps and holes).
- The macro mask is imported with `TC_MASKS` and sampled as masks; it wraps both ways.
- Image row 0 is the top of the tile. Replica OBJ UVs are in metres (u along the face and v = z on walls; u = x and v = y on roofs and the ground), so the tiling is 1 / tile size.
- Cards (`uv: "card"`): u is metres along the run and v runs from 0 at the bottom of the card to 1 at its top, so the v tiling is 1 and the texture spans the card once. `card_height_m` is the height it was drawn for; a much shorter or taller card squashes or stretches the drawing (the lattice diamonds most). The apply step clamps V (`address_y = TA_CLAMP`) on all three maps of a card set: the top row of the baluster card is opaque and the bottom row (the drain gap) clear, and with the default wrap the lower mips would blend them into a dashed line across every gap.
- Atlas (`uv: "atlas"`, the laundry here and the trees' autumn leaf atlas in `Trees/manifest.json`): each leaf card's UVs address one padded cell inside 0..1, so the tiling is 1 and the apply step clamps both axes (`address_x` and `address_y` = `TA_CLAMP`) on all three maps: filtering at the atlas border never pulls in the cell on the opposite edge. The 10 px transparent padding round each cell keeps neighbouring cells apart in the first mips.
- Laundry cells: a garment quad addresses its cell's `uv` rect from `layout.cells` in `textures.json`; the rect's top edge (v1) is the rope and the garment is drawn to scale (2.56 px per cm), so a quad of `size_cm` shows it at its real size. The pegs' x positions are in `pins_cm`, from the rect's left edge.
- Leaf litter (`leaf_litter`, key `Leaf_Litter`) is a masked set on metre UVs (u = x, v = y on the ground) that wraps both ways: the leaves run on across every edge of the tile. Soul draws the litter patches with it; the ragged outline is the patch polygon's, and inside it the holes between the leaves show the ground. The set owns its colours, so a manifest colour for `Leaf_Litter` only sets the flat colour used where no texture is loaded (give it `base_color_srgb`, `#96733D`). The leaf shapes come from `Scripts/blender/plateau_trees.py` (`FORMS`, drawn by `_leaf`): after a change to them, rerun this generator; `--check` names that cause first (`layout.stamps_sha256` in `textures.json`).
- The gutter (`lane_concrete`) is a ground set, but the Streets layer writes the gutter's own UVs (u along the ruelle, v across it, centre line at v = 0.5), so its joints run across the gutter and its damp channel along it whatever way the ruelle runs.
- `sheet_metal`: the crests run along v, so on a roof whose v runs up the slope the corrugations run down it, as they are laid. The rust runs go toward increasing image rows, down the slope.

## In the apply step

`Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py` imports the sets the manifests use to `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Textures` and gives each key a duplicate of its surface's parent with these maps swapped in, the tiling set and the tint set:

| Surface | Parent | Sets |
| --- | --- | --- |
| wall | `/Game/Plateau/Materials/M_BrickRed` | limestone, slate, buff_brick, sheet_metal, siding, parging; the trees' `tree_bark` |
| ground | `/Game/Plateau/Materials/M_Gravel`, which carries the NordSky wet-ground wrapper, so roofs and yards darken in rain | membrane, ballast, tar, lawn, pavers, soil, crushed_stone, lane_concrete |
| cutout | `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Materials/M_Replica_CutoutParent`, built by the apply step: masked, two-sided, clip 0.5, OpacityMask from the `diff` alpha | balusters, lattice, fence_boards, iron_wrought, laundry, leaf_litter |
| foliage | `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Materials/M_Replica_FoliageParent`, built the same way with the two-sided foliage shading model and SubsurfaceColor = base colour x 0.65, as the mapped trees' leaves | creeper, cedar, fountain_grass, pit_flowers; the trees' `tree_leaves` atlas |

`Trees/manifest.json` (Scripts/blender/plateau_trees.py) carries two more sets in the same format, `tree_bark` and `tree_leaves`, with their PNGs in the Trees folder. The apply step loads them with these (a tree set may not reuse a name from this folder), imports their maps from `Trees/` to the same texture folder, and gives each tree material whose `texture` names one of their maps that set: the six barks are textured wall duplicates at a 1 m x 2 m tile, and the shared autumn leaves a masked, two-sided foliage duplicate whose OpacityMask is the atlas alpha. A tree material that names a map no set covers fails the plan, so leaf cards never import as solid squares.

Content has no masked parent that fits: `M_DetailedAutumnLeaves` is the tree foliage graph with a per-instance tint. The cut-out `diff` textures keep their alpha and get Unreal's alpha-coverage mips (`do_scale_mips_for_alpha_coverage`, threshold 0.5 on A), so the share of texels over the clip stays the same in every mip and thin balusters and laths do not fade away at a distance. Textured duplicates take the manifest's metallic, capped at the set's own (0.3 for galvanised sheet), and the tint the plan records (white for tar). Batches with a masked material (cards, creeper, card plants) are imported with Nanite shape preservation Preserve Area, as the mapped trees are, so their thin pieces keep their area at a distance. `verify` checks every texture's colour space, compression, size, V addressing and alpha-coverage setting, every material's samples, samplers, tiling, macro nodes, NordSky wrappers, blend mode, sidedness, clip, shading model, OpacityMask source, metallic and tint, and every masked batch's Preserve Area.

## Methods and sources

- Greystone and slate follow Larry Gritz's RenderMan shader `stone.sl`: staggered courses, a colour per stone from its index, joint edges perturbed by noise, and a groove profile for the relief.
- Buff brick is the classic procedural brick shader (Darwyn Peachey's `brick` in *Texturing and Modeling: A Procedural Approach*, as the SIGGRAPH 2002 real-time shading course notes reproduce it; Blender's Brick Texture node has the same controls): coordinates folded into one brick module, mortar where the folded coordinate is within half a joint of the module edge, every other row offset by half a brick, a colour per brick from its index; here with a concave joint profile for the relief and the joint's top edge shaded by the brick above. Brick unit and colours: the Plateau heritage sheets (20 x 6 x 10 cm brick; yellow and beige pressed brick) and research-plateau.md §9 (`BrickYellow #D0B27A`, natural mortar `#A9A49A`).
- The large-scale zones of the gravel, ballast and tar tiles are low-frequency noise fields (25 cm to 1 m inside the 2 m tile) that thin the stones (scour), shift their tone (grime) or open bare tar; the macro mask (see above) breaks the repetition beyond the tile.
- Gravel, tar and crushed stone are splatted stones, and each texel keeps the highest one, the way Substance Designer's Tile Sampler and Shape Splatter stack pebbles with a max height blend, and the way the Blender Artists "procedural rocks and ground" breakdown builds stones from Voronoi cells with noise. Rounded stones are lumpy ellipses with a dome profile; crushed stone is the intersection of half-planes with bevelled arrises and a tilted top.
- Spangle and tar cracks are Worley cellular noise ("A Cellular Texture Basis Function", 1996): F1 cells for the spangle, F2 − F1 for the crack lines. Inigo Quilez's "Voronoi edges" notes that F2 − F1 is not a true distance to the border; for one-pixel cracks the difference does not show.
- Siding is the sawtooth band profile of Blender's Wave Texture (one wedge per lap), with the lap shadow baked under each butt as a Substance plank graph would bake it.
- The membrane lap follows the TPO installation guides: laps marked on the sheet edge and a welded seam of at least 1.5 in (Johns Manville TPO guide). The tile is one 8 ft sheet; 10 ft is the most common roll.
- The cut-out cards are drawn from signed distances, so the alpha edge is anti-aliased about the clip. The alpha-coverage mips follow Ignacio Castaño, "Computing Alpha Mipmaps": find the per-mip alpha scale that keeps the top mip's coverage at the test value.
- Virginia creeper: five obovate to elliptic, coarsely toothed leaflets of 4 to 12 cm, crimson to purple in autumn (University of Michigan *Parthenocissus quinquefolia* profile; UC Davis IPM). Guard dimensions: a gallery guard of 107 cm with openings under 10 cm (MRC de Coaticook SARP sheet *Les galeries et les balcons*, research-plateau.md §13).
- The noise is tileable value noise on an integer hash, and the normals come from the height field by central differences.
- Lane concrete is built height first and coloured after, in the order of Kai Li's "Old Stone Paver" Substance Designer breakdown (gamesartist.co.uk, 2025): pattern (the joint), height (exposed aggregate, cracks), damage, dirt in the low lines, colour, then roughness (glossier where it is damp). The damp, silted centre channel and the leaves in it are the ruelle photo in research-plateau.md (aged lane concrete about `#8B7773`).
- The card plants are crossed billboards: Kurt Pelzer, "Rendering Countless Blades of Waving Grass" (*GPU Gems*, ch. 7) for grass clumps as crossed textured quads, klamtii's tree sprites and Kevin Quinn's plant cards for *God of War Ragnarök* for whole silhouettes on crossed cards. Each card carries its plant's whole silhouette, painted back to front from signed-distance blades, fronds, leaves, bristles and discs, so the alpha edge is anti-aliased and the cards survive the alpha-coverage mips. Fountain grass: Pennisetum alopecuroides (arching, 60–90 cm, buff bottlebrush plumes in autumn); cedar: Thuja occidentalis (flattened sprays of scale leaves, inner foliage shed in fall). The tree-pit planting follows the borough's *Adoptez un carré d'arbre* programme, under which residents plant the pits.
- Laundry: the folds fan down from the pegs, as cloth hung from points forms catenary folds radiating from its supports (Jerry Weil, "The Synthesis of Cloth Objects", SIGGRAPH 1986), and the top edge sags between pegs; the garments are drawn as polygons with exact sub-scanline coverage for the alpha.
- Leaf litter is a Shape Splatter, after Adam Sharp's fallen-leaves material in Substance 3D Designer ("Making a Fallen Leaves Material in Substance 3D Designer", 80.lv, 24 October 2022, https://80.lv/articles/making-a-fallen-leaves-material-in-substance-3d-designer). He scatters a few leaf shapes with a Shape Splatter "as it gives you a mask for the shapes", varies their offset, scale and rotation, gives each its own colour, saturation and luminance, lays darker leaves under the lighter ones "to create more depth", and darkens the leaves with an ambient occlusion from their height. Here the shapes are the tree atlas's leaves, each drawn once in grey at 8 px a cm and read back into cover, midrib, stalk and dome maps; the splat samples the cover bilinearly, wraps at every edge and keeps the mask as the alpha. Each later leaf drapes over the ones under it. The tar spot is Rhytisma acerinum, the common black spot of Norway maple leaves.
- The metallic cap on weathered galvanised sheet follows Epic's physically based materials guidance: pure metal is metallic 1, but hybrid surfaces such as corroded, dusty or rusty metal take values between 0 and 1. Built-up roofs are a black bitumen flood coat under a layer of gravel, so the tar set keeps the gravel's own grey (HomeTips, "Tar & Gravel Built-Up Roofing").

The textures are generated, not scanned. The Blender previews (`Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py`) wire every set the way the apply step does, including the clipped `diff` alpha of the masked sets and the creeper's transmission (`Preview/Replica/README.md`), except that they still tint tar from the manifest colour and take the manifest's metallic: until they call `textured_tint` and `textured_metallic`, they show tar roofs darker and sheet metal more metallic than the game.
