# Plex: Plateau plex fronts, rears and roofs on the plain buildings

`python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py` writes this folder: 1,110 OBJ batches (git-ignored, rebuilt byte for byte), `manifest.json` (batches, materials, counts, source and geometry SHA-256, the `yard_trees` block), `summary.json` (fronts by type, stair shape, crown, storeys histogram and sources, streets covered, and the `roofs`, `rear` (with `yards`), `background` and `glass_cards` blocks), `fronts.json` (one record per front, including each exterior stair's head and foot in map coordinates, a `roof` and a `rear` record per lot, the `background` masses and the glass-card audit) and `yard_trees.json` (the yard trees the apply step instances, see *Yard trees*). It reads the committed `canopy_2019_clip.json` (the city's 2019 canopy over the district) and `assessment_roll_join.json`. `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_plex.py` checks it; `--quick` runs only the fixtures. A build takes about 110 s, the full test about 4 minutes.

## What it covers

The 1,407 inside buildings that have no detailed front yet: everything except the 100 Panet/Visitation route fronts, the 154 park-facing perimeter fronts, the Le Neuville tower and École Le Plateau. 1,307 get a front facing their named street, plus 383 corner side fronts, a roof and a yard side. 100 are skipped: 75 have no exposed edge facing a named street (yard and lane buildings), 11 are institutional buildings or outbuildings, 8 are towers over 24 m and 6 have footprints under 25 m². The classic plex streets are covered: Mentana 135, Saint-André 103, Cartier 83, Saint-Christophe 76, Fabre 66, Dorion 62, Garnier 59 and others.

The layer also skins the backs of 252 masses it gives no front: the 154 park-perimeter buildings and 98 of the skipped buildings (see *Background backs*).

Each front is a visual skin. Glass, door leaves and recess backs sit 4 cm in front of the retained collision mass, 1 cm ahead of the old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards. The masonry face sits 16 cm out, so the windows read as 12 cm recesses. Stair treads, stoops, porch steps, porch decks and balcony decks (`Wood_Deck`) are a separate collision batch, `collision: complex`, which means complex-as-simple like the existing `*_Collision` batches. Every other batch has no collision. The masses keep their back and side walls. Where the plex front stands taller than its retained mass, a brick band and the roof close the gap above the mass top (`top_extensions`, 1,300 lots); on the back walls the rear skin rises to that height instead.

## Rules and data

- **Storeys:** OSM `building:levels` (597 fronts). Then the assessment roll joined by address, `assessment_roll_join.json` (468). Then the 2016 municipal LOD2 height as round((h − 4.9 m) / 3.26 m), an offset and increment measured from the LOD2-versus-levels medians (108). Otherwise 3 storeys (133). When the data storeys would leave more than 1.5 m of the retained 9.6 m mass showing above the crown, one storey is added (142 fronts, flagged in `storeys_source`). Roll storeys of 7 to 12, which the roll alone is not trusted for, count when the LOD2 roof agrees within 1.5 storeys: the median height of the roof vertices of the municipal building that covers at least 60% of the footprint, all its rings together (`lod2_roofs`; the survey splits or groups some buildings, so the per-ring match above misses them). That makes the deep block 356220851 on Mentana 9 storeys (roll 9, LOD2 roof 32.2 m) instead of the default 3, with a flat parapet; its record carries the `lod2_roof` check.
- **Era:** the roll's construction year (1,090 fronts), or a seeded draw from the roll's decade histogram. Era selects the crown (wood cornice before 1890; pressed-metal cornice or slate *fausse mansarde* 1890–1905; metal cornice or parapet 1906–1915; raised brick parapet with a cast-stone medallion 1916–1939; flat parapet after that), the masonry mix (research-plateau.md §9) and the lintels (painted wood, stone or cast stone).
- **Front yard:** distance from the façade to the parallel OSM `footway=sidewalk` centreline, minus 1 m (1,207 fronts), or a road-width estimate. Exterior stairs need at least 1.05 m and stay entirely inside that yard. Curved stairs run along the wall and turn toward the street at the foot, for yards under 3 m. Straight stairs are used at 3 m and more. Lot-line fronts (yard under 1.05 m) get cantilevered balconies over the sidewalk and doors near grade. Stairs go to about 92% of 1885–1944 duplexes and triplexes, half of the 1880–1884 ones and a quarter of the 1870s ones. Among 1890–1930 residential fronts on the plex streets, 73% have one.
- **Composition:** one plex per 7.63 m lot module (wide footprints are split). The ground floor is raised 75–105 cm over a stone base with basement vents. Storeys are 320–330 cm. The stair-head balcony on the 2nd floor carries paired doors with transoms. Each upper floor has a full-width wooden balcony, 1.1–1.5 m deep, with 86 cm iron or painted railings, on consoles or (1912–1939) on columns. Windows are double-hung or casement with a ⅓ transom, with stone sills and lintels. Corner buildings on arterials, and lots with a mapped shop node, get a dépanneur-style ground floor: shop windows, a sign band, an awning and a blade sign.
- **Porch steps:** the steps from a raised porch down to the parterre are solid (3,229 steps on 1,066 porches): each grey wooden tread has its riser and closed sides down to the ground, and the bottom step is stone, so no step floats over a dark gap.
- **Coping:** every front and corner side front ends in a galvanised metal coping that laps 18 cm back over the roof and 3 cm over the face. Its ends step where the neighbouring fronts stand at another height.
- **Palette:** six brick colours, grey limestone and cast stone, white trim, black iron, five painted colours, grey deck paint, glass and slate, from research-plateau.md §9; plus the shared keys of the walls-and-roofs pass (`Roof_Membrane`, `Roof_Gravel`, `Roof_Tar`, `Sheet_Metal`, `Siding_Lap_Cream`, `Siding_Lap_Grey`, `Parged`, `Rail_Baluster`) and of the yards pass (`Rail_Lattice`, `Fence_Boards`, `Leaf_Creeper`, `Yard_Grass`, `Yard_Pavers`, `Yard_Soil`, `Yard_Gravel`), which the apply step maps to textured materials. The yards keys take colours near their texture means (`Textures/textures.json`), so their tints stay near 1.
- **Masonry mix:** the §9 split read too red in the street previews (`Preview/Replica/`), so brown, buff and stone take a few points from red within each era. Before 1890: 15% limestone, then red, dark red, brown and orange in equal parts. 1890–1915: 32% limestone, 20% red, 6% dark red, 16% brown, 6% dark brown, 15% buff, 5% orange. 1916–1939: 30% brown, 15% dark brown, 18% red, 25% buff, 12% orange. District result: 333 limestone, 358 brown, 182 buff and 434 red or orange fronts (was 317, 241, 133 and 616).

## Roofs

Each lot's roof is drawn after all the fronts, with its own seed, because each party parapet needs its neighbour's roof height. A flat Plateau roof that is replaced must be white, painted white or reflective (SRI 78 or more) or white gravel ballast; a terrace or mechanical equipment is exempt (Ville de Montréal, *Rénover un toit plat ou à faible pente*, the Plateau-Mont-Royal section). So most roofs are white membrane and older ones are tar and gravel.

- **Surface:** 811 white membrane (`Roof_Membrane`, 62%), 364 white gravel ballast (`Roof_Gravel`, 28%) and 132 black tar and gravel (`Roof_Tar`, 10%). It is the extension cap 10 cm under the front's top, or, where the front does not rise 20 cm over the mass, a skin 2 cm over the mass top. No flat roof is slate any more; mansard fronts keep their slate.
- **Party parapets:** 2,345 runs along the party lines (shared edges, or a neighbour within 25 cm): the membrane turned up a 16 cm upstand and a metal coping 18 cm into the lot, never above the front's own top. Each lot draws its half, so the copings meet on the line and step where the roofs differ. Where the lot stands 30 cm or more above a neighbour's roof, the party wall above that roof gets a brick skin 16 cm proud (659 runs), which also covers the old glass cards up there.
- **Roof props:** a brick chimney box with a cast-stone cap near a party side, about a third of the way back (1,307 lots), and a galvanised roof hatch (1,272 lots). 33 lots have a deck terrace at the back of the roof behind a cut-out baluster rail (`Rail_Baluster`, 107 cm), reached by a metal stair bulkhead with a door that stands at least 4.7 m behind the front line, about twice its height, so the street barely sees it. The props are scattered on the roof polygon with seeded positions and kept inside it, the way procedural building generators place roof clutter.

## The yard side

The rear of each lot is built after the fronts and roofs, with its own seed, so the fronts stay as they were. The street-level model is the back of a Plateau plex as the ruelle shows it (research-plateau.md §13, the Plateau-Mont-Royal and Villeray *ruelles vertes* guides): plain brick or painted siding, a door and a window or two on each floor, a wooden or steel gallery on every upper floor held by square posts from the yard, a steep straight wooden flight or a steel spiral, a yard closed toward the ruelle by a fence with a gate, and here and there a shed (*hangar*), a garage, a cedar hedge, a small cedar or a creeper.

- **Back walls.** Every exposed wall that is not a street front, a party wall or covered by a neighbour gets a plane 16 cm proud of the retained mass, up to the mass top or the front's extension top, with a sheet-metal flashing on top. The old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards on those walls are behind it. A wall a neighbour mostly covers keeps its free stretches of 1.5 m or more as pieces. The walls are sorted the way Müller et al. sort a mass's faces: `rear` (facing away from the front, 1,708), `lane` (standing on a ruelle, 311), `street` (on a street that is not the lot's front, 283), `long` (a side wall over 8 m with no neighbour within 1.2 m, 460) and `side` (1,757). Skins meeting round a convex corner end flush, so there is no 16 cm notch.
- **Materials.** Street walls are the front's own brick (common brick behind a greystone front) on a greystone base, with the front's stone sills, its lintels (stone, cast stone or painted wood by era) and taller double-hung windows; where a street wall meets the front corner, the cornice or the parapet's cast-stone band returns one 4.5 m bay along it (37 returns, after the heritage sheet *Les couronnements*). Lane and long walls are common brick. A quarter of the lots have lap siding (`Siding_Lap_Cream`, `Siding_Lap_Grey`: clapboard with visible laps, never white trim) on their rear and side walls (762 walls), but only behind the first 6 m of the lot, so the street never sees siding round a corner. Every other skin stands on a parged concrete foundation band (`Parged`); rear stoops and door thresholds are cast concrete. No rear, lane or long wall uses the rock-faced greystone.
- **Windows.** Rear, lane, street and long walls get applied windows (frame, glass and a proud sill): one per floor on a secondary rear wall, one per 4.5 m bay and floor on the others (3,498 on long walls). Side walls stay blank, a fifth of the long ones with a creeper.
- **Rear façade.** On each lot, the longest rear wall of 3 m or more gets the full treatment if its yard allows. A lot with no rear wall puts it on its long wall with the most yard in front, when the lot has 4 storeys or fewer: one stack at the end of the wall farthest from the front, with the rest of the wall windowed (86 lots). Per 7.63 m module: a half-glazed door on every floor, stacked, and one or two windows; door and window heads line up; a concrete stoop with side steps at the ground-floor door.
- **Galleries.** One on every upper floor whose door fits under the wall top (1,741 on 833 lots), 1.40–1.95 m deep across the module, two posts from the yard to the top (the modules of a wide lot share the post between them) and, on some stacks, a flat galvanised roof (`Sheet_Metal`) over the top gallery. The deck is 4 cm of grey boards (`Wood_Deck`, collision) with a painted rim board 10 cm by 2.5 cm on the outer edge (the old deck edge was an 18 cm slab). The rim runs on across the stair openings, so no flight's soffit shows through the deck edge. Each guard is built as on the Plateau, 107 cm high (over 1.89 m above grade the MRC de Coaticook gallery sheet asks for 1,070 mm): a 2x4 bottom rail over a 5 cm drain gap and a top rail with its flat cap, both geometry, and between them the lot's infill. 70% of the lots take a `Rail_Baluster` cut-out card (2x2 balusters, 4,142 runs); the rest take solid boards (2,072 runs). The card draws its own rails exactly where the geometry rails stand. Guards, rails, posts and rims are wood paint (grey, stained oak, green, burgundy, cream), never `Trim` white. Apartment blocks of five storeys or more get black steel galleries with iron pickets (373 runs).
- **Ground-floor galleries.** 40% of the straight stacks whose ground floor stands 50 cm or more up swap the concrete stoop for a low gallery across the module at the raised ground floor (71 modules): thin deck and rim board, solid steps down to the yard at the module's far end from the stair, a guard where the deck stands 60 cm or more, and a lattice skirt underneath (284 `Rail_Lattice` cards). Each skirt card keeps the 1 m height the lattice is drawn for, hangs under the rim board and runs below grade, so the diamonds are never squashed.
- **Stairs.** Every gallery is reached from the yard. *Straight* (405 stacks): a flight with risers of at most 22.5 cm runs outside the first gallery along its edge to a landing at its end; the flights above run inside the gallery's front band, each through an opening in the deck above, with a guard round the opening. Where the flight from that deck to the next passes over the far end of the opening, that end's guard is cut down to clear the flight's underside. No guard stands across a flight's treads (`test_guards_cut_where_flights_pass`). *Spiral* (494 stacks): a steel spiral of 1.4 m in the end bay of the gallery, a full turn per storey, stepping off onto each deck; it needs only 1.7 m of yard, so shallow yards get one. 30% of lots prefer the spiral. Each spiral tread keeps its `Wood_Deck` top with an iron pan 4 cm under it (16,754 treads). An iron outer stringer and a flat-bar rail follow the helix in segments of two treads, and an iron baluster stands on every second tread (7,271). Rail and balusters stay open at each deck exit. Straight treads are drawn as their top faces with a soffit under each flight.
- **Fences.** With a ruelle behind, the fence runs 2.9 m from the ruelle centreline, across the lot's full width, with a gate near the stair foot; a fence at a slant to the wall follows the nearer end. With yards back to back, it runs halfway to the next building, with no gate. With open ground behind (a court, a car park or an unmapped lane, which the old code left bare), the yard ends 11 m out, or halfway to anything nearer. Fence kinds:
  - *Board fences* (1.65–1.95 m): the weathered boards are one `Fence_Boards` cut-out card per run, with 4x4 posts at most 8 ft (2.44 m) apart and a flat 2x6 cap rail as geometry, in natural, grey or painted wood.
  - *Slatted chain-link* (1.5 m): a slat panel between galvanised posts and a top rail.
  - *Low picket fences* (107 cm, 163 lane edges): the gallery guard's rails and baluster card on posts 1.8 m apart.
  - *Gates*: a leaf between two taller gate posts, painted in a colour that is never natural wood and never the fence posts' own.
  - *Open lane edges*: 24% of the lane lots try to leave the lane edge open as a crushed-stone parking pad, 2.7 x 4.8 m (80 pads), clear of the galleries, stairs and buildings; where no pad fits, the lot gets a low fence instead. Another 14% get a low fence. So the lane edge breaks up.
  - *Side fences* run on the lateral lot lines from the house to the fence line, one per party line, in the lot's kind (9,082 fence posts in all).
  - *Outbuildings*: sheds and garages stand against the fence with their doors on the lane, under galvanised roofs.
- **Ground cover.** Every yard (1,079 lots) is covered by flat patches that never overlap: the cells of one grid in the wall frame, from the skin face to the fence line and between the lateral lot lines, each in one material. The CGA split again, now on the yard. The mix is seeded: a lawn (`Yard_Grass`) with a paver patio (`Yard_Pavers`) by the house (35%), a deep patio (23%), a yard paved throughout (15%) or gravelled throughout (`Yard_Gravel`, 9%), or a lawn with a vegetable garden at the back (17%). Soil beds (`Yard_Soil`) of 60–90 cm run along the side fences and the back fence on most lots, and the parking pad is crushed stone. Cells inside a building are split and dropped; the shed's cell is left out. Patches stand 1.0 to 2.0 cm over the floor, beds and paving a touch higher. A yard too shallow for a fence clear of the galleries still gets its ground cover (47 lots). The district totals are 22,000 m² of lawn, 22,300 m² of pavers, 9,600 m² of soil and 5,900 m² of gravel.
- **Plants.** Virginia creeper (vigne vierge) grows on a fifth of the blank side walls of 3 m or more and on the lane side of a third of the fences (508 patches on plex lots, 76 on background masses). Each patch is a run of `Leaf_Creeper` cut-out cards with a broken outline: strips 35–90 cm wide, each a convex pentagon with a slanted, slightly peaked top, taller toward the middle of the patch. A few strips are left out as gaps, and a few narrow runners climb past the rest. On fences the cards stand clear of the posts and drape 18 cm over the top. The texture supplies the autumn scarlet, crimson and wine, with some green. Cedar hedges and small cedars remain.
- **Placement rules.** Galleries and stairs keep 3 m from every drive, service and ruelle centreline (the Streets layer rule) and never overlap a building or a neighbour's structure; fences, sheds and parking pads keep 2.8 m from a ruelle, 6.5 m from a named street and 2.5 m from a driveway, and stop at buildings. When nothing fits, `fronts.json` gives the reason: for the galleries (`reason`), no rear wall of 3 m or more and no long wall (224 lots, mostly walls on the lane or narrow wings), a long wall on a block over 4 storeys (4), a building, structure or lane behind the wall (116), or a yard too shallow or lot too narrow (130); for the yard (`yard_reason`), no rear wall of 2 m or more with 2.5 m in front of it (224), the fence line blocked (3) or a yard under 1 m deep (1).
- **Collision.** Gallery decks, landings, treads and the ground-floor galleries and their steps are `Wood_Deck`, so they join the complex-collision batches. Skins, guards, fences, sheds, roofs, ground cover and plants have none.
- **Records.** Each `rear` record in `fronts.json` lists the back walls with their line (`a`, `b`, `top_cm`), material, base, windows and doors, the deck quads and heights, every flight with its foot and head in map coordinates, the gallery footprints, every guard run (`guards`: line and heights), the ground-floor galleries (`ground_decks`: quad, height, steps, foot, head, guard, skirt cards), the fence (`fence`: kind, edge, line, runs, gate and its paint, post count, shed; `null` for an open or fenceless lane edge), the side-fence segments, and the `yard` (`mix`, lane `edge`, ground cells in the wall frame, area per material, the yard and pad corners, the shed, and its `trees`). Each `roof` record gives the material, height, props and party runs (with the height a party skin starts from).

## Yard trees

`yard_trees.json` holds 573 trees as the apply step reads them, `{x, y, z, yaw, scale, kind}`, in the replica frame (cm, degrees; `scale` multiplies the shared `tree_small_02` mesh, 4.55 m tall at 1). Each lot's `yard.trees` in `fronts.json` repeats its rows with the crown and height they were given. The trees come from the Ville de Montréal canopy survey:

- **Data.** *Canopée 2019* (CC BY 4.0, donneesquebec.ca `vmtl-canopee`, resource 77e44cc1) is the ground projection of every tree crown over 3 m, traced from the CMM's August 2019 aerial photos and summer 2018 lidar, as polygons in NAD83 MTM zone 8. `python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py --clip-canopy canopee-2019.zip` streams the published 72 MB archive (the GeoJSON is 823 MB, 514,124 features). It converts each ring with Snyder's transverse Mercator inverse and the map's `prepare_lafontaine.xy`, clips to the district bounds plus 10 m, simplifies at 40 cm (the survey traces a 0.75 m raster) and writes `canopy_2019_clip.json`: 1,166 polygons, 29,581 vertices, 379 kB, with the source, licence and archive hash. The build reads only that file. A top-down check against the OSM footprints and paths lines up: the park's paths run through the gaps in its canopy, the street-tree rows follow the sidewalks and the pond's outline shows.
- **Crowns.** The canopy is filled on a 1 m grid (even-odd scanlines, so holes stay open), and every canopy cell gets its chamfer distance to the canopy edge (Borgefors' 3-4 chamfer). A crown is a disc around a local maximum of that distance, largest first, the distance-transform split that separates merged blobs before a watershed. Only cells inside a plex yard can hold a trunk.
- **Rules.** A trunk keeps 2 m from every building, 0.9 m from galleries, stairs, decks, sheds, pads, hedges and cedars, 0.6 m from fences, 3 m from every drive, service and ruelle centreline and from the traffic graph, and 4 m from the 4,571 mapped trees. The tall trees draw a crown wider than the survey's, so the house and gallery clearances grow with the drawn crown (0.4 and 0.3 of its radius), and neighbours keep 0.55 of their summed drawn crowns apart. A lot holds at most three. None stands on a parking pad.
- **Kinds and size.** A crown under 6 m across is a `small` tree, 7.5–8.5 m tall (232). The others are mature: maple 55%, ash 30% and elm 15% by a seeded draw (198, 103 and 40), after Montréal's inventories, where maples and ash lead and elm is rare. They stand 12.5 to 18 m tall from the crown (7 m + 0.75 x crown), and at least 1.5 m over the roof of a lot of three storeys or fewer, so inside the blocks they rise above the 3-storey roofs. The apply step widens or narrows each kind's crown and gives the trees no collision, like the mapped trees.

## Background backs

The old `OSM Glass` cards are 130 × 160 cm quads 3 cm in front of every wall of 2.5 m or more of every mass (`Data/Plateau/BaseGeometry/Glass.obj`, 75,339 cards). The generator reads them, matches each card to its wall by that 3 cm offset (as `plateau_landmark_visuals.py` does), and treats every mass outside the layer that has a card no detailed front covers and no taller neighbour within 25 cm hides: the 154 park-perimeter buildings, 74 yard and lane buildings, 10 institutional buildings and outbuildings, 8 towers and 6 small buildings. Each gets the yard-side skin of a plex lot on every exposed wall that is not one of its detailed fronts: brick (the perimeter front's brick half the time, else common brick; buff or brown brick on the towers, cast stone on park pavilions and government buildings) or lap siding on a quarter of the rear and side walls, a parged base, sheet-metal flashing, and windows on every wall with 1.2 m in front of it, one per 4.5 m bay and floor (6,137 windows). The skin rises over the card generator's top row where it stands above a low mass's roof. Where such a mass stands over a lower neighbour, the party wall above the neighbour's roof is skinned too (26 runs). They get no galleries, yards or roofs.

The audit in `fronts.json` (`glass_cards`) and `summary.json` places every card: 31,088 hidden by a taller neighbour within 25 cm, 11,718 behind plex fronts, 27,560 behind back-wall, party and background skins, 4,493 behind the route and perimeter fronts, 393 on the tower and the school (whose cards `plateau_landmark_visuals.py` drops) and 87 inside the Théâtre de Verdure envelope. None floats on a blank wall. `test_plateau_plex.py` repeats the audit from the written records alone.

## Budget

2,925,511 triangles in 1,110 batches: 2,214,776 for the fronts and roofs (median 1,556 per front; the roofs are 89,986, the terrace rails now one card each), 649,725 for the yard side and 61,010 for the background backs (creeper cards included). Largest batch 58,173.

- **Triangle cap 2.95 million** (2.5 million, then 2.75 million for the walls-and-roofs pass): the yards pass adds about 270,000 triangles. That covers guard rails, caps and rim boards (6,587 guard runs), fence posts and cap rails (9,082 posts), the spiral pans, stringers, rails and balusters (16,754 treads), and the ground cover and creeper cards. The spec's "about 2.5 million" for the plex layer is now exceeded by 17%.
- **Batch cap 1,200** (was 860): the walls-and-roofs keys widened the tile to 190 m; the seven yards keys stay under the cap at that tile (1,110 batches). A batch is one Nanite static-mesh actor, which is cheap.
- Kept cheap on purpose: the infill between guard rails, the boards of board fences, the lattice skirts and the creeper leaves are single cut-out cards (the masked materials are two-sided, so one face each; the terrace rail used to draw each card twice). Rails show only their sides and top. Posts stop under the cap that covers their tops. The spiral stringer faces out only, and the spiral rail is a flat bar. Rear windows and doors are applied to one wall plane instead of cut into it. Treads are top faces. The gallery posts run in one piece from the yard to the top. Ground patches are one quad per cell and never overlap. A party coping drops its face on the party line where the neighbour's coping hides it.

## UVs

UVs are in metres: vertical faces take u along the face and v = z, horizontal faces take x and y (the ground cover and the roofs), and `Leaf_Creeper` tiles the same way on its wall or fence plane. The cut-out card keys `Rail_Baluster`, `Rail_Lattice` and `Fence_Boards` take u along the run in metres and v from 0 on the card's lower edge to 1 on its upper edge. The baluster cards are 107 cm and the lattice cards 100 cm, as drawn. Fence cards are 1.65–1.95 m, against the 1.8 m drawing.

Yards: the canopy and the trees follow Ville de Montréal, *Canopée* 2019 (CC BY 4.0, https://donneesquebec.ca/recherche/dataset/vmtl-canopee; photo aérienne et lidar © Communauté métropolitaine de Montréal), converted with the transverse Mercator inverse of Snyder, *Map Projections: A Working Manual* (USGS PP 1395, 1987). The crown split adapts the distance-transform seeds of watershed segmentation, as in the scikit-image *Watershed segmentation* example (https://scikit-image.org/docs/0.25.x/auto_examples/segmentation/plot_watershed.html) and in crown delineation from canopy models (for example the ISPRS 2016 paper at https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XLI-B3/575/2016/isprs-archives-XLI-B3-575-2016.pdf); the distance is Borgefors' 3-4 chamfer (*Distance Transformations in Digital Images*, CVGIP 34, 1986, https://people.cmm.minesparis.psl.eu/users/marcoteg/cv/publi_pdf/MM_refs/1986_Borgefors_distance.pdf). The species mix follows Montréal's borough tree plans (the Verdun plan, https://portail-m4s.s3.montreal.ca/pdf/plan_arboricole_verdun_2014_200810_1.pdf: maple and ash lead, elm is rare). Guards, fences, skirts and creepers are alpha-masked cut-out cards, Epic's recommended blend mode for fences and foliage (*Material Blend Modes*, above). The creeper's layered cards of a broken outline follow the usual foliage-card practice for ivy on walls (the CG Cookie foliage-card thread, https://cgcookie.com/community/11178-collab2021-wip-raisonhomme), rather than a grown ivy like Thomas Luft's *Ivy Generator* (https://graphics.uni-konstanz.de/~luft/ivy_generator/), which would cost far more triangles. Gallery guard parts (2x4 rails, 2x2 balusters under 10 cm apart, 107 cm) and lattice skirts follow the MRC de Coaticook sheet *Les galeries et les balcons*. Open lane edges, low fences and parking pads follow the *ruelles vertes* guides. The yard ground cover is the CGA split of Müller et al. on the yard lot. No code was taken from any of them.

Brick and paint colours, door and window rhythm, stair side and shape, cornice details, roof materials and roof props are rule-and-seed estimates. They are not surveys of individual buildings. The same holds for the rear: gallery depth, stair kind, guards, fences, sheds, ground cover and plants are seeded. Lot lines are not mapped, so the rear lot line is the ruelle, the midpoint to the next building or 11 m out. The yard trees stand where the 2019 canopy shows crowns, but one generic mesh draws them all, and their species and heights are estimates from crown size. They are placed in plex yards only. Courts, car parks and the yards of the route, perimeter and skipped buildings stay bare floor. The 224 lots with no rear wall of 2 m or more get no yard, and the side yards along long walls stay bare unless a gallery stack stands there. The route buildings keep their own detailed fronts and returns. The background masses get skins, windows and creepers but no roofs, galleries or yards, and the towers are skinned at their mapped mass height. The geometry keeps the map's mirrored frame (x east, y north).
