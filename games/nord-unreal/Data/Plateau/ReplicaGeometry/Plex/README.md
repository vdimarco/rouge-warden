# Plex: Plateau plex fronts, rears and roofs on the plain buildings

`python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py` writes this folder: 1,341 OBJ batches (git-ignored, rebuilt byte for byte), `manifest.json` (batches, materials, counts, source and geometry SHA-256, the `yard_trees` block), `summary.json` (fronts by type, stair shape, crown, storeys histogram and sources, streets covered, and the `roofs`, `rear` (with `yards`), `background`, `glass_cards` and `walls_pass` blocks), `fronts.json` (one record per front, including each exterior stair's head and foot in map coordinates, a `roof` and a `rear` record per lot, the `background` masses and the glass-card audit) and `yard_trees.json` (the yard trees the apply step instances, see *Yard trees*). It reads the committed `canopy_2019_clip.json` (the city's 2019 canopy over the district) and `assessment_roll_join.json`. `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_plex.py` checks it; `--quick` runs only the fixtures. A build takes about 2 minutes, the full test about 3.

## What it covers

The 1,407 inside buildings that have no detailed front yet: everything except the 100 Panet/Visitation route fronts, the 154 park-facing perimeter fronts, the Le Neuville tower and École Le Plateau. 1,307 get a front facing their named street, plus 383 corner side fronts, a roof and a yard side. 100 are skipped: 75 have no exposed edge facing a named street (yard and lane buildings), 11 are institutional buildings or outbuildings, 8 are towers over 24 m and 6 have footprints under 25 m². The classic plex streets are covered: Mentana 135, Saint-André 103, Cartier 83, Saint-Christophe 76, Fabre 66, Dorion 62, Garnier 59 and others.

The layer also skins the backs of 252 masses it gives no front: the 154 park-perimeter buildings and 98 of the skipped buildings (see *Background backs*).

Each front is a visual skin. Glass, door leaves and recess backs sit 4 cm in front of the retained collision mass, 1 cm ahead of the old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards. The masonry face sits 16 cm out, so the windows read as 12 cm recesses. Stair treads, stoops, porch steps, porch decks and balcony decks (`Wood_Deck`) are a separate collision batch, `collision: complex`, which means complex-as-simple like the existing `*_Collision` batches. The `Collision_Only` batches collide but never render (see *Collision*). Every other batch has no collision. The masses keep their back and side walls. Where the plex front stands taller than its retained mass, a brick band and the roof close the gap above the mass top (`top_extensions`, 1,300 lots); on the back walls the rear skin rises to that height instead.

## Rules and data

- **Storeys:** OSM `building:levels` (597 fronts). Then the assessment roll joined by address, `assessment_roll_join.json` (468). Then the 2016 municipal LOD2 height as round((h − 4.9 m) / 3.26 m), an offset and increment measured from the LOD2-versus-levels medians (108). Otherwise 3 storeys (133). When the data storeys would leave more than 1.5 m of the retained 9.6 m mass showing above the crown, one storey is added (142 fronts, flagged in `storeys_source`). Roll storeys of 7 to 12, which the roll alone is not trusted for, count when the LOD2 roof agrees within 1.5 storeys: the median height of the roof vertices of the municipal building that covers at least 60% of the footprint, all its rings together (`lod2_roofs`; the survey splits or groups some buildings, so the per-ring match above misses them). That makes the deep block 356220851 on Mentana 9 storeys (roll 9, LOD2 roof 32.2 m) instead of the default 3, with a flat parapet; its record carries the `lod2_roof` check.
- **Build-year overrides:** the roll keeps the oldest unit year on a lot, which is not always the building standing there. `BUILD_YEAR_OVERRIDES` corrects it, with the source in the lot's `era_source` and `year_override`. One entry so far: OSM way 356220851 (4110–4118 rue De Mentana and 4077–4105 rue Saint-André) is the OMHM's Habitations De Mentana, 158 social-housing units in two buildings with lifts, *in operation since 1974* (OMHM, https://omhm.qc.ca/en/node/15296), not an 1885 building. It takes 1974 and red brick (the photo in OMHM's October 2008 Infochantier), so it gets the post-1945 apartment kit below. The OMHM page gives the year of operation; no source checked here gives the construction year itself.
- **Era:** the roll's construction year (1,089 fronts), an override where the roll's year is not the building's (`BUILD_YEAR_OVERRIDES`, 1 front, see below), or a seeded draw from the roll's decade histogram. Era selects the crown (wood cornice before 1890; pressed-metal cornice or slate *fausse mansarde* 1890–1905; metal cornice or parapet 1906–1915; raised brick parapet with a cast-stone medallion 1916–1939; flat parapet after that), the masonry mix (research-plateau.md §9) and the lintels (painted wood, stone or cast stone).
- **Front yard:** distance from the façade to the parallel OSM `footway=sidewalk` centreline, minus 1 m (1,207 fronts), or a road-width estimate. Exterior stairs need at least 1.05 m and stay entirely inside that yard. Curved stairs run along the wall and turn toward the street at the foot, for yards under 3 m. Straight stairs are used at 3 m and more. Lot-line fronts (yard under 1.05 m) get cantilevered balconies over the sidewalk and doors near grade. Stairs go to about 92% of 1885–1944 duplexes and triplexes, half of the 1880–1884 ones and a quarter of the 1870s ones. Among 1890–1930 residential fronts on the plex streets, 73% have one.
- **Composition:** one plex per 7.63 m lot module (wide footprints are split). The ground floor is raised 75–105 cm over a stone base with basement vents. Storeys are 320–330 cm. The stair-head balcony on the 2nd floor carries paired doors with transoms. Each upper floor has a full-width wooden balcony, 1.1–1.5 m deep, with 86 cm iron or painted railings, on consoles or (1912–1939) on columns. Windows are double-hung or casement with a ⅓ transom, with stone sills and lintels. Corner buildings on arterials, and lots with a mapped shop node, get a dépanneur-style ground floor: shop windows, a sign band, an awning and a blade sign.
- **Porch steps:** the steps from a raised porch down to the parterre are solid (3,229 steps on 1,066 porches): each grey wooden tread has its riser and closed sides down to the ground, and the bottom step is stone, so no step floats over a dark gap.
- **Coping:** every front and corner side front ends in a galvanised metal coping that laps 18 cm back over the roof and 3 cm over the face. Its ends step where the neighbouring fronts stand at another height.
- **Palette:** six brick colours (buff on the shared key `Brick_Buff_Even`, which the apply step maps to its own even buff-brick set; it has no `texture` field, so it never falls back to the red brick photo tinted up to 8 times), grey limestone and cast stone, white trim, black iron, five painted colours, grey deck paint, glass and slate, from research-plateau.md §9; plus the shared keys of the walls-and-roofs pass (`Roof_Membrane`, `Roof_Gravel`, `Roof_Tar`, `Sheet_Metal`, `Siding_Lap_Cream`, `Siding_Lap_Grey`, `Parged`, `Rail_Baluster`) and of the yards pass (`Rail_Lattice`, `Fence_Boards`, `Leaf_Creeper`, `Yard_Grass`, `Yard_Pavers`, `Yard_Soil`, `Yard_Gravel`), which the apply step maps to textured materials. The yards keys take colours near their texture means (`Textures/textures.json`), so their tints stay near 1. Tar and sheet metal follow their sets too: `Roof_Tar` is #4A4744, grey pea gravel in black tar (the set owns its colour; the old #2C2B29 rendered flat black), and `Sheet_Metal` is the set's #9CA0A1 at metallic 0.3 and roughness 0.59 (at 0.6 and 0.4 weathered galvanised sheet read brown in shade). The walls pass adds `Asphalt_Lot` (parking on lots with no yard; the street asphalt photo, a lighter #4C4D50) and `Collision_Only`.
- **Masonry mix:** the §9 split read too red in the street previews (`Preview/Replica/`), so brown, buff and stone take a few points from red within each era. Before 1890: 15% limestone, then red, dark red, brown and orange in equal parts. 1890–1915: 32% limestone, 20% red, 6% dark red, 16% brown, 6% dark brown, 15% buff, 5% orange. 1916–1939: 30% brown, 15% dark brown, 18% red, 25% buff, 12% orange. District result: 333 limestone, 358 brown, 182 buff and 434 red or orange fronts (was 317, 241, 133 and 616). Buff is `Brick_Buff_Even` everywhere in this layer, the fronts, the rears and the background skins.

## Roofs

Each lot's roof is drawn after all the fronts, with its own seed, because each party parapet needs its neighbour's roof height. A flat Plateau roof that is replaced must be white, painted white or reflective (SRI 78 or more) or white gravel ballast; a terrace or mechanical equipment is exempt (Ville de Montréal, *Rénover un toit plat ou à faible pente*, the Plateau-Mont-Royal section). So most roofs are white membrane and older ones are tar and gravel.

- **Surface:** 811 white membrane (`Roof_Membrane`, 62%), 364 white gravel ballast (`Roof_Gravel`, 28%) and 132 black tar and gravel (`Roof_Tar`, 10%). It is the extension cap 10 cm under the front's top, or, where the front does not rise 20 cm over the mass, a skin 2 cm over the mass top. No flat roof is slate any more; mansard fronts keep their slate.
- **Party parapets:** 2,345 runs along the party lines (shared edges, or a neighbour within 25 cm): the membrane turned up a 16 cm upstand and a metal coping 18 cm into the lot, never above the front's own top. Each lot draws its half, so the copings meet on the line and step where the roofs differ. Where the lot stands 30 cm or more above a neighbour's roof, the party wall above that roof gets a brick skin 16 cm proud (659 runs), which also covers the old glass cards up there.
- **Roof props:** a brick chimney box with a cast-stone cap near a party side, about a third of the way back (1,301 lots), and a galvanised roof hatch (1,266 lots). A post-1945 block has a brick lift penthouse with a metal coping instead (two on a block over 30 m deep; 7 on 6 blocks) and up to five galvanised mushroom vents (30). 33 lots have a deck terrace at the back of the roof behind a cut-out baluster rail (`Rail_Baluster`, 107 cm), reached by a metal stair bulkhead with a door that stands at least 4.7 m behind the front line, about twice its height, so the street barely sees it. The props are scattered on the roof polygon with seeded positions and kept inside it, the way procedural building generators place roof clutter.

## Post-1945 apartment kit

A block of five storeys or more built from 1945 on is not a plex. Six lots qualify (131285491 on Cherrier, 345482790 on Montcalm, 356220851 and 356257089 on Mentana, 356740897 on Brébeuf, 358133794 on Rachel Est), and the eight towers over 24 m among the background masses take the same kit. The parts follow OMHM's notice for the Habitations De Mentana (October 2008 Infochantier: concrete balcony slabs with painted steel guards, patio doors, exterior wall panels under the windows, an entrance ramp and a parking area), composed with the same split grammar as the plexes (`modern_bays`):

- **Bays** of about 3 m (`MODERN_BAY`) on every wall with 1.2 m in front of it, front and back alike, in the front's brick on a concrete base, with a concrete floor edge at every floor line.
- **Balconies:** on each upper floor every bay of a wall with 3 m in front of it and 4.5 m of length has a 15 cm concrete slab 1.3 m deep with a painted steel guard on three sides (a cap rail over a panel, 1 m high), a patio door behind it and a window beside the door. The towers take one on every second bay. 2,137 in all: 237 on the five kit fronts, 367 on the back walls (128 of them the Habitations De Mentana's stacks, see *Per-building forms*), 1,533 on the towers. They have no collision: nobody reaches them.
- **Windows and panels:** every window has a sliding sash and a painted wall panel under it (cream, grey, green, burgundy or concrete per block), in aluminium frames (`Sheet_Metal`).
- **Entrance:** a glazed double door under a concrete canopy in the middle bay of the main front, one step up (the ground floor stands 15 cm over grade).
- **Parapet:** the roof sits 80 cm under the front's coping (`MODERN_PARAPET`), and the back-wall skins rise to the coping with an inner face and a metal coping of their own; a tower's skin rises 60 cm over its mass roof the same way. The party parapets rise to the same coping.
- **Roof:** a lift penthouse and vents instead of the chimney and hatch (see *Roofs*), and no rear galleries (`reason` says so) and no lap siding.
- **Towers:** buff (`Brick_Buff_Even`) or brown brick, never red (the red ones read as flat orange-red boxes): La Rochelle at 1565 Rachel Est and the other seven.

### Per-building forms

`BUILDING_FORMS` gives a block its own form on top of the kit, with the photo it follows (`form` and `form_source` on its `fronts.json` record). One so far: the Habitations De Mentana (OSM way 356220851), `omhm_slab`, after the photo of the tower in OMHM's *Infochantier* of April 2009. The local review found the kit's continuous balcony bands with solid guards on both faces and round the corner, and a flush ground floor; the photo shows none of that.

- **Long faces** (the two 49 m walls): exposed-concrete piers (`Concrete_Exposed`, 44 cm, 8 cm proud) every 6 m bay (`SLAB_BAY`, 8 bays a face) with red brick between them. Each bay has a window half and a balcony half, so the balconies stand about one bay wide in vertical stacks, one stack per bay on each of the 8 upper floors (128 in all, half the kit's), mirrored about the middle so a stack ends each face. A balcony is a 15 cm concrete slab 1.3 m deep behind an open teal-green metal railing: a `Rail_Baluster_Teal` cut-out card on three sides under a `Paint_Teal` cap rail with corner posts, with a patio door and a small window behind it. The windows keep their aluminium frames, blinds and wall panels.
- **Ground floor set back:** the upper floors stand 1.2 m out (`SLAB_COLONNADE`) over the ground floor on a 40 cm concrete column under each pier (18), with a soffit and a concrete edge beam at the first floor. The ground floor's windows and two court doors stay on the skin plane behind the columns. The building's retained mass cannot be cut, so the upper floors move out rather than the ground floor in. Each column has a `Collision_Only` box of its own collision unit, so the traffic band can drop it alone.
- **End walls:** the Mentana and Saint-André fronts, the heads' side walls and the jogs are plain grey exposed concrete with panel joints every 2.4 m and at each floor line (`concrete_joints`), on a parged base, with no balcony and no window above the ground floor; each front keeps a glazed entrance under a concrete canopy.
- **Kept from the kit:** the parapet roof with two lift penthouses and vents, no galleries and no siding.

## The yard side

The rear of each lot is built after the fronts and roofs, with its own seed, so the fronts stay as they were. The street-level model is the back of a Plateau plex as the ruelle shows it (research-plateau.md §13, the Plateau-Mont-Royal and Villeray *ruelles vertes* guides): plain brick or painted siding, a door and a window or two on each floor, a wooden or steel gallery on every upper floor held by square posts from the yard, a steep straight wooden flight or a steel spiral, a yard closed toward the ruelle by a fence with a gate, and here and there a shed (*hangar*), a garage, a cedar hedge, a small cedar or a creeper.

- **Back walls.** Every exposed wall that is not a street front, a party wall or covered by a neighbour gets a plane 16 cm proud of the retained mass, up to the mass top or the front's extension top, with a sheet-metal flashing on top. The old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards on those walls are behind it. A wall a neighbour mostly covers keeps its free stretches of 1.5 m or more as pieces. The walls are sorted the way Müller et al. sort a mass's faces: `rear` (facing away from the front, 1,708), `lane` (standing on a ruelle, 311), `street` (on a street that is not the lot's front, 283), `long` (a side wall over 8 m with no neighbour within 1.2 m, 460) and `side` (1,757). Skins meeting round a convex corner end flush, so there is no 16 cm notch.
- **Materials.** Street walls are the front's own brick (common brick behind a greystone front) on a greystone base, with the front's stone sills, its lintels (stone, cast stone or painted wood by era) and taller double-hung windows; where a street wall meets the front corner, the cornice or the parapet's cast-stone band returns one 4.5 m bay along it (37 returns, after the heritage sheet *Les couronnements*). Lane and long walls are common brick. A quarter of the lots have lap siding (`Siding_Lap_Cream`, `Siding_Lap_Grey`: clapboard with visible laps, never white trim) on their rear and side walls, but only behind the first 6 m of the lot, so the street never sees siding round a corner, and never on a wall a named street sees (`street_sight`: from a point every 1.5 m along the wall, rays fanned from -75 to 75 degrees off its normal; a ray that crosses a named drive centreline within 45 m before any building edge sees it). The local review found a grey siding wall under a brick storey in plain view of Rachel, on a park-perimeter mass between two fronts; 127 walls are brick for this now (96 on plex lots, 31 on background masses, `street_seen_cm` on the wall record), and the test checks that no siding wall left has a clear line to a named street. Siding is never used on a block over four storeys, and never above the second storey: on a lot of three or four storeys it stops at the floor of the third, with the lot's brick above it (`upper` and `siding_top_cm` on the wall record; 708 of the 833 sided walls, background masses included). Every other skin stands on a parged concrete foundation band (`Parged`); rear stoops and door thresholds are cast concrete. No rear, lane or long wall uses the rock-faced greystone.
- **Windows.** Rear, lane, street and long walls get applied windows (frame, glass and a proud sill): one per floor on a secondary rear wall, one per 4.5 m bay and floor on the others (3,498 on long walls). Side walls stay blank, a fifth of the long ones with a creeper. Rear window and door frames are white `Trim` (PVC or aluminium; the old dark green, oak and iron frames round dark glass read as holes). Every window has a blind or curtains behind the glass (`blind`): a roller blind pulled a quarter to three quarters down (55%), a pair of curtains drawn to the sides (30%) or a blind let down nearly to the sill (15%), in white, cream or beige, a few curtains coloured. `Glass` is an opaque dark key, so the card stands 5 mm in front of it, under the meeting rail, as the street fronts' curtains do. The post-1945 kit's windows and patio doors get them too: 23,269 in all (10,281 on the yard side, 12,308 on the background masses, 680 on the six post-1945 fronts), seeded by each window's place.
- **Rear façade.** On each lot, the longest rear wall of 3 m or more gets the full treatment if its yard allows. A lot with no rear wall puts it on its long wall with the most yard in front, when the lot has 4 storeys or fewer: one stack at the end of the wall farthest from the front, with the rest of the wall windowed (86 lots). Per 7.63 m module: a half-glazed door on every floor, stacked, and one or two windows; door and window heads line up; a concrete stoop with side steps at the ground-floor door.
- **Galleries.** One on every upper floor whose door fits under the wall top (1,709 on 830 lots), 1.40–1.95 m deep across the module, two posts from the yard to the top (the modules of a wide lot share the post between them) and, on some stacks, a flat galvanised roof (`Sheet_Metal`) over the top gallery. The deck is 4 cm of grey boards (`Wood_Deck`, collision) with a painted rim board 10 cm by 2.5 cm on the outer edge (the old deck edge was an 18 cm slab). The rim runs on across the stair openings, so no flight's soffit shows through the deck edge. Each guard is built as on the Plateau, 107 cm high (over 1.89 m above grade the MRC de Coaticook gallery sheet asks for 1,070 mm): a 2x4 bottom rail over a 5 cm drain gap and a top rail with its flat cap, both geometry, and between them the lot's infill. 70% of the lots take a `Rail_Baluster` cut-out card (2x2 balusters, 4,142 runs); the rest take a solid guard of lap siding, clapboard or aluminium with its laps showing (`Siding_Lap_Cream` or `Siding_Lap_Grey`, the lot's own siding on a sided lot; 2,072 runs, and every guard cut lower than 107 cm). The old solid guard was a flat `Wood_Grey` board that read as concrete. The card draws its own rails exactly where the geometry rails stand. Guards' rails, posts and rims are wood paint (grey, stained oak, green, burgundy, cream), never `Trim` white. Apartment blocks of five storeys or more get black steel galleries with iron pickets (251 runs), except the post-1945 blocks, which have balconies instead.
- **Screens and the props floor.** Side-by-side galleries are outdoor rooms screened from each other (`plan_partitions`): a privacy screen at every post two modules of a lot share, and where a neighbouring lot's gallery ends within 60 cm along the same wall line (300 such ends), halfway between the two ends. A screen between lots is drawn once, by the lot with the lower id at its deck levels (the other lot draws one only at a level 60 cm or more from any of the first's). It runs from the wall face to the outer guard line, of lattice (60% of lots: two `Rail_Lattice` cards of 1 m, as drawn, 2 m in all) or boards (one `Fence_Boards` card of 1.8 m, as drawn), under a 2x4 cap rail with a 2x4 stile at the guard end, in the gallery paint; 438 screens, none on the steel fire escapes. They have no collision; the end guards collide. Every deck (and ground-floor gallery) records the floor left for props, for the lane life's chairs, barbecues, plants and bikes: the deck inside its guards and posts less the stair opening, the flights over it below head height, 1 m past a flight's head, 1.01 m either side of the door's centre and the way between them (`free`, `free_m2`). 1,499 of the 1,709 decks have some, 6,988 m² in all (about 4.1 m² a deck); the test walks the player capsule over every deck with that floor taken as full.
- **Ground-floor galleries.** 40% of the straight stacks whose ground floor stands 50 cm or more up swap the concrete stoop for a low gallery across the module at the raised ground floor (71 modules): thin deck and rim board, solid steps down to the yard at the module's far end from the stair, a guard where the deck stands 60 cm or more, and a lattice skirt underneath (284 `Rail_Lattice` cards). Each skirt card keeps the 1 m height the lattice is drawn for, hangs under the rim board and runs below grade, so the diamonds are never squashed.
- **Stairs.** Every gallery is reached from the yard. *Straight* (403 stacks): a flight with risers of at most 22.5 cm runs outside the first gallery along its edge to a landing at its end; the flights above run inside the gallery's front band, each through an opening in the deck above, with a guard round the opening. The guard along the opening's inner side starts 1.1 m (`HEAD_EXIT`) from the gallery's end guard, so on a narrow lot, where the top landing is short, a player still steps off the top of the flight toward the door; the opening edge left unguarded there is at most about 1 m above the flight. Where the flight from that deck to the next passes over the far end of the opening, that end's guard is cut down to clear the flight's underside. No guard stands across a flight's treads (`test_guards_cut_where_flights_pass`). *Spiral* (491 stacks): a steel spiral of 1.4 m in the end bay of the gallery, a full turn per storey, stepping off onto each deck; it needs only 1.7 m of yard, so shallow yards get one. 30% of lots prefer the spiral. Each spiral tread keeps its `Wood_Deck` top with an iron pan 4 cm under it (16,453 treads). An iron outer stringer and a flat-bar rail follow the helix in segments of two treads, and an iron baluster stands on every second tread (7,150). Rail and balusters stay open at each deck exit. Straight treads are drawn as their top faces with a soffit under each flight.
- **Fences.** With a ruelle behind, the fence runs 2.9 m from the ruelle centreline, across the lot's full width, with a gate near the stair foot; a fence at a slant to the wall follows the nearer end. With yards back to back, it runs halfway to the next building, with no gate. With open ground behind (a court, a car park or an unmapped lane, which the old code left bare), the yard ends 11 m out, or halfway to anything nearer. Fence kinds:
  - *Kind and colour per lot.* The local review found both sides of the Mentana ruelle one continuous 1.8 m brown board fence. Each lot now draws its fence from a seed of its own (`FENCE_MIX`): weathered boards (27%), painted boards (23%), lattice-topped boards (13%), chain-link (23%) or slatted chain-link (14%); the low fences and open pads keep their own draws. Over the 537 lane fences that gives 162 low fences, 111 weathered board fences, 96 chain-link, 83 painted board fences, 44 slatted and 41 lattice-topped; no look takes more than a third. `fence` in `fronts.json` gives the `kind` and the board key (`boards`).
  - *Board fences* (1.65–1.95 m): the boards are one cut-out card per run, with 4x4 posts at most 8 ft (2.44 m) apart and a flat 2x6 cap rail as geometry. Weathered boards are `Fence_Boards` with posts in natural, grey or painted wood; painted boards take the same card on a key of their own, tinted green, barn red or blue-grey (`Fence_Boards_Green`, `_Red`, `_Blue`: the `texture` field names the board set, so the apply step makes each a tinted duplicate of it), with posts and cap in the matching paint.
  - *Lattice-topped boards* (1.8–1.95 m): the boards to a 2x4 mid rail, a 45 cm band of `Rail_Lattice` over it, then the cap (282 bands, side fences included). The band squeezes the 1 m lattice drawing to 45 cm, so its diamonds are flatter than the screens'.
  - *Chain-link* (1.2–1.8 m): galvanised mesh as `Chain_Link` cut-out cards about 40 cm tall stacked to the top rail (the lattice set on a key of its own at chain-link scale: u runs 2.5 tiles a metre, so the diamonds are about 3 cm), galvanised line posts every 3 m and a top rail; its gate is mesh cards in a galvanised frame. There is no chain-link texture set; the lattice drawing at that scale is the nearest one.
  - *Slatted chain-link* (1.5 m): a slat panel between galvanised posts and a top rail, green, grey or brown.
  - *Low picket fences* (107 cm, 162 lane edges): the gallery guard's rails and baluster card on posts 1.8 m apart.
  - *Gates*: a ledged-and-braced board gate between two taller gate posts (536): the leaf is one cut-out card 92 cm wide in the fence's board key (a picket card on a low fence), with two ledges and a brace on the yard side, the brace rising from the bottom ledge at the hinge side toward the latch side, as a braced gate is built. The ledges and brace are painted in a colour that is never natural wood and never the fence posts' own. The old leaf was one flat painted panel.
  - *Open lane edges*: 24% of the lane lots try to leave the lane edge open as a crushed-stone parking pad, 2.7 x 4.8 m (81 pads), clear of the galleries, stairs and buildings; where no pad fits, the lot gets a low fence instead. Another 14% get a low fence. So the lane edge breaks up.
  - *Side fences* run on the lateral lot lines from the house to the fence line, one per party line, in the lot's kind and colour (8,935 fence posts in all).
  - *Outbuildings*: sheds and garages stand against the fence with their doors and front walls on the lane, under galvanised roofs: a 3 x 5.4 m garage on a fifth of the lane lots with room for one, a shed on two fifths of the rest (28 garages and 156 sheds, from 6 and 130). The fence stops at them.
- **Ground cover.** Every yard (1,079 lots) is covered by flat patches that never overlap: the cells of one grid in the wall frame, from the skin face to the fence line and between the lateral lot lines, each in one material. The CGA split again, now on the yard. The mix is seeded: a lawn (`Yard_Grass`) with a paver patio (`Yard_Pavers`) by the house (35%), a deep patio (23%), a yard paved throughout (15%) or gravelled throughout (`Yard_Gravel`, 9%), or a lawn with a vegetable garden at the back (17%). Soil beds (`Yard_Soil`) of 60–90 cm run along the side fences and the back fence on most lots, and the parking pad is crushed stone. Cells inside a building are split and dropped; the shed's cell is left out. Patches stand 1.0 to 2.0 cm over the floor, beds and paving a touch higher. A yard too shallow for a fence clear of the galleries still gets its ground cover (47 lots). The district totals are 22,000 m² of lawn, 22,300 m² of pavers, 9,600 m² of soil and 5,900 m² of gravel.
- **Plants.** Virginia creeper (vigne vierge) grows on a fifth of the blank side walls of 3 m or more and on the lane side of a third of the fences (508 patches on plex lots, 76 on background masses). Each patch is a run of `Leaf_Creeper` cut-out cards with a broken outline: strips 35–90 cm wide, each a convex pentagon with a slanted, slightly peaked top, taller toward the middle of the patch. A few strips are left out as gaps, and a few narrow runners climb past the rest. On fences the cards stand clear of the posts and drape 18 cm over the top. The texture supplies the autumn scarlet, crimson and wine, with some green.
- **Cedars.** The local review found the cedars dark six-sided cones (`Wood_Green`). A small cedar (93) is now three crossed `Leaf_Cedar` cut-out cards at 60 degrees round its trunk, each one tile of the cedar set's drawing (plateau_textures.py draws one soft thuja column per 1 m tile on a 3 m card, its tip at 2.85 m): as tall as puts the drawn tip at the cedar's height (2.8–4.2 m) and a third as wide, centred on the trunk, within the 75 cm the yard plan keeps round it. Card UVs take v 0..1 up the card and u from the card's own end, one tile per card (`CARD_ASPECT`), so each card shows the drawing whole instead of wherever the world's metre grid falls. A cedar hedge (74) is a row of those columns on cards along both faces, across its ends and across it about every 1.2 m (the columns repeat at the drawing's aspect, about 60 cm apart), over a dark green core that stops at 60% of the height. 881 cards in all.
- **Placement rules.** Galleries and stairs keep 3 m from every drive, service and ruelle centreline (the Streets layer rule) and never overlap a building or a neighbour's structure (the band pass above then checks every collision face against the traffic graph too); fences, sheds and parking pads keep 2.8 m from a ruelle, 6.5 m from a named street and 2.5 m from a driveway, and stop at buildings. When nothing fits, `fronts.json` gives the reason: for the galleries (`reason`), no rear wall of 3 m or more and no long wall (224 lots, mostly walls on the lane or narrow wings), a long wall on a block over 4 storeys (2), a post-1945 block (6), a building, structure or lane behind the wall (116), or a yard too shallow or lot too narrow (130); for the yard (`yard_reason`), no rear wall of 2 m or more with 2.5 m in front of it (224), the fence line blocked (3) or a yard under 1 m deep (1).
- **Collision.** Gallery decks, landings, treads and the ground-floor galleries and their steps are `Wood_Deck`, so they join the complex-collision batches. Skins, fences, gates, screens, blinds, sheds, garages, roofs, ground cover and plants have none (the fourth pass's fences and outbuildings too). The guards are drawn without collision, and each guard run also gets an invisible wall (see *Collision*).
- **Records.** Each `rear` record in `fronts.json` lists the back walls with their line (`a`, `b`, `top_cm`), material, base, windows and doors, the decks (`decks`: `z`, the `quad`, the `door` spot, and the props floor: `free`, quads in map coordinates at the deck top, `free_m2`, the outer guard line `edge` and the unit vector `out` from the wall toward the yard), every flight with its foot and head in map coordinates, the gallery footprints, every guard run (`guards`: line and heights), the privacy screens (`partitions`: line `a` to `b` from the wall face, `z0`, `z1`, `kind`, `neighbour` lot id or `null` between modules), the ground-floor galleries (`ground_decks`: quad, height, steps, foot, head, guard, skirt cards, and the same `door`, `free`, `free_m2`, `edge` and `out`), the fence (`fence`: kind, edge, line, runs, gate and its paint, post count, shed; `null` for an open or fenceless lane edge), the side-fence segments, and the `yard` (`mix`, lane `edge`, ground cells in the wall frame, area per material, the yard and pad corners, the shed, and its `trees`). Each `roof` record gives the material, height, props and party runs (with the height a party skin starts from).

- **Lots with no yard.** The 228 lots with no rear wall to hang a yard on (wings, lane buildings, the deep blocks) used to leave bare floor round them. Each open back wall that is not a street wall now gets ground cover out to the ruelle's verge (2.9 m from its centreline, the fence line), halfway to the next building, or 11 m: a lawn along the wall, and where a ruelle or open ground lets a car in and the strip is 6 m deep with 5 m along the wall, asphalt parking (`Asphalt_Lot`) with white stall lines every 2.6 m across its last 5 m. The strip is cut into cells of at most 4 m along the wall, and a cell that meets a building, a gallery or shed, another lot's yard or ground, or a road's clearance is split and the blocked parts dropped, as the yards are. 133 lots get it: 3,435 m² of lawn and 1,264 m² of parking on 12 lots, 71 stall lines (`open_ground` on the lot's rear record, with the cells in map coordinates).

## Collision

- **Traffic band.** No collision face of this layer comes within 300 cm of a traffic centreline (`Data/LaFontaine/layout.json`), the Streets layer's rule and the spec's. The collision faces are grouped in units: a front stair with the stair-head balcony it reaches, a porch with its steps, each other balcony, and a lot's rear stack. Where any face of a unit comes nearer, the whole unit leaves collision, so no stair is half walkable: its `Wood_Deck` faces move to the `WoodDeckVisual_*` batches, which render the same and report `collision: none`, and its invisible walls are dropped. 135 units on 64 lots (2,011 faces), mostly porch steps and stairs in deep front yards on streets the traffic graph cuts close to the buildings, such as front 359274566 on Rue de Bordeaux (108 cm before) and 359305900 on Rue Cartier. The test reads every collision batch back from its OBJ text and measures it with the replica test's own `World().traffic_distance`.
- **Invisible walls.** `Collision_Only` geometry collides but never renders: the apply step spawns these batches on actors hidden in game that block pawns and ignore the camera and visibility channels. Every gallery guard run (outer edges, ends, landings, the guards round the stair openings, the ground-floor galleries' guards; 6,465 runs) gets one as high as the guard, as two faces 3 cm apart so a capsule meets it from either side; the open outer side of each flight above the first gets a sloped one on the gallery's outer guard line, up to the next deck's guard (379); and the stair-head balcony on the front gets one under each closed rail (1,270 runs). A player cannot walk off a gallery or into a stair opening; a jump still clears the 107 cm guard, as it would the real one. The first flight's open side and the spirals get none, since the 84 cm player capsule (radius 42 cm in `QuebecGameplay.cpp`) barely fits a 90 cm flight or a 1.4 m spiral already.
- **Ways out.** The test walks a 42 cm capsule over every gallery deck, kept clear of the mass wall and of every guard of that deck, from the head of the arriving flight to the door and from the door to the foot of the next flight (`decks[].door` gives the spot in front of the door).

## Yard trees

`yard_trees.json` holds 565 trees as the apply step reads them, `{x, y, z, yaw, scale, kind}`, in the replica frame (cm, degrees; `scale` multiplies the shared `tree_small_02` mesh, 4.55 m tall at 1). Each lot's `yard.trees` in `fronts.json` repeats its rows with the crown and height they were given. The trees come from the Ville de Montréal canopy survey:

- **Data.** *Canopée 2019* (CC BY 4.0, donneesquebec.ca `vmtl-canopee`, resource 77e44cc1) is the ground projection of every tree crown over 3 m, traced from the CMM's August 2019 aerial photos and summer 2018 lidar, as polygons in NAD83 MTM zone 8. `python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py --clip-canopy canopee-2019.zip` streams the published 72 MB archive (the GeoJSON is 823 MB, 514,124 features). It converts each ring with Snyder's transverse Mercator inverse and the map's `prepare_lafontaine.xy`, clips to the district bounds plus 10 m, simplifies at 40 cm (the survey traces a 0.75 m raster) and writes `canopy_2019_clip.json`: 1,166 polygons, 29,581 vertices, 379 kB, with the source, licence and archive hash. The build reads only that file. A top-down check against the OSM footprints and paths lines up: the park's paths run through the gaps in its canopy, the street-tree rows follow the sidewalks and the pond's outline shows.
- **Crowns.** The canopy is filled on a 1 m grid (even-odd scanlines, so holes stay open), and every canopy cell gets its chamfer distance to the canopy edge (Borgefors' 3-4 chamfer). A crown is a disc around a local maximum of that distance, largest first, the distance-transform split that separates merged blobs before a watershed. Only cells inside a plex yard can hold a trunk.
- **Rules.** A trunk keeps 2 m from every building, 0.9 m from galleries, stairs, decks, sheds, pads, hedges and cedars, 0.6 m from fences, 3 m from every drive, service and ruelle centreline and from the traffic graph, and 4 m from the 4,571 mapped trees. The tall trees draw a crown wider than the survey's, so the house and gallery clearances grow with the drawn crown (0.4 and 0.3 of its radius), and neighbours keep 0.55 of their summed drawn crowns apart. A lot holds at most three. None stands on a parking pad.
- **Kinds and size.** A crown under 6 m across is a `small` tree, 7.5–8.5 m tall (225). The others are mature: maple 55%, ash 30% and elm 15% by a seeded draw (201, 102 and 37; the fourth pass's extra garages and sheds took a few trunk spots), after Montréal's inventories, where maples and ash lead and elm is rare. They stand 12.5 to 18 m tall from the crown (7 m + 0.75 x crown), and at least 1.5 m over the roof of a lot of three storeys or fewer, so inside the blocks they rise above the 3-storey roofs. The apply step widens or narrows each kind's crown and gives the trees no collision, like the mapped trees.

## Background backs

The old `OSM Glass` cards are 130 × 160 cm quads 3 cm in front of every wall of 2.5 m or more of every mass (`Data/Plateau/BaseGeometry/Glass.obj`, 75,339 cards). The generator reads them, matches each card to its wall by that 3 cm offset (as `plateau_landmark_visuals.py` does), and treats every mass outside the layer that has a card no detailed front covers and no taller neighbour within 25 cm hides: the 154 park-perimeter buildings, 74 yard and lane buildings, 10 institutional buildings and outbuildings, 8 towers and 6 small buildings. Each gets the yard-side skin of a plex lot on every exposed wall that is not one of its detailed fronts: brick (the perimeter front's brick half the time, else common brick; buff or brown brick on the towers, cast stone on park pavilions and government buildings) or lap siding on a quarter of the rear and side walls (never above the second storey), a parged base, sheet-metal flashing, and windows on every wall with 1.2 m in front of it, one per 3 m bay and floor (7,199 windows; the old 4.5 m bay, 6,137 windows, read as blank boxes from above). The eight towers take the post-1945 kit instead: 3,576 windows and 1,533 balconies. The skin rises over the card generator's top row where it stands above a low mass's roof. Where such a mass stands over a lower neighbour, the party wall above the neighbour's roof is skinned too (26 runs). They get no galleries or yards. Each gets a roof (`author_background_roof`; the old map left the dark grey mass top): white membrane (151), white gravel ballast (90) or tar and gravel (11), a skin 2 cm over the mass top across its footprint, with a brick chimney (240) and a galvanised hatch (234) on the masses of four storeys or fewer, the post-1945 kit's lift penthouse (9) and vents on the eight towers, and nothing on the cast-stone park pavilions and government buildings. The props stand in the frame of the mass's longest edge; `background[].roof` records them.

The audit in `fronts.json` (`glass_cards`) and `summary.json` places every card: 31,088 hidden by a taller neighbour within 25 cm, 11,718 behind plex fronts, 27,560 behind back-wall, party and background skins, 4,493 behind the route and perimeter fronts, 393 on the tower and the school (whose cards `plateau_landmark_visuals.py` drops) and 87 inside the Théâtre de Verdure envelope. None floats on a blank wall. `test_plateau_plex.py` repeats the audit from the written records alone.

## Budget

3,165,020 triangles in 1,341 batches: 2,205,886 for the fronts and roofs (median 1,564 per front; the roofs are 90,546), 743,984 for the yard side and 215,150 for the background backs (creeper cards, the towers' kit and the roofs included). Largest batch 58,745.

- **Fourth pass, triangle cap unchanged at 3.2 million:** the pass nets 4,355 triangles fewer. The Habitations De Mentana's form has half the kit's balconies and open railing cards for its solid guards (the block drops from 23,146 to 12,326), which pays for the chain-link cards (4,724), the teal railings' rails and posts (4,864), the cedar cards (1,762, replacing the cones), the lattice bands' rails and cards, the painted-board cards, the hedges' cores and the extra garages and sheds.
- **Batch cap 1,350** (the agreed ceiling is 1,400), raised from 1,200 by the fourth pass: its eight keys (`Leaf_Cedar`, `Chain_Link` and the three painted fence-board keys on every fenced tile, and `Concrete_Exposed`, `Paint_Teal` and `Rail_Baluster_Teal` on the Mentana block) take 1,341 batches at the 190 m tile; under a 1,200 cap the tile would widen to 237.5 m and the `Iron` batches split at the 60,000-triangle batch cap.

- **Triangle cap 3.2 million** (the agreed ceiling is 3.5 million): the rear-surfaces pass adds 86,050 triangles: the blind and curtain cards behind 23,269 windows and patio doors (60,632), the background masses' roofs and roof props (11,420), the gallery privacy screens (7,554) and the board gates' ledges and braces (6,444). A blind is one quad and curtains two; a screen is one or two cut-out cards.

- **Earlier cap 3.15 million**: the walls-and-collision pass adds about 158,000 triangles: the invisible guard walls and rails (32,368), the post-1945 kit (balcony slabs, guards, patio doors, windows and panels on 6 blocks and 8 towers, most of the background's 110,000 more) and the 3 m window grid on the other background skins. Balcony guards are a cap rail over one panel, not pickets, and the invisible walls are single quads.

- **Earlier caps:** 2.5 million, then 2.75 million for the walls-and-roofs pass, then 2.95 million for the yards pass, which adds about 270,000 triangles. That covers guard rails, caps and rim boards (6,587 guard runs), fence posts and cap rails (9,082 posts), the spiral pans, stringers, rails and balusters (16,754 treads), and the ground cover and creeper cards. The spec's "about 2.5 million" for the plex layer is now exceeded by 17%.
- **Earlier batch cap 1,200** (was 860): the walls-and-roofs keys widened the tile to 190 m; the seven yards keys and the walls pass's `Asphalt_Lot`, `Collision_Only` and `WoodDeckVisual` batches stay under the cap at that tile (1,164 batches); the rear-surfaces pass adds no key, but its siding, lattice, board and roof keys reach new tiles (1,199 batches). A batch is one Nanite static-mesh actor, which is cheap.
- Kept cheap on purpose: the infill between guard rails, the boards of board fences, the lattice skirts and the creeper leaves are single cut-out cards (the masked materials are two-sided, so one face each; the terrace rail used to draw each card twice). Rails show only their sides and top. Posts stop under the cap that covers their tops. The spiral stringer faces out only, and the spiral rail is a flat bar. Rear windows and doors are applied to one wall plane instead of cut into it. Treads are top faces. The gallery posts run in one piece from the yard to the top. Ground patches are one quad per cell and never overlap. A party coping drops its face on the party line where the neighbour's coping hides it.

## UVs

UVs are in metres: vertical faces take u along the face and v = z, horizontal faces take x and y (the ground cover and the roofs), and `Leaf_Creeper` tiles the same way on its wall or fence plane. The cut-out card keys `Rail_Baluster`, `Rail_Lattice` and `Fence_Boards` take u along the run in metres and v from 0 on the card's lower edge to 1 on its upper edge. The baluster cards are 107 cm and the lattice cards 100 cm, as drawn. Fence cards are 1.65–1.95 m, against the 1.8 m drawing.
