# Plex: Plateau plex fronts and rears on the plain buildings

`python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py` writes this folder: 853 OBJ batches (git-ignored, rebuilt byte for byte), `manifest.json` (batches, materials, counts, source and geometry SHA-256), `summary.json` (fronts by type, stair shape, crown, storeys histogram and sources, streets covered, and a `rear` block) and `fronts.json` (one record per front, including each exterior stair's head and foot in map coordinates, and a `rear` record per lot). `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_plex.py` checks it; `--quick` runs only the fixtures.

## What it covers

The 1,407 inside buildings that have no detailed front yet: everything except the 100 Panet/Visitation route fronts, the 154 park-facing perimeter fronts, the Le Neuville tower and École Le Plateau. 1,307 get a front facing their named street, plus 383 corner side fronts. 100 are skipped: 75 have no exposed edge facing a named street (yard and lane buildings), 11 are institutional buildings or outbuildings, 8 are towers over 24 m and 6 have footprints under 25 m². The classic plex streets are covered: Mentana 135, Saint-André 103, Cartier 83, Saint-Christophe 76, Fabre 66, Dorion 62, Garnier 59 and others.

Each front is a visual skin. Glass, door leaves and recess backs sit 4 cm in front of the retained collision mass, 1 cm ahead of the old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards. The masonry face sits 16 cm out, so the windows read as 12 cm recesses. Stair treads, stoops, porch decks and balcony decks (`Wood_Deck`) are a separate collision batch, `collision: complex`, which means complex-as-simple like the existing `*_Collision` batches. Every other batch has no collision. The masses keep their back and side walls. Where the plex front stands taller than its retained mass, a brick band and a flat cap close the gap above the mass top (`top_extensions`); on the back walls the rear skin rises to that height instead.

## Rules and data

- **Storeys:** OSM `building:levels` (597 fronts). Then the assessment roll joined by address, `assessment_roll_join.json` (468). Then the 2016 municipal LOD2 height as round((h − 4.9 m) / 3.26 m), an offset and increment measured from the LOD2-versus-levels medians (108). Otherwise 3 storeys (134). When the data storeys would leave more than 1.5 m of the retained 9.6 m mass showing above the crown, one storey is added (142 fronts, flagged in `storeys_source`).
- **Era:** the roll's construction year (1,090 fronts), or a seeded draw from the roll's decade histogram. Era selects the crown (wood cornice before 1890; pressed-metal cornice or slate *fausse mansarde* 1890–1905; metal cornice or parapet 1906–1915; raised brick parapet with a cast-stone medallion 1916–1939; flat parapet after that), the masonry mix (research-plateau.md §9) and the lintels (painted wood, stone or cast stone).
- **Front yard:** distance from the façade to the parallel OSM `footway=sidewalk` centreline, minus 1 m (1,207 fronts), or a road-width estimate. Exterior stairs need at least 1.05 m and stay entirely inside that yard. Curved stairs run along the wall and turn toward the street at the foot, for yards under 3 m. Straight stairs are used at 3 m and more. Lot-line fronts (yard under 1.05 m) get cantilevered balconies over the sidewalk and doors near grade. Stairs go to about 92% of 1885–1944 duplexes and triplexes, half of the 1880–1884 ones and a quarter of the 1870s ones. Among 1890–1930 residential fronts on the plex streets, 73% have one.
- **Composition:** one plex per 7.63 m lot module (wide footprints are split). The ground floor is raised 75–105 cm over a stone base with basement vents. Storeys are 320–330 cm. The stair-head balcony on the 2nd floor carries paired doors with transoms. Each upper floor has a full-width wooden balcony, 1.1–1.5 m deep, with 86 cm iron or painted railings, on consoles or (1912–1939) on columns. Windows are double-hung or casement with a ⅓ transom, with stone sills and lintels. Corner buildings on arterials, and lots with a mapped shop node, get a dépanneur-style ground floor: shop windows, a sign band, an awning and a blade sign.
- **Palette:** six brick colours, grey limestone and cast stone, white trim, black iron, five painted colours, grey deck paint, glass and slate, from research-plateau.md §9.
- **Masonry mix:** the §9 split read too red in the street previews (`Preview/Replica/`), so brown, buff and stone take a few points from red within each era. Before 1890: 15% limestone, then red, dark red, brown and orange in equal parts. 1890–1915: 32% limestone, 20% red, 6% dark red, 16% brown, 6% dark brown, 15% buff, 5% orange. 1916–1939: 30% brown, 15% dark brown, 18% red, 25% buff, 12% orange. District result: 333 limestone, 358 brown, 182 buff and 434 red or orange fronts (was 317, 241, 133 and 616).

## The yard side

The rear of each lot is built after all the fronts, with its own seed, so the fronts stay as they were. The street-level model is the back of a Plateau plex as the ruelle shows it (research-plateau.md §13, the Plateau-Mont-Royal and Villeray *ruelles vertes* guides): plain brick or painted siding, a door and a window or two on each floor, a wooden or steel gallery on every upper floor held by square posts from the yard, a steep straight wooden flight or a steel spiral, a yard closed toward the ruelle by a fence with a gate, and here and there a shed (*hangar*), a garage, a cedar hedge, a small cedar or a creeper.

- **Back walls.** Every exposed wall that is not a street front, a party wall or covered by a neighbour gets a plane 16 cm proud of the retained mass, up to the mass top or the front's extension top, with flashing on top. The old 3 cm `OSM Glass` cards on those walls are behind it. The walls are sorted the way Müller et al. sort a mass's faces: `rear` (facing away from the front, 1,708), `lane` (standing on a ruelle, 311), `street` (on a street that is not the lot's front, 283) and `side` (2,195). Rear, lane and street walls get applied windows (frame, glass and a proud sill): one per floor on a secondary rear wall, one per 4.5 m bay and floor on lane and street walls. Side walls stay blank, a fifth of the long ones with a creeper. Skins meeting round a convex corner end flush, so there is no 16 cm notch.
- **Rear façade.** On each lot, the longest rear wall of 3 m or more gets the full treatment if its yard allows (749 lots). Per 7.63 m module: a half-glazed door on every floor, stacked, and one or two windows; door and window heads line up; a concrete stoop with side steps at the ground-floor door.
- **Galleries.** One on every upper floor whose door fits under the wall top (1,566), 1.40–1.95 m deep across the module, with grey deck boards (`Wood_Deck`, collision), solid board guards 107 cm high (over 1.89 m above grade the MRC de Coaticook gallery sheet asks for 1,070 mm), two posts from the yard to the top (the modules of a wide lot share the post between them) and, on 328 stacks, a flat roof over the top gallery. Apartment blocks of five storeys or more get black steel galleries.
- **Stairs.** Every gallery is reached from the yard. *Straight* (380 stacks): a flight with risers of at most 22.5 cm runs outside the first gallery along its edge to a landing at its end; the flights above run inside the gallery's front band, each through an opening in the deck above, with a guard round the opening. *Spiral* (435 stacks): a steel spiral of 1.4 m in the end bay of the gallery, a full turn per storey, stepping off onto each deck; it needs only 1.7 m of yard, so shallow yards get one. 30% of lots prefer the spiral. Treads are drawn as their top faces with a soffit under straight flights.
- **Yard.** With a ruelle behind (621 lots), the fence runs 2.9 m from the ruelle centreline, across the lot's full width, with a gate near the stair foot; a fence at a slant to the wall follows the nearer end. With yards back to back (258), it runs halfway to the next building, with no gate. Board fences (591) are 1.65–1.95 m high in natural, weathered or painted wood; slatted chain-link (288) is 1.5 m with a galvanised top rail. Side fences run on the lateral lot lines from the house to that line, one per party line (1,021 runs). 134 sheds and 10 garages stand against the fence with their doors on the lane. 58 cedar hedges, 88 small cedars and 571 creepers (autumn red or green) on fences and blank walls.
- **Placement rules.** Galleries and stairs keep 3 m from every drive, service and ruelle centreline (the Streets layer rule) and never overlap a building or a neighbour's structure; fences and sheds keep 2.8 m from a ruelle, 6.5 m from a named street and 2.5 m from a driveway, and stop at buildings. When nothing fits, `fronts.json` gives the reason: no rear wall of 3 m or more (377 lots, mostly walls on the lane or narrow wings), a building, structure or lane behind the wall (93), or a yard too shallow or lot too narrow (88).
- **Collision.** Gallery decks, landings and treads are `Wood_Deck`, so they join the complex-collision batches. Skins, fences, sheds and plants have none.
- **Records.** Each `rear` record in `fronts.json` lists the back walls with their windows and doors, the deck quads and heights, every flight with its foot and head in map coordinates, the gallery footprints, the fence line, runs, gate and shed, and the side-fence segments.

## Budget

2,444,527 triangles in 853 batches: 2,130,619 for the fronts (median 1,496 per front) and 313,908 for the yard side (median 342 per lot with galleries). Largest batch 40,688. The base grid is 47.5 m. The 18 material slots need the tile widened to 142.5 m to stay under 860 batches; rear common brick is red or brown only, so the yard side adds few new tile slots. To stay under 2.5 million, rear windows and doors are applied to one wall plane instead of cut into it, guards and fences are two faces without a top, treads are top faces, and the posts run in one piece from the yard to the top.

## Sources and limits

Footprints and addresses: © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0 (`Data/LaFontaine/osm.xml`). Heights: Ville de Montréal *Bâtiments 3D 2016* LOD2, CC BY 4.0 (`Data/CityBuildings`). Year built and storeys: Ville de Montréal, *Unités d'évaluation foncière*, CC BY 4.0, joined to OSM addresses. Rebuild the join with `python3 Scripts/plateau_plex.py --join-roll <datastore dump.json>`, from the CKAN `datastore_search` rows for `NO_ARROND_ILE_CUM = REM21`. Dimensions come from the Plateau-Mont-Royal heritage sheets (December 2015). The façade grammar follows Müller et al., *Procedural Modeling of Buildings* (SIGGRAPH 2006), and the CityEngine `split` idea; the yard side uses the same paper's component split (`comp(f)`) into street, lane, side and party faces and its occlusion rule (no openings where a neighbour abuts). Rear look: research-plateau.md §13, the Plateau-Mont-Royal and Villeray–Saint-Michel–Parc-Extension *ruelles vertes* guides (Ville de Montréal), the MRC de Coaticook SARP sheet *Les galeries et les balcons* (guard heights, lattice, partly closed galleries) and press accounts of Montréal's exterior stairs and of the ruelle *hangars* the city removed.

Brick and paint colours, door and window rhythm, stair side and shape, and cornice details are rule-and-seed estimates. They are not surveys of individual buildings. The same holds for the rear: gallery depth, stair kind, fences, sheds and plants are seeded, and lot lines are not mapped, so the rear lot line is the ruelle or the midpoint to the next building. Yards stay the bare floor (no lawn, patio or paving), the guards are solid boards (no balusters, no lattice), and the buildings this layer does not cover (the Panet/Visitation route, the park perimeter, the 100 skipped buildings) keep their old glass cards on their backs, as on the park side of the ruelle behind Rachel. The geometry keeps the map's mirrored frame (x east, y north).
