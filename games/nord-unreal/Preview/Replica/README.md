# Plateau replica previews

These are Blender previews of the generated replica layers, not Unreal captures. The layers have not been imported or checked in Unreal yet, so the editor captures listed in `Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/README.md` are still owed.

## The game frame, not the real city

The game world is a mirror image of the real Plateau (`openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/notes-mirror.md`). These images show it the way the player will see it in Unreal, not the way the street looks in Montréal. For example:

- Facing Montréal-north on Papineau, the park is on your right. In the real city it is on your left.
- Signs that stand on the right of the lane in Montréal stand on the left of the lane here, as the ARRÊT in `corner.jpg` does.
- Text still reads left to right: the props are modelled for the mirrored world (`Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Props/README.md`).

When the mirror fix lands, every view flips left to right.

## How they are made

`Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py` builds each view. Run it with a Python that can `import bpy` (Blender 5.1), from `games/nord-unreal`:

```bash
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --list
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --samples 24 --threads 4           # fabre.jpg
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --before --samples 32              # fabre_before.jpg
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view ruelle_mentana --probe 640,600 560,640   # what two pixels show
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view gallery_closeup --camera=X,Y,Z,TX,TY,TZ  # another camera
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_trees.py --render-only                                            # trees_contact_sheet.png
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_props.py --out <scratch folder>                                   # props_contact_sheet.png
```

The first run indexes the OBJ bounds into `Saved/ReplicaPreview/`, and the first `--before` run also saves the old tree's model there. `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_replica_preview.py` checks the script against a small fake `bpy`, so it runs without Blender (see *Checks*). `plateau_props.py` writes its OBJs and manifest to `--out`, so a scratch folder keeps the committed props untouched.

- **Geometry.** Every OBJ is placed as written: (x, −y, z) of a game-frame point. Blender is right-handed and Unreal is left-handed, so this shows the mirrored world the way Unreal does. Each view loads the faces that reach its region: a disc of 45 m around the camera plus a wedge 22° wider than the field of view on each side, usually out to 90–170 m. A face is kept when a corner is in the region, when an edge crosses it, or when it holds the camera, so a long lane quad whose corners lie behind the camera and past the far distance stays. The OBJ index forgets files a generator has removed. It never caches tree positions.
- **Existing layers** are the ones visible in the game: the background masses, `OSM Road`, `OSM Path`, `OSM Glass`, the ground and park lawn (`GroundGeometry`), and the park, perimeter, context and landmark batches. The script leaves out what the game hides or replaces: the `OSM Brick/Cream/Sage/Roof` masses, the old canopies, and `OSM Grass`, whose mesh `plateau_ground.py` replaces with `ParkGrass`. `OSM Marking` appears only in *before* images, because the replica apply step hides it.
- **Replica layers** are `Streets`, `Plex` and `Soul`, read from their manifests. `Collision_Only` batches (the invisible walls on the gallery guards, open stair sides and stair-head rails) are never read or drawn: the apply step spawns them hidden in game. The Furniture and Soul `placements.json` rows are instanced from the `Props` meshes at position (x, −y, z) and yaw −yaw, each part in the colour variant the apply step picks from the row's `variant_seed` (`[material] + variants`, indexed by the seed modulo their count). A part the row leaves out (`without`) is not drawn, as the apply step does not place it: a cobra head on a wooden city pole has no steel pole, and a sign hung on a city pole has no post. The street-name text that the apply step adds as `TextRender` is drawn as flat black text in the same `text_slots`.
- **Shadows** follow the apply step. A layer batch casts a shadow only when `apply_plateau_replica.casts_shadow` says so: the asphalt, paint, joints, curb tops, cracks, patches, the ruelle gutter and the `Leaf_Litter` patches cast none. A prop casts one only if the apply step leaves its instanced actor shadowed (`PROP_SHADOW`, which the preview imports): the lamps, utility poles, BIXI docks and bikes, benches, hydrants, the four sign kinds, locked bikes, the three bins, mailboxes, cones and the chantier fence, 18 props in all. Gallery and yard props (chairs, barbecues, plants, dishes) cast none. Every street and yard tree casts one.
- **Trees.** Every tree stands where and how the apply step places it (`apply_plateau_replica.plan_trees`):
  - The 4,682 street and park trees of `Streets/street_trees.json` (the city inventory, CC BY 4.0, and the OSM trees it lacks) each stand on their kind's species mesh from `Trees/manifest.json`. The kinds are `norway_maple`, `silver_maple`, `honey_locust`, `linden`, `ash`, `elm` (the vase form, for elm and hackberry), `small`, `conifer` (spruces, pines, firs, cedars and hemlocks) and `larch`. A tree under `YOUNG_BELOW` of its kind's mature height stands on the kind's young mesh, which wears the kind's bark and leaves; that is 1,600 trees. Each tree sits on the ground under its trunk (pit soil, sidewalk or lawn), with its yaw and the height and trunk-spread scale of `street_tree_scale`.
  - **Fall colour.** Each row's `fall` puts it in one of the plan's groups per kind, mesh and fall. A kind's own fall (yellow for the broadleaf kinds, evergreen for the conifer, gold for the larch) wears the kind's leaves. Another fall wears that group's tinted leaves (`fall_leaves`): the kind's leaf atlas with a tint that brings the kind's cells to the fall's colour, so red maples, Amur maples, serviceberries and Freeman maples turn red and the oaks russet. That is 453 trees in seven groups and four leaf materials: `Tree_Leaves_Autumn_norway_maple_red` (`#A1352B`), `_silver_maple_red` (`#9A371E`), `_small_red` (`#AF5F39`) and `_linden_russet` (`#884027`). The preview builds one tree collection per group, on the kind's own OBJ, with only the leaves slot changed, as the apply step sets the tinted leaves on that group's actor.
  - The 565 Plex yard trees of `Plex/yard_trees.json` stand on their species meshes, scaled uniformly to their row's height. The old kind `maple` maps to `norway_maple`.
  - Each species OBJ splits into its `bark` and `leaves` groups (`apply_plateau_replica.tree_slot`). It keeps the normals the generator bent to the crown, and takes the textured bark and the masked, two-sided autumn leaf atlas. A mesh is read only when one of its trees is in the view.
  - The old summer tree (Poly Haven `tree_small_02`) appears only in `--before` images, or when `street_trees.json` does not exist. That matches the game: once the street trees stand, the apply step clears `QuebecVegetation`'s instances. Before that step, the saved map's stand-in holds it on the 4,571 OSM tree nodes (`plateau_rendering.osm_tree_positions()`), so a *before* image puts it there, with that file's scale and yaw and the summer leaf material `refine_plateau_trees.py` gives it.
- **Fallen leaves.** The Soul layer's `Leaf_Litter` patches use the masked, two-sided `leaf_litter` set on world-metre UVs that tile both ways (2 m a tile), like every other set (below). The patches overlap at one height by design, so overlaps show the same leaves with no seam. Unreal rasterises both faces to the same texel. A path tracer does not: a bounce ray leaving one patch meets the coplanar patch at once and reads it as cover, so the first renders of the fifth pass showed every overlap as a black polygon (its first `papineau.jpg`, x 1010–1230, y 555–620, and `mentana.jpg` x 460–615, y 598–620). The preview therefore shows the litter to camera rays only (`CAMERA_ONLY`). It still receives light, and it casts no shadow in game anyway.
- **Colours and textures.** Replica materials take the apply step's own plan row for their key (`apply_plateau_replica.material_row`). Asphalt and brick multiply their Poly Haven texture by the target colour divided by the texture mean, capped at `TINT_LIMIT` (8). Every key that takes a replica texture set is wired the way `apply_plateau_replica.textured_graph` wires it. That covers `Stone`, `Slate`, the shared keys (among them `Brick_Buff_Even`, the fourth pass's `Concrete_Lane_Aged`, `Leaf_Cedar`, `Plant_Grass_Fountain`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, `Iron_Wrought` and `Cloth_Laundry`, and the fifth pass's `Leaf_Litter`), the plan's other textured keys such as `Rail_Baluster_Teal` and `Concrete_Exposed`, and the trees' bark and leaves, the tinted fall leaves among them:
  - the set's `diff`, `nor_dx` and `rough` maps from `Textures/` (or `Trees/` for the bark and the leaf atlas), with the row's tiling (1 / tile size on metre UVs, 1 / tile length by 1 on the card UVs, 1 on the atlases);
  - the set's address modes (`apply_plateau_replica.address_modes`). Tiling sets wrap, `Leaf_Litter` among them. The laundry and leaf atlases clamp both axes (the image's *Extend* mode). Cards wrap U and clamp V. Blender has one extension for both axes, so for a card V is clamped to the centres of the first and last texel rows before a *Repeat* lookup, which is what a clamped V samples: the top of a fence, grass, flower or iron card never blends in its bottom row;
  - base colour = diffuse × the row's tint: colour / `mean_linear` capped at 8, or white for a set that owns its colour (`Roof_Tar`, `Cloth_Laundry`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, `Leaf_Litter`);
  - the macro variation of `add_macro` on the sets that have one (`Brick_Buff_Even`, `Concrete_Lane_Aged`, `Leaf_Litter`, the roofs and the crushed stone): the tint × lerp(1 − s, 1 + s, mask R at a 17.3 m tile) × lerp(1 − s, 1 + s, mask G at a 5.3 m tile) from `T_Replica_Macro_mask`;
  - roughness from the red channel of `rough`, the row's metallic (the manifest's, capped by the set: `Sheet_Metal` at 0.3), and the normal map read with the Normal Map node's DirectX convention, as Unreal reads it;
  - for the masked sets (`Rail_Baluster`, `Rail_Lattice`, `Fence_Boards`, `Leaf_Creeper`, `Leaf_Cedar`, `Plant_Grass_Fountain`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, `Iron_Wrought`, `Cloth_Laundry`, `Leaf_Litter` and the tree leaves), a texel is kept only where the diffuse alpha reaches the set's clip (0.5), through a Mix Shader with a Transparent BSDF. The alpha is read channel-packed, so the colour under a hole is never premultiplied. Cycles shades both faces, as the two-sided masked materials do;
  - the two-sided foliage sets (`Leaf_Creeper`, `Leaf_Cedar`, `Plant_Grass_Fountain`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, the tree leaves) also transmit light: a Translucent BSDF of base colour × 0.65 is added, the foliage parent's `SubsurfaceColor`.

  Every other replica material is a flat colour, including `Stone_Cast`, `Wood_Deck`, `Trim` and the wood paints. Existing materials use the colours and textures that `upgrade_plateau.py` gives them. Daylight emissive lenses are dimmed to 8 % of their Unreal strength.
- **Light.** An autumn afternoon: a Hosek-Wilkie sky and a warm sun from the south-south-west, 31° up, unless the view says otherwise.
- **Probe.** `--probe X,Y ...` builds the view and, instead of rendering, casts a camera ray through each pixel and prints the batch, material, game-frame point (cm), normal and whether the face is seen from behind. Rays pass through the clear texels of the masked cards and leaves, read with the set's address modes, as the clipped material does. A tree's object name tells its kind, mesh and fall: `T_linden_young_russet_bark` is a young tree of the linden's russet group.
- **Checks.** `Scripts/test_plateau_replica_preview.py` (stdlib, 4–10 s) checks:
  - every replica-set material against the apply step's plan row: maps and their folder, the address modes (the exact V clamp on cards, *Extend* on the atlases), tiling, tint, macro variation, metallic, mask and transmission. Each of the six fourth-pass keys is read from the manifest that uses it, and `Leaf_Litter` is among the masked keys;
  - the yard trees on their species meshes, and the bark and leaves slots and triangle counts of every species OBJ, mature and young, the conifer and larch among them;
  - every one of the 4,682 street trees on its kind's mature or young mesh with the plan's transform and the per-kind counts of `street_trees.json`; the counts per kind and fall; one collection per kind, mesh and tinted leaves, exactly the plan's tinted groups, with red and russet among them; a tinted collection reading the kind's own OBJ with the kind's bark and the group's leaves; and each tinted leaf material built as the kind's leaves are, with its own tint;
  - no old summer tree beside the species trees, and in a `--before` build the old tree on the 4,571 OSM tree nodes, not at the inventory rows;
  - that the young mesh is read from the kind's young OBJ and wears the kind's materials;
  - that the OBJ index forgets removed files and drops cached tree positions;

- **Technique sources.**
  - The cut-out wiring follows the usual Cycles setup for clipped transparency: image alpha into the factor of a Mix Shader with a Transparent BSDF, clipped at 0.5 (Katsbits, *Cycles, Transparency & Blender 4.x+*, https://www.katsbits.com/codex/?p=8373). The image's *Channel Packed* alpha mode keeps the colour and alpha channels independent (Blender Manual, *Image Settings*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/image/image_settings.html).
  - The per-axis clamp copies what Unreal's `TA_Clamp` does, the OpenGL clamp-to-edge rule. Coordinates are held between the centres of the edge texels, [1/(2N), 1 − 1/(2N)], so a linear filter never reaches past the edge (SGI, *SGIS_texture_edge_clamp*, https://www.opengl.org/registry/specs/SGIS/texture_edge_clamp.txt; Khronos forum, *Understanding of GL_CLAMP_TO_…*, https://community.khronos.org/t/understanding-of-gl-clamp-to/75971). Blender's image node clamps both axes at once with *Extend* (Blender Manual, *Image Texture Node*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/textures/image.html), so a card clamps V with a Clamp node and keeps *Repeat* for U.
  - The foliage translucency stands in for Unreal's two-sided foliage model, where the subsurface colour sets how much light passes through (Epic, *Shading Models*, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine).
  - The face-keeping rule is the per-primitive frustum test made exact for large faces. A corner-only test misses faces that span the view (Paul Bourke, *Frustum Culling*, https://paulbourke.net/miscellaneous/frustum/; Cesium, *Tighter Frustum Culling*, https://cesium.com/blog/2017/02/02/tighter-frustum-culling-and-why-you-may-want-to-disregard-it/). So each edge is clipped to the wedge's two half-planes the way Liang and Barsky clip a line parametrically (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang%E2%80%93Barsky_algorithm; Liang, Barsky and Slater, UC Berkeley 1992, https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1992/6271.html). A face that holds the camera is found with the crossings (even-odd) test (Haines, *Point in Polygon Strategies*, Graphics Gems IV, 1994, https://erich.realtimerendering.com/ptinpoly/).
  - The shadow rules and the camera-only litter use Cycles' per-object ray visibility. Turning off an object's *Shadow* rays stops it casting shadows, and the other ray types hide it from bounces while the camera still sees it. Instanced objects keep the visibility of the objects they instance, so a prop's parts carry its rule (Blender Manual, *Object Data: Visibility*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/cycles/object_settings/object_data.html; checked with a small scene in Blender 5.1). Why coplanar faces go dark: excluding the face a ray leaves "does not deal with false positives against adjacent (near) coplanar triangles" (NVIDIA, *Solving Self-Intersection Artifacts in DirectX Raytracing*, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/solving-self-intersection-artifacts-in-directx-raytracing; Wächter and Binder, *A Fast and Robust Method for Avoiding Self-Intersection*, Ray Tracing Gems, 2019, https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2019-03_fast-and-robust-method-avoiding-self-intersection). Two patches at one height are exactly that case.
  - The macro variation, the leaf-card normals, the species trees, the fall tints and the litter texture follow the apply step, the texture generator, the tree generator and the replica generator, which name their own sources (`Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Textures/README.md`, `Trees/README.md`, `openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/design.md`).

  No code was taken from any of them. The last fifth-pass set adapted no new technique: it re-rendered and re-probed the views with the script as it stood.

## Limits of these previews

- Shading is Cycles path tracing, not Lumen. There is no comic post-process, wet street, exposure adaptation or fog.
- Cycles shades every face from both sides. Unreal culls the back of one-sided materials, so a face wound the wrong way shows here but not in the game; `--probe` reports `seen_from_behind` for the faces it hits.
- Cycles samples the full-resolution card and leaf textures many times per pixel, which stands in for Unreal's alpha-coverage mips but is not the same filter.
- Only faces that reach each camera's region are loaded, so the far side of the park in `papineau.jpg` and the ends of long streets and lanes stop at bare ground or sky.
- The existing materials' colours are the `upgrade_plateau.py` palette or plausible stand-ins (the park lawn, `ParkGrass`, reads tan in `papineau.jpg` x 0–1050, y 380–440), not the Unreal materials themselves.
- Unreal's Nanite, LOD and cull distances are not reproduced, and every tree is drawn at full detail. The shadow rules are, but not the shadow method (virtual shadow maps) or Lumen's screen-space contact shading.
- The litter is hidden from bounce rays, so it adds no warm bounce light to the walls round it. In the game it would add a little.
