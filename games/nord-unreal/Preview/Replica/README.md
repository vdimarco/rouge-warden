# Plateau replica previews

These are Blender previews of the generated replica layers, not Unreal captures. The layers have not been imported or checked in Unreal yet, so the editor captures listed in `Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/README.md` are still owed.

## The game frame, not the real city

The game world is a mirror image of the real Plateau (`openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/notes-mirror.md`). These images show it the way the player will see it in Unreal, not the way the street looks in Montréal. For example:

- Facing Montréal-north on Papineau, the park is on your right. In the real city it is on your left.
- Signs that stand on the right of the lane in Montréal stand on the left of the lane here, as the ARRÊT in `corner.jpg` does.
- Text still reads left to right: the props are modelled for the mirrored world (`Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Props/README.md`).

When the mirror fix lands, every view flips left to right.

## Parked cars

The curbs are parked. Each legal curb space in `Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Streets/parked_cars.json` (`Scripts/plateau_parking.py`, 2,025 spaces) holds a car in these images. **The game fills these spaces at runtime.** `AQuebecParkedCars` (`Source/Quebec/QuebecParkedCars.cpp`) reads the same spaces from the generated `Source/Quebec/LaFontaineParking.h` when the map starts. No car is placed in the map file. The preview draws each space the way that fill draws it:

- **The car.** Each car uses the `AQuebecCar` fleet in `Data/SeventiesCars`, with the space's `body` (0 fastback coupe, 1 notchback sedan, 2 long-roof wagon) and `paint` (`M_Car70_Paint0` to `3`: teal, red, ochre, navy).
- **Paint.** The body and roof take the paint on their `Paint` slots. The cabin, tyres, rims, lamps and chrome keep their own surfaces from the fleet manifest.
- **Placement.** The pivot stands `PivotHeight` (100.5 cm) over the road point, turned to the space's yaw. The tyres and rims sit at the wheel centres and are rolled 90°.
- **Rules from the C++.** The preview reads these numbers from `Source/Quebec/QuebecParkedRules.h`, the header the game compiles.
- **Shadows.** The body, cabin and roof cast shadows. The tyres and rims do not, as in the game.
- **Cull distances.** Each part disappears past its cull end, measured from the camera to the car's pivot: rims at 35 m, tyres at 60 m, and the body, cabin and roof at 90 m. The game also fades parts out over the last 10 m. The preview has no fade: it draws a part whole up to its cull end. In `aerial.jpg`, 60 m up, 44 spaces lie within 90 m of the camera, but all of them are below the frame, so no car shows.

The game also leaves a space empty when something already stands in it at load: the starter car, a saved car, one of the 32 runtime parked cars, or a runtime street prop (the cones and barricades of the two runtime chantiers, the curb bins). The preview cannot know those, so it draws every space. Traffic, people and dogs are not drawn.

## How they are made

`Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py` builds each view. Run it with a Python that can `import bpy` (Blender 5.1), from `games/nord-unreal`:

```bash
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --list
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --samples 24 --threads 4           # fabre.jpg
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --before --samples 32              # fabre_before.jpg
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view ruelle_mentana --probe 640,600 560,640   # what two pixels show
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view gallery_closeup --camera=X,Y,Z,TX,TY,TZ  # another camera
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_trees.py --render-only                                            # trees_contact_sheet.png
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_props.py --out <scratch folder>                                   # props_contact_sheet.png
```

The first run indexes the OBJ bounds into `Saved/ReplicaPreview/`, and the first `--before` run also saves the old tree's model there. `python3 Scripts/test_plateau_replica_preview.py` checks the script against a small fake `bpy`, so it runs without Blender (see *Checks*). `plateau_props.py` writes its OBJs and manifest to `--out`, so a scratch folder keeps the committed props untouched.

<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --fov 60 --res 960x540 --camera=44688.8,-29209.3,160,44726.4,-28876.3,40     # three leaf bags by a blue bin at the curb
<blender-python> Scripts/blender/plateau_replica_preview.py --view fabre --fov 60 --res 960x540 --camera=51330.9,28587.6,188,51011.3,28382.0,120     # a carved pumpkin on a stoop landing

- **Geometry.** Every OBJ is placed as written: (x, −y, z) of a game-frame point. Blender is right-handed and Unreal is left-handed, so this shows the mirrored world the way Unreal does. Each view loads the faces that reach its region: a disc of 45 m around the camera plus a wedge 22° wider than the field of view on each side, usually out to 90–170 m. A face is kept when a corner is in the region, when an edge crosses it, or when it holds the camera, so a long lane quad whose corners lie behind the camera and past the far distance stays. The OBJ index forgets files a generator has removed. It never caches tree positions.

- **Trees.** Every tree stands where and how the apply step places it (`apply_plateau_replica.plan_trees`):

A kind's own fall (yellow for the broadleaf kinds, evergreen for the conifer, gold for the larch) wears the kind's leaves. So red maples, Amur maples, serviceberries and Freeman maples turn red, the oaks russet, and the 17 Colorado blue spruces (*Picea pungens* and its 'Glauca' form) blue-grey (`#6F8E9A`).   - the set's address modes (`apply_plateau_replica.address_modes`). Tiling sets wrap, `Leaf_Litter` among them. The laundry and leaf atlases clamp both axes (the image's *Extend* mode). Cards wrap U and clamp V. Blender has one extension for both axes, so for a card V is clamped to the centres of the first and last texel rows before a *Repeat* lookup, which is what a clamped V samples: the top of a fence, grass, flower or iron card never blends in its bottom row;

The repair's matt lane concrete (`rough` about 0.86 dry, 0.75 in the damp channel) comes in this way with no preview change;
  - for the masked sets (`Rail_Baluster`, `Rail_Lattice`, `Fence_Boards`, `Leaf_Creeper`, `Leaf_Cedar`, `Plant_Grass_Fountain`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, `Iron_Wrought`, `Cloth_Laundry`, `Leaf_Litter` and the tree leaves), a texel is kept only where the diffuse alpha reaches the set's clip (0.5), through a Mix Shader with a Transparent BSDF. The alpha is read channel-packed, so the colour under a hole is never premultiplied. Cycles shades both faces, as the two-sided masked materials do;
  - the two-sided foliage sets (`Leaf_Creeper`, `Leaf_Cedar`, `Plant_Grass_Fountain`, `Plant_Pit_Flowers`, the tree leaves) also transmit light: a Translucent BSDF of base colour × 0.65 is added, the foliage parent's `SubsurfaceColor`.

- **Technique sources.**
  - The cut-out wiring follows the usual Cycles setup for clipped transparency: image alpha into the factor of a Mix Shader with a Transparent BSDF, clipped at 0.5 (Katsbits, *Cycles, Transparency & Blender 4.x+*, https://www.katsbits.com/codex/?p=8373). The image's *Channel Packed* alpha mode keeps the colour and alpha channels independent (Blender Manual, *Image Settings*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/editors/image/image_settings.html).
  - The per-axis clamp copies what Unreal's `TA_Clamp` does, the OpenGL clamp-to-edge rule. Coordinates are held between the centres of the edge texels, [1/(2N), 1 − 1/(2N)], so a linear filter never reaches past the edge (SGI, *SGIS_texture_edge_clamp*, https://www.opengl.org/registry/specs/SGIS/texture_edge_clamp.txt; Khronos forum, *Understanding of GL_CLAMP_TO_…*, https://community.khronos.org/t/understanding-of-gl-clamp-to/75971). Blender's image node clamps both axes at once with *Extend* (Blender Manual, *Image Texture Node*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/render/shader_nodes/textures/image.html), so a card clamps V with a Clamp node and keeps *Repeat* for U.
  - The foliage translucency stands in for Unreal's two-sided foliage model, where the subsurface colour sets how much light passes through (Epic, *Shading Models*, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/shading-models-in-unreal-engine).
  - The face-keeping rule is the per-primitive frustum test made exact for large faces. A corner-only test misses faces that span the view (Paul Bourke, *Frustum Culling*, https://paulbourke.net/miscellaneous/frustum/; Cesium, *Tighter Frustum Culling*, https://cesium.com/blog/2017/02/02/tighter-frustum-culling-and-why-you-may-want-to-disregard-it/). So each edge is clipped to the wedge's two half-planes the way Liang and Barsky clip a line parametrically (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang%E2%80%93Barsky_algorithm; Liang, Barsky and Slater, UC Berkeley 1992, https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1992/6271.html). A face that holds the camera is found with the crossings (even-odd) test (Haines, *Point in Polygon Strategies*, Graphics Gems IV, 1994, https://erich.realtimerendering.com/ptinpoly/).
  - The shadow rules and the camera-only litter use Cycles' per-object ray visibility. Turning off an object's *Shadow* rays stops it casting shadows, and the other ray types hide it from bounces while the camera still sees it. Instanced objects keep the visibility of the objects they instance, so a prop's or a car's parts carry its rule (Blender Manual, *Object Data: Visibility*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/render/cycles/object_settings/object_data.html; checked with a small scene in Blender 5.1). Why coplanar faces go dark: excluding the face a ray leaves "does not deal with false positives against adjacent (near) coplanar triangles" (NVIDIA, *Solving Self-Intersection Artifacts in DirectX Raytracing*, https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/solving-self-intersection-artifacts-in-directx-raytracing; Wächter and Binder, *A Fast and Robust Method for Avoiding Self-Intersection*, Ray Tracing Gems, 2019, https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2019-03_fast-and-robust-method-avoiding-self-intersection). Two patches at one height are exactly that case.
  - The parked cars.
    - *Instancing.* They use Blender's collection instancing: an empty instances a collection, and the geometry is stored once however many places use it. That lets Cycles render 2,025 cars from 14 collections (Blender Manual, *Collection Instancing*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/scene_layout/object/properties/instancing/collection.html; *Instances*, https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/dev/modeling/geometry_nodes/instances.html). This mirrors the game's instanced components per part mesh and paint.
    - *Cull distances.* Each part disappears at its cull end, as an instanced static mesh component's `InstanceEndCullDistance` hides its instances. The fade from `InstanceStartCullDistance` is left out (Epic, *UInstancedStaticMeshComponent*, https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/API/Runtime/Engine/UInstancedStaticMeshComponent).
    - *Art.* The car art is the game's own fleet (`Data/SeventiesCars`, an original procedural design), so no outside art source was adapted.
  - This set adapted no new technique. Its short search was for defect 1's fix, not for the preview: see that defect.
  - The macro variation, the leaf-card normals, the species trees, the fall tints, the litter texture and its outline breakup, the bike paint and the October props follow the apply step, the texture generator, the tree generator, the replica generator and the props generator, which name their own sources (`Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Textures/README.md`, `Trees/README.md`, `Props/README.md`, `openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/design.md`).

  No code was taken from any of them.

## Limits of these previews

- Shading is Cycles path tracing, not Lumen. There is no comic post-process, wet street, exposure adaptation or fog.
- Cycles shades every face from both sides. Unreal culls the back of one-sided materials, so a face wound the wrong way shows here but not in the game; `--probe` reports `seen_from_behind` for the faces it hits.
- Cycles samples the full-resolution card and leaf textures many times per pixel, which stands in for Unreal's alpha-coverage mips but is not the same filter.
- Only faces that reach each camera's region are loaded, so the far side of the park in `papineau.jpg` and the ends of long streets and lanes stop at bare ground or sky.
- The existing materials' colours are the `upgrade_plateau.py` palette or plausible stand-ins (the park lawn, `ParkGrass`, reads tan in places), not the Unreal materials themselves.
- Unreal's Nanite, LOD and cull distances are not reproduced for the layers and trees, and every tree is drawn at full detail. The parked cars' cull ends are reproduced, measured to each car's pivot, but not their 10 m fades. The shadow rules are reproduced, but not the shadow method (virtual shadow maps) or Lumen's screen-space contact shading.
- The litter is hidden from bounce rays, so it adds no warm bounce light to the walls round it. In the game it would add a little.
- The preview draws every parking space. The game leaves a space empty where something already stands at load, and it swaps a car for a real `AQuebecCar` when a player takes or hits it.
