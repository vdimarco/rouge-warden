# NORD: One More Tomorrow

Campaign story and mission treatment, 2026-10-10. **Eight-mission design; missions 1 and 2 now have a native prototype implementation.** Automated progression/save/reload, bounded rendered inspection, packaged startup and six static performance scenes pass; physical play and audio acceptance remain pending. Missions 3–8, the midpoint reveal and final restoration are planned content. The treatment below describes the intended complete campaign, including presentation not yet present in the prototype.

## The pitch

Your crew has organized a gloriously underfunded neighbourhood festival in Parc La Fontaine. The stage is borrowed, the cars are questionable, and everybody has inexplicably brought plumbing supplies. It is supposed to be one of those days you wish would never end.

The Boogeyman intends to grant that wish. He has quietly donated the souvenir booth, race projector, pond illumination, court equipment and theatre press as one sponsored festival programme.

Calling himself **the Curator**, he collects perfect days. His travelling souvenir booth prints beautiful comic postcards of the park. At sunset, the postcards begin moving, people's shadows stop following them, and thick black borders creep across the paths. His plan is to bind the whole neighbourhood into a single perfect comic-book page at midnight. Everyone inside its perimeter will remain trapped in the festival moment he declares perfect, unable to leave or make another one.

The crew begins by rescuing disrupted festival events. At the midpoint, they discover that their triumphant endings have supplied the very scenes the Curator needs. To save everyone, they must break his machinery, rescue the people inside the pictures and turn his immaculate finale into the least predictable show Montréal has ever seen.

The emotional thread is simple: a beloved place stays alive because people keep returning, changing it and making new memories. The crew's friendship should be felt through who helps whom during play. The ending earns another ordinary day in the park.

## Tone and antagonist

Joyful supernatural adventure with a little genuine unease. Strange shadows and empty streets create tension; recoverable captivity provides stakes. The jokes come from the crew's chemistry, the Curator's impossible standards and heroic uses for mundane equipment. Rescued residents get dignity, agency and useful roles in the comeback.

The Curator has a long ink-dark coat, white archivist gloves and a face that slips between smiling printed portraits. His attacks resemble page turns, crop marks and black registration lines. His mobile presses look like battered projection equipment strapped to ordinary cars. These are proposed original designs.

He is afraid of good things ending. He calls captivity preservation and treats people like exhibits. His understandable fear does not excuse his choices. The finale physically stops him before anyone offers compassion. He does not become harmless because someone delivers a speech.

His sales pitch: **“The best day of your life. Carefully protected from the rest of it.”**

## Rules the player can learn and exploit

1. **A Scene Press needs a physical frame and a captured ending.** Local traps can temporarily imprison people in loops replaying old postcards already in his collection. Recording a new charge is a separate action. Binding this living park requires five fresh charges: organized events, participating people and publicly acknowledged closing cues recorded by his donated equipment. An ordinary goodbye or merely saying “finished” has no magic power. The first four mission endings supply four scenes; the theatre ovation synchronizes them and supplies the fifth charge for the final binding.
2. **The frame is machinery as well as magic.** Angled worklights and car headlights expose wet ink seams and retaining clamps. A plunger attaches to an exposed clamp and physically opens it. Daylight and arbitrary bright light cannot defeat the magic. Early encounters release local fixtures and captive panels; the master binding stays inside an armored chassis until its final closing operation.
3. **He moves along connected ink borders.** The stolen projector, inverted in mission 7, casts a blank panel that interrupts a border and forces him to emerge at a readable location. Ordinary worklights reveal the route but cannot create this gap. The master press can repair local damage until its binding is broken, so stealing the projector alone does not save the park.
4. **Printed people are recoverable.** Breaking a local frame releases its captives, but the recorded scene remains in the central book. Recovering the people and destroying the master binding are separate goals. The final binding would make the captivity permanent.
5. **An unexpected action disrupts his recorded choreography briefly.** Each encounter defines a visible alternative to his repeating pattern: a different driving line, a redirected light or an improvised stage cue. Random jumping and arbitrary noise do not bypass the puzzle. An interruption exposes a clamp; the player must still act.

Midnight is a story deadline, not a timer counting down throughout free exploration. Only clearly announced encounter windows are timed. Hazard telegraphs use shapes, motion and captions as well as sound; timing puzzles do not require hearing or recognizing a song.

## Act I — Best day ever

### 1. A Table for Eternity — chalet and terrace

**Opening:** The crew's first festival meal is interrupted when the Curator's souvenir camera photographs the terrace. Chairs slide into unnervingly straight rows. Several festivalgoers become flat, smiling cutouts that repeat a toast without making a sound.

**Play:** Follow a displaced shadow to a worklight; redirect its beam to expose the camera's ink cable. Learn sprint/jump by crossing the low dressing obstacles, attach the plunger to the exposed retaining clamp, and pull during the camera's clearly telegraphed recovery. Finish by driving a light-equipped car into an indicated clear position so the last trapped table can be freed on foot. Author the parking bay outside the existing 4.5 m car-entry range of the clamp access, so E can perform the intended action without trying to re-enter the car. No passengers or building interior are required.

**The fun moment:** The “perfect” terrace keeps tidying itself while the crew turns the furniture into a ridiculous improvised light rig. The solution should feel like using the scene, not finding a key.

**Story/reward:** The terrace comes back to life and becomes the campaign hub. The rescued residents cheer; an apparently harmless souvenir of that cheer slips into a black delivery slot. The crew gains its portable worklight and plunger interaction. Checkpoint before the final camera cycle.

### 2. No Such Thing as a Quiet Lap — the street ring

**Setup:** A Curator promotional car announces that the streets will become a “permanent pedestrian still life.” Green Cap challenges it to one last festival lap while the others investigate its projector.

**Play:** Race an authored circuit on the existing surrounding roads. Ordered checkpoints lead through open sections for speed, tighter corners for braking and a final controlled handbrake turn. The projector paints a tempting “ideal racing line” that becomes an ink trap; spotting its repeating cue and taking the clearly marked alternative opens the rival's final light gate. This is the first mission that must sell the improved racing-style handling.

**The fun moment:** Cross the finish while the rival's grandiose victory banner folds around its own car. Co-op players drive separate cars; nobody needs a passenger seat or has to wait in a race lobby.

**Story/reward:** Black Cap spots a second souvenir being collected. The crew wins a roof-mounted worklight for subsequent missions and unlocks a replayable circuit. Clean driving earns a bonus, but collisions with ambient traffic do not make harming bystanders the optimal strategy. A missed checkpoint or reset resumes at a safe course checkpoint rather than restarting the campaign.

### 3. Still Life, Moving Target — pond paths

**Setup:** The festival's donated pond-illumination display switches on. The reflection shows a perfect sunny afternoon, even as the real park darkens. A picnic and its residents are trapped in that reflection. The Curator offers to keep every summer from ever ending.

**Play:** Stay on mapped shore paths. Watch the repeating reflection to identify which of several projector beams is false; move the worklight through two readable positions to break the loop, then sprint across the newly exposed safe path and pull the real press's clamp. The second beam changes the route, so this is a short spatial puzzle rather than repeated identical switches.

**The fun moment:** The false postcard pond peels upward like a sheet of paper, revealing the very irritated picnic underneath. Nobody needs to swim, drive into water or rely on new pond terrain.

**Story/reward:** The shoreline activity returns. White Cap insists on checking the released people before chasing the escaping courier. One resident describes hearing the same perfect toast over and over. The third captured ending disappears into the delivery system. Checkpoint after solving the reflection, before the exposed dash.

## Act II — The perfect trap

### 4. Match Pointless — sports courts

**Setup:** A festival exhibition is stuck one point from a flawless finish. The Curator's paper ushers keep returning every ball and every person to their prescribed spot. Red Jersey initially wants to beat the loop at its own game.

**Play:** Run, jump and redirect a slow, oversized festival ball through visible target panels while dodging telegraphed sweeping ink lines. The early pattern teaches timing; the final “winning” target simply restarts the loop. Redirect the ball into the projector's return gate instead, exposing its clamp and freeing the players. Use a simple authored ball interaction, not a full tennis simulation or precise racket controls.

**The fun moment:** The best play is an absurd bank shot that saves someone instead of scoring. The supposed referee tries to issue a penalty to the light itself.

**Story/reward:** The released court group brings its portable lighting to the evening show. The crew notices the fourth souvenir bearing the same collector's mark. No endlessly repeating rally is required; the restart is demonstrated once, then the alternative receives a clear visual hint. Checkpoint before the changed final pattern.

### 5. Standing Ovation — Théâtre de Verdure

**Setup:** With the terrace, streets, pond and courts rescued, the crew believes the festival can still happen. They bring their recovered equipment and people to the theatre for a comeback show.

**Play:** Operate the show: aim a followspot, deploy a deliberately ridiculous backdrop and trigger the final curtain cue. Players choose the order of the first two jobs, moving through the backstage space between them. Timing affects a celebration bonus, never basic story completion. The crowd answers every operated section, giving the player an audible and visible payoff. Establish the framed stage/audience boundary during rehearsal. The final cue is server-gated until all connected players have active pawns in the unframed lighting/loading position; show a clear regroup objective. Late joiners enter that safe position, including during the cue transition. Their backstage assignment, not unexplained immunity, keeps the playable crew free when the trap activates.

**The fun moment and reversal:** The applause becomes the sound of a turning page. The restored scenes appear as four giant comic panels around the stage; the ovation fills the fifth. The stage is the master book. The Curator needed the crew to make endings worth stealing.

**Story/reward:** People begin flattening into illustrations across the park. The page turn folds the captured audience into three captive panels at the terrace, pond and courts, visibly identifying every group on White Cap's missing-person list; other festivalgoers reach the safe hub. The early rescues were real, but the captured endings survived. Black Cap identifies the repeated marks; Skull Shirt owns the mistake without a joke, and White Cap changes the immediate goal: get everybody out. The master binding is still armored and cannot yet be reached. Play a short escape from the unframed loading edge through a visibly torn access panel. The equipment and allies earned earlier remain essential to the recovery. Checkpoint immediately before the final cue, and again after the escape; do not make players repeat the rehearsal to see the reveal again.

## Act III — Make room for tomorrow

### 6. Nobody Left in the Picture — three threatened gathering points

**Setup:** The crew could strike the theatre immediately, but residents are still inside the pictures. They choose the rescue first. The Curator calls that choice inefficient.

**Play:** Rescue three short groups at previously visited locations, in any order. At the terrace, use a parked worklight to expose the fixture holding a guest frozen mid-toast, then release it. At the pond, use the reflection to locate a reversed clamp before guiding residents through an exposed ink-free lane. At the courts, interrupt the return loop while moving ink sweeps threaten the approach. Escort sections are short, regroup automatically at safe spots and bank each rescued group. NPCs must route around props and recover safely if separated; fragile followers and long slow walks would spoil this mission.

**The fun moment:** The people the crew saved earlier now guide the next group out, bring lights or point out safe paths. The rescue becomes visibly collective rather than the player collecting helpless NPCs.

**Story/reward:** White Cap reads back the missing-person count as it reaches zero. Memphis drops the jokes for one line: “Okay. Now we get our park back.” The rescued crowd starts preparing the final countershow at a safe hub. Use recovery at the current group checkpoint; losing a life or disconnecting never erases groups already rescued. Mission ends with the crew's new plan, not another stolen staged celebration.

### 7. Can't Tow Tomorrow — surrounding streets

**Setup:** The Curator's convoy is drawing the final continuous border around the park. One wagon lays the border, one feeds it ink, and one carries the projector. That projector can cast an empty panel if the crew inverts its print plate. The master press still holds the five charged scenes and can repair the border; the convoy is the way to obtain an opening, not the final source of his power.

**Play:** Intercept an authored convoy on the mapped street ring. Read its repeating beam pulse, overtake the ink feeder into an indicated stopping bay, and pull its exposed clamp on foot. Interrupting the feed stalls the border-laying wagon and isolates the projector car. Capture that car, invert its print plate and drive the escape leg while blank panels open gaps ahead. This is one evolving convoy encounter, not three identical stop-and-loot chases. Solo play banks each disruption, with safe space to park and get out. In co-op, drivers and runners can work on different segments without simultaneous compulsory actions. If a wagon escapes a first attempt, it returns on a readable route and completed disruptions remain banked.

**The fun moment:** The convoy's enormous “DO NOT DISTURB” signs become the crew's improvised mobile stage decorations. The return drive crosses a restored street with residents cheering from clear safe areas. Use scripted mission routes and yielding rules; a random traffic jam cannot be the intended interception solution.

**Story/reward:** The crew owns a temporary, mobile interruption it must carry back to the theatre; the master press can still repair unattended breaks. Green Cap gives a route call, then accepts someone else's better line. Keep the captured wagon's cosmetic setup if the mission resets; checkpoint the escape separately from the interceptions.

### 8. Leave the Last Page Blank — theatre and its approach

**Setup:** The Curator tears up his perfect storyboard and tries to close the book by force. The crew turns the theatre into a deliberately unruly performance assembled from every earlier rescue. Residents stay behind safe boundaries; they are allies and an audience, not a new hostage meter.

**Play, three phases:**

1. **Draw him out.** Drive the projector car through three readable projection gates on the approach loop. Its blank panels cut the Curator's connected route and draw him back toward the stage. His page-turn attack has a clear warning and a safe alternative line. Damage/reset returns to the current phase checkpoint.
2. **Break his composition.** Park the projector in a marked safe position, holding a gap across the stage border, and continue on foot. Clamp access remains outside that car's 4.5 m entry range. The Curator starts the final closing operation to seal the interruption; only now does the armored chassis open. The theatre rearranges into a repeating sequence of ink sweeps and exposed stage paths. Redirect the worklight and pull two accessible retaining clamps while the crew's improvised cues disrupt the sequence. Each clamp stays removed after a retry. Co-op may split these jobs; solo receives sequential openings of sufficient length.
3. **Unbind the book.** With his route severed and both clamps removed, the Curator becomes physical at the master press. Use the plunger mechanic learned in the first mission to tear the binding free. The final tug is a readable action, not a surprise rapid-button challenge or a huge health bar. The five stolen scenes release into the park.

**The payoff:** The Curator offers the player one perfect day, forever. The crew chooses the people waiting outside his frame. The book breaks; the show ends voluntarily. Nobody has to perform forever to remain safe.

The next morning, the park is ordinary and alive. A chair is out of place. Somebody's car still needs washing. Two residents arrange another match. The eight crew get a quiet breakfast at the terrace; the final panel has room for whatever happens next. The captured book rests empty beside the Curator, who is offered one unoccupied seat. Whether he takes it is left open; his control over the park has already ended.

**Player reward:** Restored festival/free-roam state, replay access to the street circuit and court challenge, and the earned projector car decoration. Finish the story without requiring a perfect race time, a particular crew appearance or every optional activity. Checkpoint every boss phase; returning after campaign completion must preserve the restored world rather than restarting the threat.

## Pacing and quality bar

Target an initial campaign of roughly 80–100 minutes, to be revised through playtesting. Most missions should take 6–12 minutes, with a slightly longer finale. Free-roam between chapters has no failure clock. Keep introductions short and let most dialogue play during actions; subtitles and a repeatable current-objective summary carry essential information. A player can skip presentation without skipping required objective state.

Every mission needs a distinct central action, an authored surprise, a visible consequence and a local retry. A route marker may explain where to go; it cannot be the entire task. The first half's apparently happy endings foreshadow the reveal with the same collector mark and delivery animation. The second half repays those rescues through active allies and equipment rather than invalidating the player's work.

Main story progress requires neither collectible grinding nor performance medals. Optional clean-driving, clever-route and stage-timing bonuses are for replay. Protect the park and its residents; destructive driving is not the progression currency. Existing eight-character selection stays cosmetic and available throughout the campaign.

All missions must work solo and with every supported multiplayer party size. Vehicle legs use separate cars; passenger seating is not assumed. Completed subgoals remain banked, so a single player can park, get out and perform the runner's action. Party changes cannot leave a task requiring an absent player's car or avatar. The host owns campaign progression; late joiners receive the current phase, freed groups, removed clamps and restoration state. Save at mission boundaries, with recoverable encounter checkpoints for retries; define disconnect/load behavior before implementation. The first two native encounters pass automated shared-phase, in-session checkpoint and actual mission-boundary save/reload checks. Physical co-op and full packaged campaign/save flow remain unverified. The larger campaign’s freed-group, restoration and later-mission recovery behavior remains proposed.

Missions 7 and 8 have **one shared projector car**. Ordinary cars do not inherit its ability. Any player may claim its available driver's seat under the existing occupancy rules; other players can take separate cars to scout the route, operate already-accessible cues or handle on-foot jobs. None of those supporting jobs is mandatory simultaneously. A disconnected driver releases the seat, and a lost/unreachable projector recovers at a phase-safe checkpoint with its mission state intact and without spawning a second copy. Show the current carrier and recovery option clearly. Test these cases separately from ordinary car replication.

Review story presentation at both 1280×720 and 1920×1080: readable captions/objectives, visible hazard telegraphs through comic filtering, subtitles and quiet-mode visual cues. Playtest comprehension of the midpoint twist, handling enjoyment, mission variety, retry frustration and the final emotional payoff separately from automated correctness. The current six static performance scenes pass the unchanged frame-time requirement; worst-case driving/co-op performance remains unverified and future failures must remain visible.

## Build order and implementation boundary

The initial treatment was a documentation-only pass. The following records the subsequent native prototype; source implementation and accepted play are separate milestones.

**First playable slice: missions 1 and 2.** Prove the opening's mystery and worklight/plunger interaction, then prove a genuinely fun street circuit with the proposed driving improvements. Use a temporary Curator proxy and placeholder presses before investing in finished antagonist art or cinematics. Review these two missions in solo and co-op before committing to the remaining encounter content.

The default native map now spawns `AQuebecCampaign`. The opening validates mouse-aimed worklight alignment/hold, actual sprint and airborne cable crossing, green-beat clamp pulls, a stationary aligned car beam and an on-foot table release. The race validates ordered directional driving crossings, a blue side gate around an ink trap, a 180-second limit and safe checkpoint recovery. A projection rival provides a visible pace reference. All progression decisions run on authority. The original three proximity objectives remain only in `-NordNoCampaign` legacy mode. Car entry/exit retains priority and each car has one occupant.

The slice uses proxy presses, paper guests, a noncolliding projection car, short visible plunger pulls, signs and HUD crew/Curator banter. A temporary souvenir moves into a collector slot after the terrace rescue. It does not yet deliver the full voiced/cinematic dialogue, displaced-shadow opening, self-tidying terrace, complete souvenir-delivery presentation, permanent equipment rewards or polished antagonist performance described above. There is no facial animation or passenger seating.

Controls add E to acquire/release the worklight or pull a reachable clamp, mouse aim while operating the light, R for recovery and T for race replay after completion. C remains cosmetic and F2 remains private. Fresh progress is 0; the host writes completed mission count 1 or 2 to `NordCampaign_v1`; partial mission-1 load restarts the opening, partial race load returns to RaceReady after mission 1, and completed load retains both scenes. Encounter steps and race gates stay banked only within the session. T preserves saved completion 2. Unsupported schema/progress loads stay in session-only mode with a warning; save failure is surfaced rather than reported as success. `-NordNoCampaignSave` disables persistence; automated tests use `-NordCampaignQA` and the isolated `NordCampaign_StorySliceQA_v1` slot. See [StorySlice verification](../Verification/StorySlice.md).

Remaining implementation includes missions 3–8: pond reflection logic, court ball/sweep encounters, the theatre reveal, rescued-group routing, convoy events, unique projector ownership/recovery, final phases and replicated world restoration, along with complete narrative presentation. Current controls, possession, curb traversal, collision, character selection, private F2, shared progress and disconnect recovery remain regression requirements. Native action assertions and fixture-driven gates do not establish comprehension, physical driving enjoyment, naturally completed race pacing or multiplayer fun; those require manual review before extending the campaign.
