# Decisions

The simulation stays deterministic and browser independent. It records facts: a kill feed with streak and multi-kill counts, team pings, and skirmish timers on heroes. Presentation modules read these facts. `team-chat.js` writes bot chat, `announcer.js` maps events to sound and banners, and the renderer draws minimap markers.

`createMatch(kind, seed, lineup)` accepts a lineup of ally and enemy kits. Without a lineup the old picks stay, so seeded tests keep their matches. `assignIdentities` accepts an ordered list of identities so the draft names match the battle.

Draft picks are a pure function of the player's hero and a seed. Bots fill missing roles first (front line, mage, support, carry), avoid a kit already on their team, and never take an identity already picked. The board is skippable with Enter or a tap.

Assist: a healthy bot with no target in sight moves toward an allied hero who fought an enemy hero in the last three seconds, within 1900 units. A team rally point holds for 12 seconds and draws healthy bots within 3200 units. This uses the existing `combatDecision` order: danger and retreat still win.

Multi-kills use a 12 second window per killer. Streaks count kills without a death. Ending a streak of three or more is a shutdown. A tower alarm fires when an enemy damages a ward, at most once per ward per 14 seconds.

Sound uses Web Audio only: a master compressor, a generated reverb, noise and oscillator voices. Sounds from world positions pan by screen offset from the camera and fade with distance. The announcer voice uses the device speech engine when it has an English voice. Each call also has a musical stinger, so the cue works when speech is off or missing. The player can turn the voice off in settings. Recorded clips from free packs carry the main calls and impacts: TripleSnail's announcer kill calls (CC BY 3.0) and Kenney's fighter voice, impact, RPG and interface sounds (CC0). They are trimmed, mono, loudness-normalized MP3s, 528 KB in total, loaded on the first tap. Multi-kill and streak names follow the recorded lines. Credits are in `audio/CREDITS.md` and the help sheet. Each clip falls back to synthesis or device speech until it loads. No new dependencies are added.

No fal key is present in this environment, so the existing skill atlases are kept for every hero.

## Desktop camera, menu, sound and large screens

The player asked for the view to keep following the hero while the mouse pushes it left or right, said full screen lagged on an ultra-wide monitor, heard no sound, wanted Esc for the menu and a clearer menu button, and said the pause on mouse leave fought the camera.

- Camera: `Renderer.setLook(n)` takes the pointer's horizontal position from -1 to 1. Past a 12% dead zone the push grows with the square of the distance, to 34% of the screen width at the edge, and eases at a 0.2 s time constant. It adds to the follow target, so the camera never detaches. The last pointer position holds when the mouse leaves the window. Edge scrolling is gone; minimap look and Space stay.
- Pause: the mouseout and mouseenter handlers no longer pause or resume. Blur and hidden tabs still pause; focus or a click resumes.
- Esc: the keydown handler opened the menu dialog, and the browser then treated the same Esc as the dialog's close request, so the menu closed at once. `preventDefault()` on Esc fixes this. After full screen starts, `navigator.keyboard.lock(['Escape'])` sends Esc to the game in Chrome and Edge (a long press leaves full screen). In other browsers Esc leaves full screen; `fullscreenchange` then opens the menu, and closing it or the next click in the match asks for full screen again, since a key press cannot.
- Menu button: the faint 44 px pause circle becomes a pill with a menu icon, "Menu" and an Esc key cap, beside a speaker button. Phones hide the key cap and, under 520 px, the word.
- Sound: in local Chromium the match measured 0.06 to 0.11 RMS at the output after both a mouse start and a keyboard start, so no single cause of silence was found. The game now makes the state visible and recoverable: the speaker button shows and toggles the saved setting, a match that starts muted says so, and any click or key resumes a context that is not running (before, only the first click started audio).
- Large screens: a CPU profile at 1920x1080 showed the main thread 98% idle; frame time doubled from 1280x720 to 1920x1080, so the cost is pixel fill. `resize()` now limits the backing store to 2560x1440 pixels (3440x1440 draws at a pixel ratio of 0.86). `adapt(ms)` smooths the frame interval; when it stays over 1.5 times the refresh interval (and over 20 ms), quality drops by 20% to a floor of 35% of the budget. It rises 15% when frames return to the refresh rate, after a hold that doubles with each drop, up to 60 s.

## Second pass after "try again"

The player tested the merged first pass and said only "try again". A workflow ran five investigations (a static sound trace, live sound measurement, the frame loop, render cost, camera and input) and two skeptics per finding. 29 of 40 findings survived. Production served the merged build, so the player had tested the new code.

- Judder on high-refresh screens. The simulation steps at 60 Hz, but the renderer drew raw positions on every refresh while the camera eased every refresh. At 144 Hz most frames had no step, so the hero moved 0 or about 5 px per frame against a gliding camera. That reads as lag, and it is zero at 60 Hz, which is why earlier tests never saw it. main.js records each unit's and missile's position before `step()`. `drawBetweenSteps()` draws them at `prev + (pos - prev) * a`, with `a` the accumulator remainder times 60, shifts `state.time` back by `(1 - a) / 60` for the draw, and restores both in `finally`. A move of 80 units or more (recall, respawn, rift jump) is drawn at once. Measured at a simulated 144 Hz: hero jitter 1.43 px without, 0.089 px with; 0 at 60 Hz either way.
- `adapt()`. The first sample of a match came from a `last` re-seeded inside a click, so the refresh estimate could latch near 0 and the resolution never rose again. The re-seeds are gone (`frame()` sets `last` every rAF and clamps negative intervals). Each 1.5 s window is judged by its median. A slow window starts a descent of up to three 20% steps (frames land on whole refresh intervals, so one step can show no change). A descent that never speeds frames up is undone, and the next try waits 60 s, doubling after each failed descent. The floor is 0.5. Resize and a scene repaint restart the window.
- Creature sheets. The bank kept 8 sheets and evicted unseen ones, so returning creatures were fetched and decoded again on the main thread during play. It now keeps all 18 (2 MB) and decodes with `createImageBitmap`.
- Esc. Chrome tells the player to hold Esc to leave a keyboard-locked full screen, and each key repeat toggled the menu. Repeats are ignored in the game and in the arcade switcher.
- Full screen. Leaving full screen pulled the player back in on "Keep playing" or the next click. Now the menu offers "Back to full screen" and "Keep playing windowed", and a "Play windowed / Play full screen" row; each choice saves `tidebreak.fullscreen`. A `leavingFullscreen` flag keeps a chosen exit from reopening the menu.
- Resize. Every resize ran `resetInput()`, which cancelled the hero's order and cleared held keys, and key repeats were ignored, so a held W stayed dead after a full-screen toggle. The resize handler now ends only pointer gestures, and movement keys re-register on repeat.
- Minimap. One left click set a free camera that only Space or the button cleared, so the view stopped following the hero. The look now lasts while the minimap is held, lands at once, and release recenters.
- Camera tuning. The push reaches full strength at 90% of the way to the edge (`reach * (2 - reach)`), and the move-order lead plus the push share one cap. Holding Space sets the look to 0, so the view centres on the hero.
- Sound. In default Chrome every path into a match measured 0.06-0.25 RMS with a clean harness (state read through CDP with `userGesture: false`; Playwright's own evaluate calls give the page activation). The only in-page way to stay silent was the mute trap: controls labelled with their state, so a player who heard nothing and pressed "Sound on" muted the game and saved it. Controls now name the action. A `.muted-chip` shows on hero select and in the HUD while sound is saved off. The speaker ignores a click within 1.5 s of waking a stopped context. "Test sound" plays a chime, reads an AnalyserNode on the master bus and names the outside-the-game causes (tab mute, site setting, mixer, output device; ultra-wide monitors often take the audio over DisplayPort or HDMI). The engine records refused `play()` calls and retries them on the next input, starts and pauses every track in the first gesture (Safari's per-element rule), starts the draft music before `requestFullscreen()` takes the click, rebuilds the context after Chrome's `error` event (a lost output device), and plays the fallback score at an audible level.
- Diagnosis. `perf.js` shows median and p95 frame time, slow-frame share, draw time, steps per frame, canvas size, quality and the WebGL renderer string (with `?perf` or a menu row, copyable as JSON). When the renderer is SwiftShader, llvmpipe or a basic render driver, the HUD says the browser draws without the graphics card.

### Review fixes

An adversarial review of the second pass (five reviewers, a skeptic per finding) confirmed 20 findings, about 12 distinct defects:

- Quality steps did nothing on common screens. At 1920x1080 at 1x the budget term was already above the screen's own ratio, so quality only changed the canvas below 0.56: the first two steps were no-ops and a fill-bound GPU stayed at 30 fps. `backingRatio(width, height, deviceRatio, quality)` now scales the screen's ratio (limited by the budget) by `sqrt(quality)`, so the backing pixel count is in proportion to quality on every screen. The raise floor is 21 ms, so 50 Hz and 48 Hz screens recover too.
- `adapt.test.mjs` now drives the real `backingRatio`, with cost in proportion to backing pixels, and has cases for the floor, 144 Hz at every other refresh and 50 Hz. Three deliberate regressions (the old ratio, no raise floor, a .35 floor) each fail it.
- Test sound wrote a long result into the paged menu after the pager had measured it, which hid the rows below. It now opens its own panel after the measurement, with Test again and Back.
- The HUD "Sound is off" chip covered the score on screens up to about 760 px wide; it is hidden there, where the crossed-out speaker shows the mute. The hero-select chip uses its short form ("Sound off") up to 999 px, so the settings cog stays on screen on landscape phones.
- In Chrome the hold-Esc exit happens after the first press has opened the menu, so the windowed choice never showed. The full-screen exit handler now redraws an open pause menu with the choice.
- "Play full screen" is hidden where element full screen does not exist (iPhone Safari).
- `lost()` kept timestamps from the closed context's clock, which silenced throttled sounds and held the battle music; it clears them.
- The performance readout counted menu, draft and paused frames and never reset its steps figure. It samples only while a match steps, keeps steps in the same 600-frame window, resets at match start, sits under the minimap (top centre on short screens), and hides the graphics-card note while it shows.
- Two browser checks could not fail: the 60 Hz smoothness window measured a hero pinned against an obstacle, and the full-screen check left the menu only through a path that saved "windowed". Each motion window now starts the hero at the same open spot and asserts it walked; 75 Hz is added. The full-screen section also closes the choice with Esc and checks that clicks stay windowed, and checks the hold-Esc order.
