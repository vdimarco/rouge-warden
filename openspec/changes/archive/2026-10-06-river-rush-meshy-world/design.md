# Design

Use Three.js with a fixed chase camera and distance-relative floating origin. Meshy 7.1 via the connected fal account generates three PBR assets: timber raft, tropical bank outcrop and a tall palm. Optimize textures and share cloned geometry/materials across bank scenery. Keep the approved photographic paddle rider as an animated cutout on the real raft to preserve the face; do not claim a new rigged character.

CPU buoyancy and the GPU water vertex shader use the same directional waves, world distance and simulation time. Four surface probes produce heave, pitch and roll; damped springs, bounded steering lean and landing impulses provide contact without changing logical lanes or collision windows. Normal-based lighting, moving crest foam, raft wakes and ripples around hazards provide water depth cues. No gameplay videos are used in the 3D renderer. Software WebGL uses derived low-detail meshes, cheaper water shading and a smaller backing buffer. Hardware uses the detailed shader and models. Transient image/model HTTP failures receive two bounded retries; persistent required-art failures expose a working retry button. Shadows remain local to the raft and nearby hazards; distant scenery shares resources and remains bounded.

Reduced motion disables wave displacement, cosmetic bobbing, particles and decorative movement while preserving visible controls and obstacle motion. Pause and page hide stop simulation and frame rendering. Missing generated models retain primitive substitutes; missing WebGL uses the existing playable 2D renderer. GPU loss pauses the run and offers a restart in fallback rather than leaving an active invisible run.

Verify on phone, desktop and short landscape using real keyboard/touch input, screenshots and renderer status. Test buoyancy coherence, frame-rate stability, bounded transforms and landing recovery with pure math tests. Report headless software rendering limits separately from physical-device performance.
