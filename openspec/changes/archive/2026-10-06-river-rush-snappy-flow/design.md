## Direction
Target direct, short input response and continuous coherent forward motion. Base speed rises from 30 to 50 m/s, jump .66 s, duck .60 s, visual lanes reach 95% within about 80 ms. Existing seed-based distance rows use speed-aware spacing; test 40 delayed-input courses and fast action chains. Authoritative lane changes stay immediate and no animation locks input. Swipe consumption resets the segment origin so a long drag can cross two lanes, with a 26-pixel initial threshold and deliberate 56-pixel additional segments.

Use registered raft centers/bottoms for the eight existing frames and normalize hero poses to one raft width. Precompute six alpha-correct in-betweens per paddle frame at loading (48 immutable cropped frames, about 33 MB RGBA), then sample the cached cycle without hot-loop blending and switch action silhouettes immediately, avoiding full-body jump/duck ghosting. Keep short landing recoil; shorten reward flights and bursts. Draw projected foam toward the camera at actual distance speed, strengthen near-water streaks and lane-swap wakes while leaving hazards readable.

Copy fal.ai video only on a decoded frame change (requestVideoFrameCallback, currentTime fallback), at source-size bounded resolution. Render its cache once; no redundant still beneath an opaque video. Scale playback by run speed. Use still plus foam while loading; defer costly GPU initialization until a video failure or sustained delay. Preserve freeze through late decode, early pause/resume, live preferences and video/GPU loss. Cap canvas backing buffers at 1.2 million pixels for narrow views and .9 million for wide views, rather than unconditional DPR2; skip repeated rendering and snapshot updates while paused/lost.

Report browser observations, not physical-device guarantees.
