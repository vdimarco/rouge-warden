## Decisions
Remove the raised spring-arm target. Clamp the camera component orbit itself to -25..-5 degrees. Normal input shares those bounds so reversing a gesture responds immediately; the worklight permits its wider aim rotation while the camera remains framed. Use the existing spring arm and controller ticks.

Mobile priority means an 800×450 landscape frame or portrait frame no taller than 800 pixels, with a 1.5 Mbps bandwidth ceiling. Five receiver samples below 24 FPS or above 120 ms buffer delay, or an observed freeze, lower the long edge to 640 pixels and the ceiling to 1 Mbps until reconnect. Drag gain is normalized to the original 1280-pixel long edge. Native startup bitrate is 2 Mbps; desktop can ramp to its original 12 Mbps ceiling. FPS falls back to decoded-frame counters when a browser omits its optional FPS field. Keep native graphics unchanged. Compare a forced relay camera sweep with baseline, plus direct playback. Codec/capture changes require advancing real video and measured improvement before shipping. Adapted source: [Epic UE5.8 Stream Tuning Guide](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/stream-tuning-guide) and installed PixelStreaming2 settings/frontend sources; hardware H264 can avoid VP8 software latency, but its prior fence stalls require verification.

Camera acceptance includes head/feet projection at both bounds in wide and tall layouts, actual rendered screenshots and worklight completion. Native unit checks alone do not establish phone/carrier performance.
