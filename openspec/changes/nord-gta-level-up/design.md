# Design

## Constraints
- The cloud container has no Unreal Engine and no GPU. Every native claim stays open until the host builds and runs it.
- The host compiles with clang `-Wall -Werror -Wshadow`. PR #404 broke the Linux build with one local that shadowed a member. No local, parameter or lambda parameter may reuse a member name, own or inherited.
- Tests call `ServerSetDrive`, `MoveForward`, `MoveRight` and `ServerInteract` through reflection with hand-built parameter structs. Their signatures stay unchanged.
- Pinned checks stay true: exactly 14 + 96 residents, 16 traffic cars, one campaign actor, three feedback sounds, four music log lines, save version 1 with 0–2 missions, camera pitch −25…−5, Toronto with no crowd and one car, empty captions after a phase change.
- The cloud cannot write `.uasset` or `.umap` files. Every gameplay feature works with engine basic shapes, existing assets and procedural sound. New art arrives through editor scripts that the host runs, and the code uses it when `LoadObject` finds it.

## Shared contracts
`Source/Quebec/QuebecGTA.h` holds the shared enums and the free-function namespaces: `QuebecPeople`, `QuebecCombat`, `QuebecCrime`, `QuebecJobs` and `QuebecNotice`. Each namespace has one source file. Systems call each other only through that header, so the files can grow in parallel.

`AQuebecPlayerState` (new, `QuebecPlayerState.h/.cpp`) carries health, armour, down state, weapons, ammo, aim lock, heat, stars, search state and cash. A driver's character stops replicating while the car carries it, so this state cannot live on the character. It updates at 10 Hz, and the server forces an update on each change. `AQuebecGameMode` sets `PlayerStateClass`.

Pure rules live in engine-free headers in the `QuebecSkyModel.h` style, so the container builds and runs them with g++ and clang++ under `-Wshadow -Werror`: `QuebecKnockModel.h` (tumble pose over time), `QuebecHeatModel.h` (heat, stars, search time), `QuebecAimModel.h` (target choice in a cone with hysteresis) and `QuebecSteerAssist.h` (lag-tolerant steering).

## People and knockdowns
Residents and pedestrians have no collision, and they must not get any: placement checks, traffic sweeps and `CanFitAt` would then treat them as walls. Car strikes use a server-side test instead. `AQuebecCar` keeps its transform before the move and calls `QuebecPeople::StrikeWithCar` after the move, for player-driven, police and shoved cars. The test checks each person's capsule against the car's swept box.

The crew and civilian skeletons have no physics assets, so a ragdoll is not possible. A knockdown is a procedural comic tumble: a ballistic arc from a replicated start time, launch velocity and spin, then a lie-down, a dazed hold with circling stars, and a stand-up. Every peer computes the same pose from server time, so replication costs a few floats per knockdown. Residents hold their route clock while down, as `Acknowledge` already does. A player hit by a car is launched on the server, loses health and cannot move until standing.

People flee from gunfire and fast cars. `QuebecPeople::Alarm` sends each civilian away from the source along a clear direction for a few seconds, then they return to their route. At most 32 residents flee at once.

A fast car must still hit a person it is aimed at. So the warning scan uses one rule, `QuebecKnock::CarReaction`. A person within 145 cm of the car's centre line (half the car, half the person and a 20 cm margin) freezes and faces the car. A person between 145 and 260 cm dives away. Below 450 cm/s a car never knocks anyone down: civilians in its swept box take a 0.6 s sidestep, and no crime is reported. A knocked player ignores the car that hit them for 1.6 s on the server and on their own client, so the tumble does not snap back.

## Combat
Weapons: fists, hockey stick, pistol, SMG and shotgun. Pickups are actors at fixed points and respawn after 60 s. Firing runs on the server. The client sends its aim; the server picks a target with `QuebecAimModel` (25° cone, 35° hysteresis, lock kept for 4 s) and checks line of sight against the world. People have no collision, so hits on people are cone tests plus one visibility trace. Tracers, muzzle flashes and impact words are multicast. Sounds are procedural `USoundWaveProcedural` bursts, like the car engine. Melee snaps the player's yaw up to 90° toward the target and hits during a short window.

A driver can be hurt. `QuebecCombat::DamagePlayer` gives a driver 50% of a hit and the car 60% as body damage. Shots at a goon, or at police who already chase the shooter, add no gunfire heat, and only civilians count as witnesses. The client sends `ServerFire` once per press and `ServerStopFire` on release, and the server keeps an automatic weapon firing in between. Getting into a car releases Fire and Aim. Effects come from a pool of at most 32 per world, the shot sounds are made once and replayed, and beyond 120 m only the sound plays.

At zero health the player is knocked out (K.-O.). The pawn is never destroyed: it lies down, the banner shows for 4 s, and the player returns at the Hôpital Notre-Dame stop on Sherbrooke with full health. Cash stays; weapons and stars clear.

## Police
Crimes add heat when a police officer or car sees them (foot 55°/40 m, car 35°/70 m) or when two civilians are within 25 m. Thresholds for 1–5 stars: 10, 45, 100, 180, 300 heat. While any officer sees the player, a search circle follows the player. When none sees the player, the stars blink, and they drop after 15 s + 10 s per star.

The dispatcher keeps police cars and officers up to a cap for the star level: 1★ one car; 2★ two cars; 3★ three cars and officers who shoot; 4★ and 5★ four cars and four officers in total. Two co-op viewers already share one GPU, so the cap stays low. Cars appear 120–200 m away on the road graph and out of every player's view. They pursue along road segments when far, and directly with a 1.2 s prediction when near. A police car is an `AQuebecCar` with `bPolice`: white with a blue band, a light bar and a procedural siren. It drives through an autopilot input on the player handling model, and the remote-input failsafe does not apply to it. Officers are a light character class that steers with `AddMovementInput` and needs no navmesh. Officers hurt a driving player through `DamagePlayer`. The dispatcher checks which roads are open in small steps, four sweeps per server tick, starting at `BeginPlay`, so the first star causes no hitch. The server gets no camera updates from a remote player who drives, so for that player it tests spawn visibility from a chase camera 7 m behind the car, with an 80° cone.

An officer next to a slow player on foot holds for 2 s and arrests the player (ARRÊTÉ). The player returns at Poste de quartier 38 on Rachel. A garage on the map clears the stars when no officer sees the player enter.

## Side jobs
`AQuebecSideJobs` is a separate actor from the campaign. Its save data is separate too, so the campaign save version and story checks do not change. One job runs at a time, shared by both players. A player starts it with E at a yellow marker. Every marker is at least 15 m from each QA anchor. Jobs: a timed delivery, a car-thief takedown, ink goons at the Théâtre de Verdure, and a three-star escape. R inside a job runs the job's own retry, not the campaign retry. After MISSION RATÉE, R restarts the failed job from its marker for 30 s; then R is the campaign recovery again. A player who is down or being arrested cannot use R. The escape job wins only when the stars clear, by losing the police or through the garage rule above. Ramming the thief car is not a crime. When no job runs, the radar shows job markers only within 100 m and pickups within 60 m, so the calm HUD captures keep their look. Race gate 0 starts the race only when no job is active. Side jobs are off in Toronto and under `-NordNoCampaign`.

## Living street
The 96 runtime residents stay 96. Some walkers get a companion: a leashed dog, a stroller or grocery bags, set by a replicated byte. The dog and the stroller are components of the resident, built from basic shapes or from the imported Blender meshes when the host has imported them. The dog trots with procedural leg swing and stops to sniff at trees. Traffic cars come to a full stop at junctions marked with ARRÊT signs. They wait for people on the crossing, and now and then they pull to the curb for a while with hazard lights on. The first curb stop comes 2–5 minutes into a session, after the population test's motion window. Each traffic car looks for people within 20 m about 7 times a second and checks their positions on every traffic update. Street props register in a per-world list, so a moving car does not scan every actor.

## Lag-tolerant driving
The phone wheel sends graded steering. New axis keys give ±0.33 and ±0.66 steering in `DefaultInput.ini`, so the browser can send three levels per side with no new transport. The wheel uses hysteresis, so a jittery thumb does not flicker between levels. On the server, `QuebecSteerAssist` applies a speed-based steering rate, a yaw damper and road-heading straightening near the centre of the wheel. It is active only on roads, so the driving fixtures at (0,0), 137 m from any road, keep their pinned numbers. Off the road, every input, keyboard or analog, turns in at exactly the old rates. On a road, easing the wheel to a smaller level on the same side releases fast, so a late correction does not hold the old lock. Wall contact keeps sliding speed instead of stopping the car. Possessed cars update at 60 Hz.

## Plateau streetscape
`Scripts/plateau_replica.py` is a stdlib-only generator in the existing prepare/apply pattern. It writes OBJ batches and a manifest to `Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/` (git-ignored, deterministic). `Scripts/apply_plateau_replica.py` imports them on the host as an additive layer. Repeated props go into `AQuebecReplicaInstances` actors, one per prop part, colour variant and collision class; each has a static hierarchical instanced mesh as its root, in the `AQuebecParkInstances` style. Props that block cars get simple box collision from the generator's collider boxes. `/Game/Plateau/Replica/Props` is always cooked, because the companion code loads those meshes by path.
- Streets are cut to measured cross-sections (Rachel about 14 m curb to curb, Papineau 17.1 m, Sherbrooke 21.0 m, residential streets 10–12.5 m: Mentana 11.0, Fabre 12.3, Garnier 12.5). The layer uses dark asphalt, yellow centre lines, white stop bars, crosswalks of the Québec kind and green bike-lane paint with bollards on Rachel.
- Sidewalks are grey concrete with an 18 cm curb. The `SM_Concrete_Collision*` naming and the 17–25 cm curb top keep runtime curb parking working. The layer adds front parterres and tree pits, since the fourth pass where the city's inventory puts sidewalk trees (it first used the 8.4 m street-tree spacing).
- The 1,409 plain buildings get plex fronts with a cheaper street-level budget. Storeys and type come from tagged levels and the municipal heights; the lot module is 7.63 m. Fronts get brick in local colours, stone sills and lintels, painted cornices, balconies at each upper floor, straight or curved black iron stairs and paired doors.
- Corners get white street-name signs with black text (Montréal's real design, not blue), ARRÊT signs at the 77 mapped stops, lamps at the 288 mapped points and red hydrants at the 200 mapped points. BIXI docks go at mapped stations.
- The lived-in layer comes from the owner's reference article ("City building games have a Soul Problem pt.2", Radical Elements). It adds patched asphalt and potholes, leaves in gutters, weeds, soft grass edges, cones, a construction fence, laundry lines, utility poles and wires over ruelles, bins, locked bikes, rust streaks and murals, varied by a per-lot seed.

- The back of each block follows the same split grammar by face type: street, lane, side and party faces, and no openings where a neighbour abuts. Plex lots on yards get rear walls with doors and windows, galleries on posts with balusters or boards (1,070 mm guards), straight or steel spiral stairs, lane fences on posts with gates, sheds, garages, ground cover and creepers. Retained masses outside the plex layer get a proud skin with windows wherever OSM glass cards would float. Backyard trees come from the city's Canopée 2019 data, clipped to the district and committed with attribution.
- Roofs follow the borough's white-roof rule: mostly white membrane or white gravel, some tar and gravel, metal copings on parapets, a chimney and a hatch per lot. Only mansards stay slate.
- Laundry lines run from gallery pulleys to a pole in the lot's own yard, because the city forbids lines on utility poles. The 14 ruelles vertes in the district come from the city's open data and get planted beds, entry arches and murals.
- Textures are made by a stdlib generator (`plateau_textures.py`) with DirectX normal maps and packed roughness, height and cavity. Cut-out cards (balusters, lattice, fence boards, creeper leaves) use built masked two-sided parents with alpha-coverage mips. The Plex budget is 3.2 million triangles in at most 1,350 batches, the caps in `plateau_plex.py` (`TRIANGLE_BUDGET`, `MAX_BATCHES`), under the agreed ceiling of 3.5 million triangles and 1,400 batches; the fourth pass raised the batch cap from 1,200 because its eight new keys take 1,341 batches at the 190 m tile, and `test_plateau_plex.py` checks that the spec's numbers are at least the code's caps. Each batch is one Nanite static-mesh actor, and the host profile decides whether that holds.
- Third pass, gameplay first. No collision stands within 300 cm of a traffic centreline: a stair, porch or balcony that reaches into that band loses its collision as a whole, and its deck faces move to visual-only batches. Gallery guards, the open sides of upper flights and the rails of stair-head balconies get invisible walls (`Collision_Only`): the apply step puts them on actors hidden in game that block pawns and ignore the camera. A 42 cm capsule walks every gallery deck in the test, so no guard traps the player at a flight head.
- Fourth pass, street trees: every street and park tree comes from the Ville de Montréal's public tree inventory (CC BY 4.0, clipped into `Data/Plateau/Inventory/`), with its species and trunk, plus the few OSM trees it lacks; `Streets/street_trees.json` lists them one row each for the apply step. The curb stays where it was measured, so a tree the inventory puts in a sidewalk pit moves to one 65–80 cm behind the curb, slid clear of ramps, furniture and the next pit; each pit gets a low wrought-iron guard and residents' planting as cut-out cards. Curb extensions remain only within 15 m of a corner or where the data lists one (a saillie or median in the inventory, an OSM choker); other trunks the carriageway covers are re-seated or left out (fifth pass, below). Extensions merge only where no parked car fits between them. The ruelle gutter is aged lane concrete sunk between the asphalt halves on its own UVs, with leaves drifted along it. Sources: the inventory and research-plateau.md §10.4 (Mentana's 120 trees all stand in sidewalk pits, with no extension); for the new texture sets, Kai Li's "Old Stone Paver" Substance breakdown (height first, dirt in the low lines, colour, then roughness) for the lane concrete, Pelzer's "Rendering Countless Blades of Waving Grass" (GPU Gems ch. 7), klamtii's tree sprites and Kevin Quinn's God of War Ragnarök plant cards for the crossed cedar, fountain-grass and pit-planting cards, and Weil's "The Synthesis of Cloth Objects" (SIGGRAPH 1986) for folds that fan down from the laundry pegs. Ruelles are paved fence to fence. On the capture streets the ground from curb to building line is covered: parterres reach the façade, side passages between plexes get concrete walks, and every raised slab edge over lower ground gets a riser, the skirt terrain renderers use to hide cracks, so no gap shows the floor under a sidewalk.
- Fourth pass, lane life: each strand of a pulley clothesline hangs as a true catenary set by its mid-span sag (its parameter by bisection, its vertex and length from the relations in Wikipedia's "Catenary", Determining parameters), not the parabola the old code and the Maya catenary generator 80.lv describes use. Garments come from the `Cloth_Laundry` atlas, spaced along the rope by their own widths as J. Fuchs's procedural clothesline HDA for Unreal spaces its items (her rope is a Vellum simulation; a closed form keeps the build stdlib-only and deterministic). Each garment is a strip of quads whose top follows the drawn rope and whose hem folds in front of and behind the rope's plane, the pinned-cloth folds of Weil (SIGGRAPH 1986). The ruelle preview camera moved 32 m along the Mentana and Saint-André lane to a stretch where a line with washing stands 13 m ahead, because the lots near the old spot had no room for a line; the test proves the line is in sight over the fences. Ornamental grasses are three crossed `Plant_Grass_Fountain` cards (Pelzer's crossed-quad clumps; Kevin Quinn's plant cards). Tags and fence murals go only on solid fence faces (boards, the boards under a lattice band, slat panels), never on pickets or chain-link.
- Fourth pass, species trees: the apply step (`plan_trees`) puts every `street_trees.json` row on the Trees mesh for its kind, one NoCollision instanced actor per kind and mesh (13). A species table in `plateau_replica.py` picks the kind; elm and hackberry take the new vase `elm`: a trunk that forks at about 3.8 m into five leaders that keep their lean, limbs long at the top that arch out, drooping twigs and yellow leaf cells. Every kind but small has a young tree, a single leader about 5 m tall with its crown above 2.1 m, used for trees under 55% of the kind's mature height (1,520), so a new planting is not a shrunken mature tree with branches at knee height. Height scales from the row (0.55–1.65) and crown width from the trunk diameter. The old summer tree's instances are cleared from `QuebecVegetation` (its label and transform stay), the seven route detail trees are hidden in game, and `plateau_rendering.tree_positions` reads `street_trees.json`, so the Plex yard trees keep clear of the new positions. `refine_plateau_trees.py` defaults to autumn leaves. The apply step imports and checks the tree meshes before any layer mesh, so a bad slot name stops it before anything is saved, and both host blocks build the git-ignored tree OBJs first. Sources: the vase after the Lady Bird Johnson Wildflower Center's and the Minnesota DNR's descriptions of Ulmus americana and Flora of North America (SEINet), built with Weber and Penn's 1995 parameters; no outside technique fit the young trees, which follow the inventory's trunk sizes and research-plateau.md §10.4. Clearing the vegetation also stops `QuebecVegetation`'s falling leaves, which spawn near its own instances; bringing them back is a C++ change left for the host build.
- Fourth pass, Plex: the Habitations De Mentana (OSM way 356220851, `BUILDING_FORMS`) follows the photo in OMHM's April 2009 Infochantier: grey concrete piers every 6 m with red brick between them, one-bay balconies in 16 vertical stacks (128) with teal open railings, plain grey concrete end walls and end-block sides with panel joints and no balconies, and a ground floor behind 18 concrete columns, each with its own collision box. The block's mass cannot be cut, so the upper floors stand 1.2 m out over the ground floor; the 6 m bays, the overhang and the 1 m railings are estimates from that one photo. Lane fences are seeded lot by lot (537: low pickets, weathered boards, chain-link, boards painted green, red or blue-grey, slat panels and lattice-topped boards, no look over a third, still without collision), with 28 garages and 156 sheds facing the lane. The 93 cedars are three crossed `Leaf_Cedar` cards each and the 74 hedges are rows of cards over a dark core, with no cone left. A siding wall that a named street sees along a clear line is brick (127 walls, the Rachel wall among them): the occlusion test of Müller et al. (2006) as a ray cast. Sources: crossed cut-out cards after klamtii's tree sprites, Kevin Quinn's God of War Ragnarök plant cards and Dev Haz's "Cardboard Nature: Volume 2 (Cutouts)" (Epic forum listing, flat and crossed cutouts); chain-link as one flat masked plane after World of Level Design's "Making of Chain Link Fence" and Epic's Opacity Mask content example. There is no chain-link texture set yet, so a small-scale grey lattice stands in.
- Fourth pass, regeneration order: the tree meshes (`plateau_trees.py --no-render`), then `plateau_plex.py`, then `plateau_replica.py` (Streets, Furniture, Soul), then `plateau_textures.py --check`. The layers read each other both ways: the Plex yard trees keep clear of `street_trees.json`, which `plateau_replica.py` writes, and the Streets ruelle paving and the Soul lane life read the Plex yard records and Glass batches. Each manifest hashes what it reads, the Plex one `street_trees.json` too, so a stale layer shows; after a change to either side, the two generators run in turn until neither output moves. The integration run of this pass rebuilt every layer in that order and nothing moved, and a second build of each is byte-identical.
- Fifth pass, streets. No trunk stands on carriageway or parking asphalt outside a curb extension. A tree the inventory maps on the carriageway is re-seated from its own record: the side of the street (`Rue_cote`, a compass letter on Montréal's grid, read as the curb whose outward normal leans to it), the place (`Emplacement`) and the distance from the pavement (`Distance_pave`). A sidewalk tree goes to a pit 65–80 cm behind the curb of its side, sliding up to 12 m; a parterre tree to the lawn that distance behind the curb of its side; a median tree onto its street's mapped median (two parallel carriageways of the same name with no sidewalk between); any other is left out and listed for the host (25: Rue De Champlain's and part of Avenue du Parc-La Fontaine's medians, which OSM does not map, and four Roy Est trees whose side has no room for a pit). Conifers and larches take their own Trees kinds, and each row carries its late-October colour (`fall`), so the apply step and the preview tint each kind and colour group without per-instance data. The ruelle gutter is 0.5 cm deep: the old OSM road (`Road.obj`, z 6.0) stays in the map under the ruelles, and a 1.5 cm V dipped under it, so it showed through as two pale lines; the vertices now keep 3 mm over it, a small real gap as decals keep over a coplanar surface, and the texture makes the gutter dark. Sealed cracks and potholes are as rough as the asphalt (0.88): at 0.62 the sky still drew the crack as a white hook. Behind the street-front yards a ruelle is paved out to the first Plex lot line, fence, shed or wall within 15 m, so the open ground beside the Habitations De Mentana is no longer bare floor; within 15 m of a sidewalk the lane keeps its fence-line edge, which the parterres meet. Sources: the city inventory's field descriptions (donnees.montreal.ca, "Arbres"); for fall colour the Morton Arboretum's tree pages (northern red oak russet-red to bright red, white oak wine-red), University of Illinois Extension on oaks in fall (pin oak red) and the Jardin botanique de Montréal (Espace pour la vie) on trees for fall colour; for the gap over a coplanar surface Valve's `$decal` page and Microsoft's Direct3D decaling page. No outside technique fit the re-seating or the lot-line paving, which are clearance rules on the city and OSM data.
- Fifth pass, apply step. The street trees stand on one actor per kind, mesh and late-October colour. A tree of its kind's own colour (yellow broadleaf, evergreen conifer, gold larch) wears the kind's leaves. The red maples and the russet oaks get their own actors on the same meshes, and each actor's leaves slot takes a duplicate of the atlas material with its own tint: the tint brings that kind's atlas cells, weighted by cards and cover, to the fall's mean colour. That is 7 more actors and 4 materials, with no new mesh or triangle. It uses no per-instance data, which needs a VertexInterpolator, and that turns masked Nanite opaque in UE 5.8. Every street-tree actor carries the `NordStreetTree` tag, which the dogs' sniff search reads. The plan stops if a trunk stands within 300 cm of a traffic centreline outside a pit or a ruelle's edge (every centreline that near a 380 cm service lane), or within 200 cm of any, where a car's side passes; the pass-4 data failed it on the three trunks the tech critic named. The fallen-leaf key `Leaf_Litter` is a contract key: masked, tiling on metre UVs, its own colours, no shadow. Source for the tint: the hue variation of SpeedTree trees in Unity (`SpeedTreeImporter.hueVariation`), which shifts each tree's colour toward one colour by an amount; here the shift is set per kind and colour group from the atlas statistics, instead of by world position.
- Fifth pass, lane life. No laundry hangs in a tree. The Soul build reads the crowns the game draws: the Plex yard trees and the street and park trees on their species meshes, scaled as the apply step scales them (its `street_tree_scale`, mirrored in `plateau_replica.py` and checked row by row against it). A crown is a cylinder round the trunk, half the mesh's crown width across, from its crown base up. A pole spot is rejected when a line's two strands or the washing it can carry (the longest garment drop under the most sag) would enter the inner 0.7 of a crown above its base or pass within 1.5 m of a trunk. The module then tries its other fence and yard spots; failing those, the pole serves only the floors whose lines are clear, or the yard hangs no washing. The local review found 180 of the 295 lines with washing strung through crowns. Now 187 lines remain (114 with washing, 586 garments), and 239 gallery modules under trees hang none. The lots near the old ruelle camera are all under 15–18 m ashes and maples, so the camera moves 40 m further along the same lane (`street_view(lane, 43500, ...)`), where lot 356252971's line with 14 garments stands 12 m ahead. The flat one-colour leaf polygons are gone. Fallen leaves are ragged patches of the `Leaf_Litter` set: under every street and park crown, on curb drifts and in the ruelle gutters. Each patch is a fan whose outline wanders and is cut back, ray by ray and then triangle by triangle, to the flat ground it starts on, so no patch hangs over a curb, a ramp, a dropped curb or a park path. The leaves cover min(0.9, 1.6 × (1 − leaf retention)) of a crown's ground, from the Trees manifest. A street tree's leaves also fall in two small strays toward its drip line, on any flat ground there, so the litter thins out instead of ending at one edge. Curb drifts come more often and wider under crowns, and only against a drawn curb on the replica asphalt. Every patch carries world metre UVs, so overlapping patches show the same leaves with no seam. The litter is about 67,000 triangles of the Soul layer's 300,000. Sources: Adam Sharp's fallen-leaves material (80.lv, 2022) for the texture set the patches use, and Epic's `WorldAlignedTexture` material function ("Texturing Material Functions") for tiling one texture in world space across separate meshes, baked here into the UVs. The crown clearance is a rule on the data; no outside technique was needed.

Since the fourth pass the Mentana block takes its own slab form on top of the kit (above). The eight background towers are buff or brown brick with balconies. Buff brick, ballast, tar and yard gravel are new 2 m texture tiles, and a macro mask sampled at 17.3 m and 5.3 m breaks up their repeat.

## Known issue left for a follow-up: the world is mirrored
The pipeline puts +X east and +Y north into Unreal's left-handed frame. The map is therefore a mirror image of the real Plateau: facing north on Papineau, the park is on the player's right instead of the left. The radar draws real north-up geography, so it turns against the view. A fix negates Y across every map layer, every gameplay coordinate header, the campaign positions, the saved actors and the QA scripts, and then needs the full host QA. That is too large to add without a host build. This change keeps the current frame, so its new geometry lines up with gameplay, and records the fix as the first follow-up.
