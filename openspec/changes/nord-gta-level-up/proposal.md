# NORD: a Plateau you recognize, and GTA play

## Why
The owner played the current build and said four things. The map still does not look like the Plateau. Cars cannot hit people. There is no police, no weapons, no combat and too few missions. Driving on a phone over the stream is very hard because of lag.

The current map gives detailed fronts to about 154 park-facing buildings and the Panet/Visitation walk. Most other buildings are plain tall brick boxes. Streets and sidewalks read as one beige surface. Cars treat people as walls or pass them. The phone wheel sends digital A/D keys, so a small drag gives full lock, and each correction arrives late.

## What changes
- **Plateau streetscape.** A new offline generator turns mapped footprints into Plateau plexes: two or three storeys, 7.6 m lots, brick in local colours, painted wood cornices, balconies on each upper floor and black iron exterior stairs to the second floor. Streets get dark asphalt, yellow centre lines, white crosswalks and stop bars, grey concrete sidewalks with 18 cm curbs and tree pits. Corners get white street-name signs with black text, ARRÊT signs, lamps, hydrants and BIXI docks. The host imports the batches with one editor script.
- **A lived-in layer.** The owner pointed to Alex Lingris's "City building games have a Soul Problem pt.2" (Radical Elements, October 2026): a city should reward a casual look with wear, clutter and traces of the people who live there, made by procedural rules, not by hand. The generator adds patched asphalt and potholes, leaf drifts in gutters, weeds at wall bases and curb joints, soft edges where sidewalk meets grass, orange cones and a construction fence, laundry lines and wooden utility poles with sagging wires over the ruelles, recycling and compost bins at the curb, bikes locked to railings, rust streaks under iron stairs, faded paint and original murals on side walls. A per-lot seed varies every choice, so each block holds small surprises. Play also leaves traces: tyre marks from hard braking stay on the road for a while, and cones and bins that a car hits stay where they fall until they respawn.
- **A street that feels alive.** The owner wants citizens walking dogs and pushing strollers, and cars that move and park. The runtime population adds dog walkers with a leashed dog, parents pushing strollers, people carrying groceries, cyclists on the Rachel bike path and people sitting on stoops and balconies. Traffic cars stop at the ARRÊT signs, wait for people who cross, pull into free curb spaces and park; their driver gets out and walks to a door. Other drivers come out, get into a parked car and pull back into traffic. Density stays inside the current frame budget by using the existing shared-time route model.
- **People can be hit.** A car that strikes a resident, pedestrian, police officer or player at speed knocks them down in a comic tumble. They lie dazed, then stand up and run away. No blood. Players lose health.
- **Police.** Crimes give the offender wanted stars, from one to five: hitting people, attacking someone, shooting near people, ramming police. Police cars and officers come after the player. They lose interest when they lose sight of the player for long enough. A garage on the map clears the stars. Officers can arrest a slow player on foot (ARRÊTÉ). A player at zero health is knocked out (K.-O.). Both respawn at a nearby fixed point.
- **Weapons and combat.** Fists, a hockey stick, a pistol, an SMG and a shotgun. Pickups sit at fixed points and come back after a delay. Aim assist locks the nearest target in front, so touch play works with stream delay. Hits knock people down. A health and ammo HUD appears.
- **Missions.** Four GTA-style side missions start from yellow markers: a timed delivery, a chase that ends when the target car is rammed to a stop, a fight with the Curator's ink goons, and an escape from a three-star chase. The campaign, its save file and its checks do not change.
- **Lag-tolerant driving.** The phone wheel sends three graded steering levels per side instead of full lock. The server smooths steering, damps yaw, keeps the car on the road heading when the wheel is near centre, and lets the car slide along walls. Keyboard driving keeps its current feel.

## Out of scope
Building interiors, swimming changes, new crew characters, voice acting, a money economy beyond a mission cash counter, car explosions, gore and online matchmaking. The Toronto map gets the gameplay systems but not the Plateau streetscape.

## Who checks what
This change is written in a cloud container that has no Unreal Engine and no GPU. The container checks: C++ syntax against a stub header tree with UE's Linux warnings, pure logic in native unit tests, Python generator tests, browser unit tests and Blender preview renders. The owner's host PC must build both targets, run the native automation tests, run the editor import, cook, and play on a phone. The tasks list keeps those host steps open until the host does them.
