# In Full Swing: one-tap phone swinging, wall climbing and roof antennas

## Why

The player said the phone game was hard to play and slow. They want it to look and feel like a Spider-Man game: point, tap, and rush through the air. A measured baseline confirmed it. Two taps per swing (SWING, then LET GO) gave an average of 8 m/s and 80 m covered in 12 s. A phone with no motion sensors could not plunge a clog at all, because a plunge needed a yank. A PC hint ("Press F to yank.") showed on the phone, and the top buttons covered the score.

The player then asked to climb buildings like Spider-Man (W/up arrow up, A/D sideways), to catch the small antennas on top of the towers, and to get on top of the tower (the Needle).

## What changes

- Phone play: one tap swings. The rope lets go by itself past the bottom of the arc and flings you on. A tap at open sky picks a good building ahead. A rope that catches gives a speed kick. The phone has faster physics (higher speed cap, less drag, more gravity). The camera turns to follow your flight. The view widens with speed, and comic speed lines show at the screen edges. A tap on a clog plunges it with no yank. The phone has its own tutorial lines, and its buttons no longer cover the score or the spoken lines.
- Flat play (mouse and phone): you can climb walls. Fly or walk into a wall to hold on. W/S or the up/down arrows climb, A/D or left/right go along it, Space jumps off, and a rope swings you off. At the top you step on to the roof. Under an overhang you move out to its face and climb on, so the Needle can be climbed to its tip. A phone shows a climb pad while you hold a wall.
- The roof antennas on towers over 60 m are colliders. A rope catches them and you can climb them.
- The game version goes to 1.4.0 (with the Quest APK version), so installed copies fetch the new files.

## Out of scope

Headset (VR/AR) play keeps its controls and physics. The trial rings, the map, full screen and an over-the-shoulder view are separate requests that follow this change.
