# First Breath: biology fact sheet

This fact sheet is for a game about a baby's life from ovulation to birth, told from the baby's point of view. Research date: 2026-10-17.

## How to read this sheet

- Each fact has its source URL next to it.
- Access tags show how the fact was checked:
  - **(R)**: the page was opened and read.
  - **(A)**: the PubMed abstract was read in full (fetched from the Europe PMC API).
  - **(S)**: only search-result text was seen. Check the page before you put the fact into the game.
- NCBI pages (StatPearls, PMC) showed a reCAPTCHA page to automated fetches. ACOG returned HTTP 402. Facts from those sites are tagged (S) or replaced with other sources.
- All week numbers are **gestational age (GA)** unless a fact says "after fertilization".
- Sizes for weeks 4 to 19 are head to bottom (crown-rump). From week 20, NHS measures head to heel. Because of this, the NHS length jumps from 15.3 cm at week 19 to 25.6 cm at week 20. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-20/

## 0. The two dating clocks

| Clock | Starts at | Who uses it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gestational age (GA), also called menstrual age | First day of the last menstrual period (LMP) | Parents, midwives, doctors, scans, due dates | (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/ ("Pregnancy is dated from the first day of your last period.") ; (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth |
| Fertilization age (post-conception or post-ovulation age) | Fertilization, about 2 weeks after the LMP | Embryologists, Carnegie stages | (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table ("for clinical Gestational Age (GA) measured from last menstrual period, add 2 weeks") |

Key conversions:

- Ovulation happens around day 14 of a typical cycle. So fertilization is near the end of GA week 2. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23439-ovulation ; (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth ("Fertilization occurs during the third week.")
- Full term is about 40 weeks GA. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
- 40 weeks GA = 280 days from LMP. That is about 266 days (38 weeks) after fertilization. (Derived: 280 - 14.)
- The Carnegie system has 23 stages. They cover the first 8 weeks after fertilization (about 56 to 60 days). Stages are set by body shape, not by age or size. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table ; (R) https://embryo.asu.edu/pages/carnegie-stages
- Day counts differ slightly between sources. embryology.ch sets fertilization as day 0. UNSW counts days after ovulation, so fertilization is day 1. (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/preimplantation/segmentation-migration/migration-embryon.html ; (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Week_1

Mixed clocks cause conflicts. Example: "the embryo becomes a fetus".

- NHS says "around" week 8. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-8/
- Cleveland Clinic says the fetal stage begins around week 9. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth
- MedlinePlus says "At the end of the 10th week of pregnancy, your baby is no longer an embryo." (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm
- In embryology, the embryonic period ends at Carnegie stage 23, 56 to 60 days after fertilization. That is the end of GA week 10. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table

Trimesters also differ by source:

- NHS: 1st trimester "up until week 12". NHS sections are weeks 13 to 27 and weeks 28 to 40. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/ ; (S) https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/13-to-27/
- Cleveland Clinic: "The first trimester lasts until the end of the 13th week of pregnancy." The same page also says "until the beginning of the 13th week". (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/9699-first-trimester

### Recommended convention for the game

1. Use one main clock: **"Week N"**, which is gestational age. Players, the NHS and scan reports all use it.
2. Chapters 1 to 4 (ovulation to implantation) all happen in GA weeks 2 to 4. In these chapters, also show a small second clock: **"Day N after fertilization"**, with fertilization as Day 0. Show the matching GA week in brackets, for example "Day 3 after fertilization (week 3)".
3. Never show a fertilization-age week without its label.
4. Change "embryo" to "fetus" at the **end of week 10**. Add one short line that explains this. Many apps say week 8 because they count from fertilization.
5. Use trimester borders **1 to 13, 14 to 27, 28 to 40**. State the borders on the chapter cards. NHS ends the first trimester at week 12, so do not show the borders as exact.
6. Sizes: show the length in cm or mm, plus one comparison object. Change from head-to-bottom to head-to-heel at week 20, and show a short note at that point.

## 1. Ovulation

1. Each month, several follicles in the ovaries start to grow an egg. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23439-ovulation
2. Usually, one dominant follicle is selected. It releases its egg at mid-cycle. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22068695/
3. A follicle grows for about two weeks. Around day 14, a sudden surge of luteinizing hormone (LH) makes the follicle burst and release the mature egg. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23439-ovulation
4. The human egg is about 0.1 mm across. A sperm is about 46 µm long. So the egg is about two sperm lengths wide. (R) https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=111184 ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22848657/
5. The egg leaves inside a cloud of cumulus cells. Under the cells is a clear shell, the zona pellucida. The inner layer of cells is the corona radiata. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/ ; (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
6. The egg is paused in the middle of its last division (metaphase II). It finishes this division only after a sperm fuses with it. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
7. Fimbriae are finger-like ends of the fallopian tube. They move closer to the ovary near ovulation, extend over it and catch the egg. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/24346-fimbriae
8. Tiny hairs (cilia) on the fimbriae beat toward the uterus. This sweeps the egg into the tube. The fimbriae have more cilia than the rest of the tube. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/24346-fimbriae
9. The egg cannot swim. Beating cilia and contractions of the tube's muscle wall move it. (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/preimplantation/segmentation-migration/migration-embryon.html
10. The egg survives about 12 to 24 hours after ovulation. Then the body reabsorbs it. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23439-ovulation
11. Sex can lead to pregnancy from about five days before ovulation to one day after. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/23439-ovulation
12. In a study of 221 women, almost all pregnancies came from sex in the six days that end on ovulation day. The chance per cycle went from 0.10 (sex five days before ovulation) to 0.33 (sex on ovulation day). (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7477165/

## 2. The sperm journey

### Numbers and body

1. One ejaculation releases tens of millions of sperm, and sometimes hundreds of millions. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm
2. A sperm has a head, a midpiece and a tail. A cap on the head (the acrosome) holds enzymes. The midpiece holds mitochondria that power the tail. The tail is the longest part. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm
3. Only a minority of sperm enter the cervix. Only a few thousand reach the fallopian tubes. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/
4. In one human study, women were inseminated about 18 hours before planned surgery. A median of **251** sperm were found in both tubes together (range 79 to 1,386). (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8150897/

### Vagina, cervix and mucus

5. Sperm land in the vagina, which is acidic and has immune defenses. They quickly enter cervical mucus. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/
6. Just before ovulation, cervical mucus becomes clear, slippery and stretchy, like egg white. This makes swimming easy. At other times it is thick, and it blocks sperm. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/21957-cervical-mucus
7. Cervical mucus filters out sperm with a poor shape or weak swimming. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/
8. After sperm arrive, white blood cells (leukocytes) gather in the cervix. They may remove sperm by eating them (phagocytosis), or clear bacteria. This is a normal response. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3979580/

### Uterus, utero-tubal junction, choosing a tube

9. Waves of uterine muscle contraction carry sperm fast. In one scan study, sperm-sized particles placed at the cervix reached the tube in about **1 minute**. Scientists think this fast trip is passive. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8671281/
10. Late in the cycle, these waves sent the particles mostly into the tube on the side of the ovary with the dominant follicle. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8671281/
11. In the human tube study, both tubes held similar numbers of sperm. But the ampulla on the ovulating side held a larger share. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8150897/
12. The utero-tubal junction is a narrow gate into the tube. In mammals, there is evidence that sperm need specific surface molecules to pass it. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26183721/

### Waiting, survival and becoming ready

13. In the tube, most sperm bind to the tube lining. This reservoir keeps them alive and able to fertilize until ovulation. Released sperm detach and re-attach many times as they move up. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26183721/ . Human data are less clear. One human study did not see sperm bound to the tube lining. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8150897/
14. Sperm can stay alive in the female reproductive tract for up to five days. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/sperm
15. Sperm must change inside the female tract before they can fertilize. This change is called capacitation. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/ ; (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
16. In human lab tests, only **2 to 14%** of sperm were capacitated at any one time. The ready state lasted about **50 minutes to 4 hours**. Each sperm reaches it only once. New sperm keep becoming ready. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7479932/
17. Near ovulation, sperm become capacitated and hyperactivated. Hyperactivation is a stronger swimming pattern. It helps them push through mucus in the tube, the cumulus and the zona. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/

### Guidance: flow, heat and chemicals

18. **Rheotaxis:** mouse and human sperm turn to swim against a gentle fluid flow. In mice, mating starts a flow in the tube within 4 hours. Sperm roll (rotate) as they swim, and this holds them in the stream. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23453951/
19. **Walls:** sperm that meet a wall tend to keep swimming along it. The female tract seems to use this to guide sperm. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26183721/
20. **Thermotaxis:** capacitated human sperm swim toward warmer places. They responded from at least 29 to 41 °C. They sensed gradients below 0.014 °C per mm, which is less than 0.0006 °C over one sperm length. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22848657/
21. To follow heat, human sperm swim faster and turn less often when they move toward warmer water. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25609239/
22. **Chemotaxis:** cumulus cells around the egg release progesterone. Progesterone opens the CatSper calcium channel in the sperm tail. This triggers hyperactivation, chemotaxis toward the egg and the acrosome reaction. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21412339/
23. Only 2 to 12% of human sperm respond to signals from the follicle. The sperm that respond are mostly the capacitated ones. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7479932/
24. Follicular fluid from different women attracted sperm from different men by different amounts. This choice did not always match the woman's partner. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32517615/
25. Scientists think thermotaxis and chemotaxis together guide sperm to the egg. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/

### Speed

26. In one lab study, the straight-line speed of single human sperm was 0 to 35 µm per second at 37 °C. 35 µm/s is about 2 mm per minute, which is less than one body length per second. (R) https://bionumbers.hms.harvard.edu/bionumber.aspx?id=114281 (primary: Van den Bergh et al. 1998, Hum Reprod 13:3103)
27. Game note: swimming alone is slow. Contractions, flows and guidance do much of the work (facts 9, 18 and 25).

## 3. Fertilization

1. Fertilization usually happens in the fallopian tube. It can sometimes happen in the uterus. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11585-conception . The site is the ampulla, the wide outer part of the tube. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/ ; (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/preimplantation/segmentation-migration/migration-embryon.html
2. **Corona radiata:** sperm pass through this layer of cells with the enzyme hyaluronidase on the sperm membrane and by beating their tails. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
3. **Zona pellucida:** a clear protein shell. A sperm binds to it. The acrosome reaction releases enzymes, for example acrosin, that help the sperm get through the zona. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
4. **When the acrosome reaction starts:** textbooks say it starts when the sperm touches the zona. In a mouse study, most sperm that fertilized started the reaction earlier, in the cumulus. For humans, the answer is still open. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21383182/
5. **Fusion:** the sperm protein Izumo1 binds the egg protein Juno. The pair is the same in several mammals, including humans. Mouse eggs without Juno cannot fuse with sperm. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24739963/
6. The sperm membrane fuses with the egg membrane, and the egg takes the sperm's contents inside. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
7. **Only one sperm:** after fusion, the egg sheds Juno from its membrane quickly. This is a likely membrane block to more sperm. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24739963/
8. **Zona block:** cortical granules in the egg release their contents. Enzymes change the zona so that other sperm cannot bind or get through. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
9. The egg finishes its last division and pushes out a second polar body. (R) https://anatomy.ttuhscep.edu/embryo/03firstweek.htm
10. **Two pronuclei, no fusion:** the egg nucleus and the sperm nucleus become two pronuclei. They move together. In human zygotes, the parents' chromosomes gather where the pronuclei touch, "yet separated". The two sets join only when the nuclear envelopes break down at the first division. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33964210/
11. A sperm can get through the egg's outer layer within a few minutes. The full process can take several hours. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11585-conception . StatPearls says fertilization finishes in about 24 hours. (S) https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK542186/
12. The fertilized egg is a zygote. It is Carnegie stage 1, about 0.1 to 0.15 mm across, with two pronuclei. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table

### Facts

1. The zygote divides into 2 cells, then 4, then more. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/11585-conception
2. The embryo does not grow during these divisions. Its cells get smaller. It stays about 0.1 to 0.2 mm across from Carnegie stage 1 to stage 5 (about the first 12 days). (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table
3. Beating cilia and muscle contractions of the tube move the embryo toward the uterus. (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/preimplantation/segmentation-migration/migration-embryon.html
4. The blastocyst forms about five to six days after conception. Before implantation, it has about 70 to 100 cells. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22889-blastocyst
5. The inner cells (the inner cell mass) become the embryo. The outer cells become the membranes that feed and protect it. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002398.htm ; (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22889-blastocyst
6. **Hatching:** the blastocyst breaks out of its clear zona shell. Cleveland Clinic says this happens one to three days after the blastocyst enters the uterus. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22889-blastocyst
7. **Attachment:** the outer cells release a sticky protein that binds to the uterine lining (endometrium). (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22889-blastocyst
8. **Invasion:** an outer layer of fused cells (the syncytiotrophoblast) releases enzymes. It surrounds the blastocyst and lets it move into the uterine wall. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Week_2
9. The human embryo buries itself fully inside the lining (interstitial implantation). (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12050279/
10. Spaces (lacunae) open in the syncytiotrophoblast and fill with the mother's blood. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Week_2 ; (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/fetal-membranes-and-placenta/development-of-the-placental-villi/the-three-villus-types/
11. **hCG:** the implanting embryo's syncytiotrophoblast releases hCG in week 2 after fertilization. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Week_2
12. In pregnancies that lasted at least six weeks, hCG first appeared in urine 6 to 12 days after ovulation. In 84% of them, this happened on day 8, 9 or 10. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10362823/
13. hCG nearly doubles every three days for the first 8 to 10 weeks. It peaks around week 10. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/22489-human-chorionic-gonadotropin
14. hCG keeps the corpus luteum making progesterone. Progesterone keeps the uterine lining in place. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
15. Some home tests can show pregnancy at about 3.5 weeks GA. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/

## 5. Embryo and placenta

### Building the placenta

1. The placenta grows from the embryo, not from the mother. It has the same genes as the fetus. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
2. At week 4, the outer layer of cells will later become the placenta. A small yolk sac feeds the embryo for now. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-4/
3. **Chorionic villi** grow in three steps (days after fertilization):
   - Days 11 to 13: primary villi. Cytotrophoblast cells push into cords of syncytiotrophoblast.
   - After day 16: secondary villi. Connective tissue (mesoblast) grows in.
   - End of week 3: tertiary villi. Blood vessels form inside and join the embryo's circulation.
   (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/fetal-membranes-and-placenta/development-of-the-placental-villi/the-three-villus-types/
4. The lacunae join into one space, the intervillous space. The villi sit in "lakes" of maternal blood that has no vessel walls. (R) https://histology.siu.edu/erg/placenta.htm ; (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/fetal-membranes-and-placenta/development-of-the-placental-villi/the-three-villus-types/
5. The fetal blood stays inside vessels in the villi. Only a thin cell layer (the syncytiotrophoblast) and the fetal capillary wall separate the two bloods. All exchange of gases, nutrients, hormones and waste happens across this layer. (R) https://histology.siu.edu/erg/placenta.htm
6. Maternal and fetal blood never come into direct contact. (R) https://embryology.ch/en/embryogenese/fetal-membranes-and-placenta/development-of-the-placental-villi/the-three-villus-types/ ; (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
7. About 100 to 150 spiral arteries of the mother feed the placenta. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Placenta_Development
8. **Low oxygen at first:** full maternal arterial blood flow into the placenta starts only at the end of the first trimester. Oxygen in the placenta rises from below 20 mmHg at 8 weeks to above 50 mmHg at 12 weeks. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11106583/
9. **Gland food at first:** before then, the uterine glands feed the embryo. They stay active until at least week 10, and their secretions flow into the intervillous space. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12050279/
10. The placenta takes over most hormone production by the end of the first trimester. Before that, the corpus luteum does most of it. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
11. At term, the placenta is about 22 cm long, about 2.5 cm thick at the center and about 500 g. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
12. At week 21, the baby becomes heavier than the placenta. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-21/

### What the placenta does, and what crosses

13. The placenta acts as the fetus's lungs, kidneys and liver until birth. It gives oxygen, glucose and nutrients. It removes carbon dioxide and waste. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
14. **Antibodies:** IgG is the only antibody class that crosses the human placenta in large amounts. A receptor (FcRn) on the syncytiotrophoblast carries it across. Transfer depends on gestational age. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22235228/
15. Most antibodies pass near the end of pregnancy. This protection lasts for several months after birth. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta
16. Harmful antibodies can cross too, and they can cause short-term autoimmune disease in the newborn. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22235228/
17. **Not filtered: alcohol.** "Alcohol in the mother's blood passes to the baby through the umbilical cord." There is no known safe amount. (R) https://www.cdc.gov/fasd/about/index.html
18. **Not filtered: smoke.** Carbon monoxide is "particularly harmful to developing babies". Cigarettes can restrict the baby's oxygen supply. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/stop-smoking/
19. **Not filtered: some viruses.** A pregnant person with CMV can pass it to the baby. About 1 in 200 babies is born with CMV. (R) https://www.cdc.gov/cytomegalovirus/congenital-infection/index.html
20. Placenta hormones: hCG, progesterone, estrogen, human placental lactogen, placental growth hormone and relaxin. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/22337-placenta

### Umbilical cord

21. At week 5, the embryo already has some of its own blood vessels. A string of them will make up the umbilical cord. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/1st-trimester/week-5/
22. One umbilical vein brings oxygen-rich blood from the placenta to the fetus. Two umbilical arteries take oxygen-poor blood back. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/umbilical-cord
23. A white jelly (Wharton's jelly) wraps and protects the cord vessels. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/umbilical-cord ; (R) https://histology.siu.edu/erg/placenta.htm
24. By the end of the second trimester, the cord is about 50 to 60 cm long and 1 to 2 cm thick. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/umbilical-cord

### Neural tube, heart, embryo to fetus

25. **Neural tube:** it closes 21 to 27 days after conception. That is GA week 6. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Abnormal_Development_-_Folic_Acid_and_Neural_Tube_Defects
26. The front end of the neural tube (rostral neuropore) closes at Carnegie stage 11, days 23 to 26. The back end (caudal neuropore) closes at stage 12, days 26 to 30. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Carnegie_stage_table
27. Folic acid helps the nervous system form and gives some protection from spina bifida. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/week-by-week/1-to-12/5-weeks/
28. **First heartbeat:** the heart starts to beat about 22 to 23 days after fertilization (GA week 5 to 6). Blood flow starts in week 4 after fertilization. (R) https://embryology.med.unsw.edu.au/embryology/index.php/Cardiovascular_System_-_Heart_Development

### Hearing and voice

5. Around week 15, the baby starts to hear the mother's heartbeat and the sounds of her gut. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/

### Hiccups, swallowing, thumb sucking

9. Hiccups can happen by week 19. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/articles/7247-fetal-development-stages-of-growth . The mother may feel tiny hiccups later. ### Body and lungs

14. A soft hair (lanugo) grows over the body. The eyes become sensitive to light. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-15/

### Sleep and wake

21. Before 36 weeks, body movement, eye movement and heart rate each have cycles. But they do not yet change together. True linked states (quiet sleep, active sleep, awake) show only at **38 to 40 weeks**. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7094856/

### Stages of labor

- Regular labor contractions come about three to five minutes apart. (R) same page
- Oxytocin makes the uterus contract. Pressure on the cervix and vagina sends nerve signals that release more oxytocin. This makes stronger contractions (a positive feedback loop called the Ferguson reflex). (R) https://teachmephysiology.com/reproductive-system/pregnancy/labour/

### Cardinal movements of a head-first birth

The baby must turn to fit the shape of the pelvis. Sources: (R) https://www.glowm.com/article/heading/vol-11--labor-and-delivery--presentation-and-mechanism-of-labor/id/414323 (Dutta, "Mechanism of Normal Labor"), (R) https://oacapps.med.jhmi.edu/OBGYN-101/Text/Labor%20and%20Delivery/mechanism_of_normal_labor.htm, (R) https://openstax.org/books/maternal-newborn-nursing/pages/15-2-stages-of-labor

| # | Movement | What the baby does |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Engagement | The widest part of the head (the biparietal diameter) reaches or passes the pelvic brim. |
| 2 | Descent | The baby moves down. This continues through stages 1 and 2. |
| 3 | Flexion | The chin tucks onto the chest. The smallest part of the head now leads. (OB-GYN 101; GLOWM) |
| 4 | Internal rotation | The back of the head (occiput) turns toward the mother's front, under the pubic bone. (GLOWM) |
| 5 | Extension | The head tips back and comes out under the pubic arch. The perineum stretches (crowning). (GLOWM) |
| 6 | Restitution | The head is out. It turns back to line up with the shoulders again. (GLOWM) |
| 7 | External rotation | The shoulders turn inside to a front-to-back line. The head turns more, in the same direction. The face turns toward one of the mother's thighs. (GLOWM; OpenStax) |
| 8 | Expulsion | The front shoulder comes out, then the back shoulder. The rest of the body follows by bending sideways (lateral flexion). (GLOWM; OpenStax describes the front-then-back shoulder order) |

- Most lists name 7 movements and join restitution with external rotation. Some lists count only the 4 rotations. The movements overlap and do not happen as separate steps. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34214716/

### First breath, fluid and shunts

1. At birth, the baby's lungs are filled with fluid. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002395.htm
2. The baby takes its first breath within about 10 seconds after delivery. (R) same
3. The fluid drains out or is absorbed. Most of it is absorbed into lung cells. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002395.htm ; (R) https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/perinatal-physiology/perinatal-physiology
4. At the first breath, surfactant spreads into the air sacs. (R) https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/perinatal-physiology/perinatal-physiology
5. More oxygen in the lungs lowers the resistance to blood flow in the lungs. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002395.htm
6. **Fetal shortcuts (shunts):** the foramen ovale moves blood from the right atrium to the left atrium. The ductus arteriosus moves blood from the pulmonary artery to the aorta. The ductus venosus lets most of the oxygen-rich blood from the cord skip the liver. (R) https://www.stanfordchildrens.org/en/topic/default?id=fetal-circulation-90-P01790
7. The cord is clamped, so oxygen from the mother stops. The lungs expand. The change in pressure closes the shunts. (R) same
8. A flap pushes against the foramen ovale and closes it. The flap fuses later in about 75% of people. (R) https://www.msdmanuals.com/professional/pediatrics/perinatal-physiology/perinatal-physiology
9. The ductus arteriosus narrows and closes by about 24 to 72 hours after birth. (R) same
10. The whole change from fetal to newborn life needs: clearing lung fluid, releasing surfactant, steady breathing, big changes in blood flow and pressure, and control of heat and energy. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23164177/
11. **Cord clamping:** NICE says do not clamp the cord in the first minute unless there are concerns. (S) https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/qs105/chapter/Quality-statement-6-Delayed-cord-clamping

### First cry and first minutes

12. Babies do not always come out crying like they do in films. It can take a few seconds before they cry or splutter to clear their airways. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-39/
13. The skin is dark red or purple at first. It turns brighter red after the first breath. Hands and feet may stay blue. (R) same
14. Breathing can take a few minutes to become regular. Fast breaths and slow breaths alternate. Pauses of up to 5 seconds are normal. (R) same
15. Newborns lose heat. They make warmth by burning brown fat. Newborns rarely shiver. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002395.htm

### Skin-to-skin

16. Skin-to-skin contact starts as soon as possible after birth. (R) https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/baby/baby-basics/caring-for-your-baby/skin-to-skin-contact-with-your-newborn/
17. It helps control the baby's temperature, breathing and heart rate. It starts feeding instincts. It releases oxytocin. Hearing the parent's heartbeat and voice calms the baby. (R) same
18. Newborns learned to suck in a way that played their mother's voice instead of a stranger's. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7375928/

### Apgar score

19. The score is checked at 1 minute and 5 minutes after birth. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003402.htm . If the 5-minute score is below 7, checks continue every 5 minutes up to 20 minutes. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/23094-apgar-score
20. Five parts, each scored 0, 1 or 2 (total 0 to 10). Scoring from (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003402.htm :

| Letter | Part | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Appearance (skin color) | pale blue | body pink, hands and feet blue | all pink |
| P | Pulse (heart rate) | no heartbeat | below 100 bpm | above 100 bpm |
| G | Grimace (reflex) | no reaction | grimace | grimace and a cough, sneeze or strong cry |
| A | Activity (muscle tone) | loose and floppy | some tone | active motion |
| R | Respiration (breathing) | not breathing | slow or irregular | cries well |

21. A score of 7 to 10 is reassuring, 4 to 6 is moderately abnormal, 0 to 3 is low. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/23094-apgar-score
22. A score of 10 is unusual. Almost all newborns lose 1 point for blue hands and feet. (R) https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003402.htm
23. A low score does not mean the child will have long-term health problems. (R) same
24. Dr Virginia Apgar, an anesthesiologist, described the score in 1952. The letters A-P-G-A-R were added in 1962 to make it easy to remember. (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/diagnostics/23094-apgar-score

## 10. Myths to avoid, and what science says now

| Myth | What current science says | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| "Millions of sperm race, and the fastest one wins." | There is no mass race. Only a few thousand sperm enter the tubes, and a few hundred or fewer are near the egg. Reviewers say "there seems to be no competition ... among large numbers of sperm cells that are racing towards the egg". The first fast trip is carried by uterine contractions. Sperm wait in the tube. Only 2 to 14% are ready (capacitated) at a time, and only for 50 minutes to 4 hours. So the sperm that fertilizes must be ready at the right time and place. Flows, walls, heat and chemicals guide it. Game framing: win by being ready and following the signals, not by top speed. | (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16607290/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16272225/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8671281/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8150897/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7479932/ |
| "The female tract picks the best sperm." (Overcorrection) | Only motile sperm get through the mucus. But it is not proven that the tract always selects the "best" sperm. Some species let abnormal sperm reach the tube. | (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26386468/ |
| "The egg waits passively, and a heroic sperm drills in." | This story comes from cultural stereotypes, not data (Martin 1991). The female side is active. Fimbriae catch the egg. Uterine waves aim sperm at the correct tube. Cumulus cells release progesterone. Follicular fluid attracts some men's sperm more than others. The egg's Juno receptor is necessary for fusion. | Martin 1991, Signs 16(3): https://doi.org/10.1086/494680 ; (R) https://my.clevelandclinic.org/health/body/24346-fimbriae ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8671281/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21412339/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32517615/ ; (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24739963/ |
| "The acrosome reaction always happens on the zona." | In mice, most sperm that fertilize start it earlier, in the cumulus. For humans, it is not settled. | (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21383182/ |
| "The two nuclei fuse into one." | In human zygotes, the two pronuclei touch but stay separate. The parents' chromosomes join when the nuclear envelopes break down at the first division. ## 11. Sensitive facts: a design note

- Early loss is common. In one study, 25% of detected conceptions ended in early loss. The risk was higher when implantation happened later. (A) https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10362823/
- Recommendation: do not make a fail state that looks like a real miscarriage, or that blames the player or a parent. Use "try again" for gameplay failure, for example a sperm that misses the egg.
- Do not give medical advice in the game. Facts about alcohol, smoking or reduced movements can link to NHS or CDC pages. They should not tell a player what to do about their own pregnancy.
