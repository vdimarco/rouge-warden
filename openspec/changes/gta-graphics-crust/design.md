# A pale deposit, not another clay multiply

The National Park Service Preservation Brief 1, "Assessing Cleaning and Water-Repellent Treatments for Historic Masonry Buildings", describes efflorescence as a light deposit of soluble salts on the face of masonry. The brief is about cleaning, not shading. What is taken from it is the look of the deposit: paler than the clay, sitting on the unit, absent from stone.

The crust is an albedo lerp to `(0.84, 0.82, 0.76)` on clay units whose hash is at least 0.64, on the face above the joint and inside the edges. Vertical streaks keep it from reading as a sticker. Mortar is applied after the crust, so the bed stays its own colour. Glass, doors, rails, and the lamp apron are drawn later and cover the face where they sit. A height field still does not fit this one-colour shader.

Unreal is not installed here. The picture is the same straight elevation as the face round, with the crust added. It is not an engine capture.
