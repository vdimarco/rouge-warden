# One lap, paid once

No new rendering technique is involved, so none of the usual art references fit. The buoy is an engine cylinder with the comic lime and cream materials the atelier already uses. The new part is the lap rule, the same kind of engine-free number the paddle speeds already use.

The buoy is the north-pond swim point, more than 50 m from the berth, so the two reaches do not overlap. The island sits between them, which is why a straight crossing is not the lap. Only a pedalo advances the leg. Dock becomes Buoy at the buoy, and Buoy becomes Done at the berth. Done stays Done, and the 120 cash is written on the driver's session purse the way the stall writes a sale. Nothing is saved. Car handling and the paddle speeds are the branches this rule does not take.
