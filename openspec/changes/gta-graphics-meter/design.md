# An edge rule and quarter marks

Edward Tufte, The Visual Display of Quantitative Information (1983), puts reference marks on a bar so its length can be judged without a printed number. The health and armour bars gain three cuts at the quarters. A paper lip covers only the filled length. A cut inside the fill is ink. A cut past the fill is paper, so it still shows on the dark track.

Scott McCloud, Understanding Comics (1993), treats the panel border as the line that separates a panel from the scene. The mission card already draws that rule 4 scale units in, and this column's padding is only 4 scale units, so a second inset rule would sit on the type. The street rule is drawn on the edge, clamped so it stays inside that padding, with the existing warm accent on the left.

The column fill goes from opacity .34 to .62 so the border has a body. A calm street still hides the column. Unreal is not installed, so `QuebecGameHUD.cpp` was not compiled. The picture is an offline shade of the column at scale 1 on a 1280×720 frame, and again at phone scale on a 390×844 frame. The words are a stand-in. The game strings are unchanged.
