# Design

## Constraints
- The cloud container has no Unreal Engine, no GPU and no dotnet. Every native, C# and frame-time claim stays open until the host builds and runs it.
- The host compiles with clang `-Wall -Werror -Wshadow`. No local, parameter or lambda parameter may reuse a member name, own or inherited.
- The canonical spec requires 60 FPS native and WebRTC ceilings and forbids lowering production visual settings without an approved tradeoff (`openspec/specs/nord-stream-responsiveness/spec.md`, `openspec/specs/nord-unreal/spec.md`).
- The desktop gate stays as it is: `Scripts/profile_package.py`, p95 below 16.67 ms at Epic and 100%.
- The world is mirrored, and a fix will flip Y (`openspec/changes/nord-gta-level-up/design.md`). Route data must survive that fix without hand edits.
- Capture and profile scripts must not overwrite historical `Preview/` and `Verification/` files (`CLAUDE_HANDOFF.md`). Gauntlet output goes to new timestamped folders.

## Decisions
1. **Two meanings, one loop.** "Gauntlet loop" means a `docs/gauntlet/` round loop scored with rubric v1. This change adds the third NORD loop, `docs/gauntlet/gta-mobile.md`. An Unreal Gauntlet run on the host measures each round.
2. **Keep the 60 caps and judge frames against 33.3 ms.** A 30 cap added about 30 ms of receiver buffering: 73 ms against 43 ms (`Verification/DrivingLatency.md`). At a 30 cap the phone ladder can only step down, because it needs 35 FPS to step up (`Browser/config.mjs`). FrameTime therefore has a 16.7 ms floor, and GPUTime shows the headroom.
3. **Split "30 FPS latency" in two.** The frame-rate floor is gated. Delay (round-trip time, jitter, jitter buffer delay and target, decode, processing, end to end) is reported in its own section, apart from the frame-rate results, as `nord-stream-responsiveness` asks ("records decoded frame rate and receiver buffering separately"). The delay section itself is never gated. One figure in it also has a limit in the stream gate: the mean jitter buffer delay of at most 120 ms per frame. That limit is the player's own lag rule (`receiverLagging` in `Browser/config.mjs`), so it checks stream health, not a delay target. No other delay figure is gated.
4. **One viewer is gated.** The gate is one phone viewer on NordHost, with NordFriend1 idle and asleep. Two viewers and the TURN relay are report-only rows. The co-op target stays with nord-stream-speed.
5. **The tuned settings are an opt-in profile.** Production stays unchanged until the user approves paired captures. A separate change flips the default.

## Route
The stops come from the five replica manifest cameras (`Data/Plateau/ReplicaGeometry/Streets/manifest.json`). They are the capture streets with curb-to-building coverage, plex skins and mapped trees, so they are expected to be the heaviest. Round 1 checks this by ranking per-leg p95.

| Stop | Eye position (cm) | Target (cm) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 Sherbrooke | (36000, 6200, 170) | (41000, 8800, 300) |
| S2 Papineau | (24500, 44000, 170) | (27500, 49500, 300) |
| S3 Fabre | (-6000, 44000, 170) | (-2600, 48800, 300) |
| S4 RachelPark | (-15500, 30500, 170) | (-11000, 33500, 250) |
| S5 Mentana | (-30500, 21800, 170) | (-27000, 26500, 300) |

The order is S1 to S5, then back to S1. `QuebecGauntletModel.h` `FForwardRoads` finds the path between stops over the through roads of `LaFontaineExtension::RadarRoadSegments`: the open segments without dead-end branches, because the car never turns back. `FRoadGraph::Route` is not used, because it can run to the junction ahead and straight back. Each stop's road point is the nearest point on a through road. Rue Rachel along the park is a dead end, so the S4 road point is on the nearest through road, 18 m from its eye. The car turns round the block south of S1. `FinishLoop` then rounds each junction into an arc the car can drive. The offline model gives legs of about 526, 460, 165, 462 and 1,876 m: a 3,488 m driven line, about 3.5 km. The host has not measured it yet. The route test reports the length and requires 3.0 to 4.0 km, a range centred on the modelled line. `WALK_ROUTE` and the campaign RaceRoute do not reach these streets.

`make_gauntlet_route.py` generates `QuebecGauntletRoute.h` from the manifest. A test fails when the two disagree, so the mirror fix needs one regeneration.

The subsystem seats the avatar in a car at S1 with `Interact` and disables pawn input, as `Scripts/test_race_drive.py` does. Each tick it feeds `MoveForward` and `MoveRight`. It steers at a point ahead on the rounded line and slows for the slowest corner within the police bend range ahead, as the police road follower does (`QuebecPolice.cpp`). Corner speeds come from the police `CornerSpeed` (`QuebecPursuitModel.h`). The lateral error is measured from the rounded line. Speed is capped at 15 m/s. The stuck rule is the police one: reverse for 1.1 s after 1.2 s stuck. `SetAutopilot` is not used, because a possessed car ignores it, and the chase camera reacts to speed only for a possessed car. Car handling matches at 30 and 120 Hz, so laps repeat across frame rates.

Perf and stream runs keep ambient traffic and residents, and pass `-NordNoPolice -NordNoCampaign -NordNoSideJobs -NordNoCampaignSave`. Look runs also pass `-NordNoAmbient`. The sky is always pinned. Day is `-NordHour=15 -NordWeather=Clear -NordFreezeTime -NordFreezeWeather`. Night rain, the worst case with 24 lamps, 2,000 rain streaks and moon shadows, is `-NordHour=22 -NordWeather=Rain -NordFreezeTime -NordFreezeWeather`. A run without hour and weather exits with code 3, because QA runs do not spawn the sky otherwise (`QuebecSky.cpp`).

A perf run is one warm-up lap, which is discarded because it compiles shaders, plus N measured laps. The offline model drives a lap in about 296 s in a perf run and about 307 s in a look or stream run, where the car stops at each stop. Holds do not count, and the lap limit is 360 s of driving. A stream run also drives one warm-up lap first, so its gated lap is warm too. Otherwise a first-use shader compile or texture load during the gated lap could fail the gap and freeze gates for a reason that the perf run never shows. Each stop has two fixed views, `<stop>-eye` (the manifest pose at 85°) and `<stop>-chase` (the car stopped at the road point, facing the next leg, after 2.5 s for the camera and 120 frames for TSR). The HUD is off. Sizes come from `Browser/config.mjs` with the sampler viewports: rung 0 is 1412x652 and 652x1412, rung 1 is 1058x488 and 488x1058. The tuner works at 1412x652. Confirmation adds portrait. Rung 1 is used in the stream pass only.

## Measurement
**Native.** The subsystem starts the CSV profiler after the warm-up lap and stops it at the end, as the HORU template does after the map loads. It adds a CSV event at each leg start. The parser reads FrameTime, GameThreadTime, RenderThreadTime, RHIThreadTime and GPUTime (with `-csvGpuStats`) by name and records any missing column. The runner samples VRAM and GPU use per process each second with `nvidia-smi`, and each game process's CPU use from `/proc` (VP8 encodes on the CPU). The subsystem logs a `NORD_GAUNTLET summary` line from its own frame-delta counter. A p95 mismatch over 1 ms against the CSV flags the run.

**Native gate**, after the warm-up lap: FrameTime p95 at most 33.3 ms over the lap and on each leg. The other limits apply over the lap only: p99 at most 50 ms; at most 1% of frames over 50 ms; at most one hitch (over 66.7 ms) in any 30 s of frame time and none over 250 ms; peak VRAM at most 6,000 MiB per process, so two slots fit the 12,000 MiB guard; a clean `render_log_guard.py`; every profile CVar read back as requested; and the `loop.json` `fixed` CVars read back as `t.MaxFPS 60` and `r.AntiAliasingMethod 4`. With several measured laps, p95 and p99 are the median of the per-lap values, and the frame share and the hitch rule use every measured frame. The per-leg p95 values are also ranked in `native/legs.json`, so round 1 can find the heaviest leg.

**Stream.** `Browser/stream-sample.mjs` is extended, not replaced. It keeps mobile Chromium, touch and DPR 1. It records each `requestVideoFrameCallback` frame in `frames.json` and the page's `performance.timeOrigin` in `sample.json`. It also records candidate-pair round-trip time, inbound jitter, processing delay, jitter buffer target, the frontend `latencyCalculated` event, encoder QP and every size change. A QA-only `?pin=` keeps the rung fixed, and `build.mjs` refuses `NORD_QA_PIN=1` in a Vercel build. The car starts 95 s after the first BeginPlay, so NordFriend1 is asleep (60 s warm-up plus 30 s grace). It drives the warm-up lap, and the gated lap starts at the first `NORD_GAUNTLET leg=` line.

The runner cuts the gated lap out of the sample by the host log times. A frame's wall time is `timeOrigin` plus its `expectedDisplayTime`. From the frames in the lap the runner computes the p50, p95 and p99 decoded intervals, the share over 33.3 and 50 ms and the longest gap. A sample without `timeOrigin` leaves the two interval gates unverified, and its whole-sample intervals are report-only. That `expectedDisplayTime` uses the `performance.now()` clock, and that the page clock matches the host's `time.time()` within a few ms, are written from memory and unverified until a host run. The runner computes the lap's delay figures and encoder QP from the samples inside the lap, so the verdict does not need the sampler's own summary. At the first `done`, `fail` or `result` line the runner writes `END` to the trigger file. `stream-sample.mjs` then takes one last sample, makes no shot and writes its report, as at the end of `--seconds`. Without `END`, for example when the host stops before such a line, it runs to `--seconds`, 925 s by default: the 95 s delay, the 60 s ready grace, the warm and gated laps at the 360 s lap limit, five 4 s holds and 30 s. When the host quits first, the sampler's summary leaves out the samples at the end that have no connected peer, and records `streamEndedTimeMs`. Such a sample does not pass and exits non-zero, also as a standalone 60 s sample, because its summary covers less than it was asked to. That a real host exit leaves no connected peer is unverified. After the done line the host waits for its CSV file (30 s at most), then writes `native/route.json` and its `result` line (`QuebecGauntlet.cpp` `Complete`). So when the sampler stops, the runner follows the host log on to the `result` line, until the host quits, or for at most the CSV wait and 15 s, before it copies the log and judges the run. The runner also follows NordFriend1's log (`stream/friend-events.json`) and records the host command line, its world flags and the CVar readback in `run.json`.

**Stream gate**, solo, direct, both rungs, landscape and portrait, over the gated lap: mean decoded FPS at least 29; zero freezes; at most 1% of intervals over 50 ms; longest gap at most 500 ms; jitter buffer delay at most 120 ms per frame on average (a stream-health limit, see decision 3); `NORD_STREAM_FPS` at least 29 in every 5 s window, and decoded FPS within 3 of native. These follow the nord-stream-speed rules. The same verdict also needs: no size change inside the lap, a received size equal to the pinned rung's size in that orientation, no awake NordFriend1 `NORD_STREAM_FPS` window inside the lap, exit code 0, every CVar read back and at least one `nvidia-smi` process listing.

**Input row.** `stream --look` drags on the stream during the whole lap. Its stream figures are report-only. It gates the car's largest lateral error from `native/route.json` (at most 2.5 m) and the exit code. `gauntlet_loop.py route <stream run>` checks the stop order and the lateral error of any stream run.

**Report-only rows.** Two viewers, with one sampler per slot. Forced relay. Delay, labelled "host-PC headless Chromium with --disable-gpu, not a phone, not off-LAN".

**Contention.** The runner reuses `browser_streaming.preflight()` and records other GPU processes before and after. Any foreign GPU process labels the run "contended", and a contended run is never ranked.

**Output.** Each run writes `Saved/Gauntlet/<UTC>-<label>/` (git-ignored): `run.json`, `native/*.csv`, `native/legs.json`, `native/route.json`, `look/`, `stream/`, `verdict.json` and `review.html`. A gate's status is pass, fail, unverified or contended. After a round, only `Verification/Gauntlet/<YYYYMMDD>-<label>.json` (no PNGs) and approved stills in `Preview/Gauntlet/<YYYYMMDD>/` are committed.

## Visual check
The ceiling reference is all `sg.*` at 3, screen percentage 100, VSM on, Lumen GI and reflections at Epic, and TSR, as in the desktop release look. It is rendered twice. Run-to-run SSIM must be at least 0.98 on every image, or the round is invalid. The metric is luma SSIM with an 11x11 Gaussian window, sigma 1.5, K1 0.01 and K2 0.03, written in numpy. NVIDIA FLIP mean error is report-only when `flip-evaluator` is installed on the host, at a recorded 40 pixels per degree. The provisional floors, calibrated in round 1, are SSIM at least 0.85 on every image and a mean at least that of the current production profile. If the user rejects a candidate that passed, the floor rises above that candidate's lowest image, and the change is recorded. A look run takes both skies at each stop, so it switches the sky in place. Street wetness follows rain slowly (45 s to wet, 240 s to dry), and part of the wait before each still counts frames (120 for TSR). Without a fix, the wetness in a still would depend on the frame rate, and the day stills after a rain stop would show wet streets. Two fixes were possible: one look run per sky, or a settled switch. The settled switch is chosen. Under `-NordGauntlet`, `QuebecSky.cpp` `HostSetWeather` starts the new weather and the wetness settled, as a `-NordWeather=` start does, and logs `NORD_SKY_WEATHER command <weather> settled`. The clock is frozen, look runs have no traffic, and the sky lights turn in one step, so the scene is steady while the frame waits run. One look run per sky would double the look runs per candidate and change the runner and the tuner. At each stop during the stream run, the subsystem holds 4 s, the runner writes a trigger line, and the sampler saves the decoded frame and a full-page shot. Their SSIM against the native still is report-only, because traffic is live.

## Tuning
The knob ladders live in `Data/Gauntlet/loop.json`. The baseline is today's `QUALITY`.

| Knob | Steps | Baseline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shadows | `sg.ShadowQuality` 0 + VSM 0, 1 + VSM 0, 2 + VSM 0, 2 + VSM 1, 3 + VSM 1 | 1 + VSM 0 |
| `sg.GlobalIlluminationQuality` | 1, 2, 3 | 1 (floor) |
| `sg.ReflectionQuality` | 1, 2, 3 | 1 (floor) |
| `r.ScreenPercentage` (TSR) | 50, 60, 67, 75, 85, 100 | 75 |
| `sg.ViewDistanceQuality`, `sg.FoliageQuality`, `sg.EffectsQuality`, `sg.PostProcessQuality` | 1, 2, 3 | 2 |
| `sg.ShadingQuality` | 2, 3 | not set by `QUALITY`; the host readback gives it |
| `sg.AntiAliasingQuality`, `sg.TextureQuality` | 2, 3 | 2 (floor) |

Each enabled knob has one step below today's value for the downward search, or a `floor` reason in `loop.json` that says why it has none, and `gauntlet_tune.py check_loop` refuses a knob with neither. GI and reflections stop at 1, because steps 0 and 1 both leave Lumen off (from memory, unverified). Anti-aliasing quality stops at 2, because screen percentage cuts the TSR cost first. Textures stop at 2, because they cost VRAM, not frame time. The TSR floor of 50% and the cost of the new bottom steps are from Epic's guides, from memory, and unverified until the host measures them. `r.Nanite.MaxPixelsPerEdge` joins only if Nanite meshes show on the route. `-NordStreamBudget` joins when nord-stream-speed lands it. `loop.json` `fixed` names the CVars no candidate changes, `t.MaxFPS 60` and `r.AntiAliasingMethod 4` (TSR), so `check_loop` refuses a knob step that sets one. Every expected-CVar file also holds the fixed CVars that the candidate does not set (`gauntlet_tune.expected_cvars`), so every run reads them back. `t.MaxFPS 60` comes from the candidate or the phone profile. `r.AntiAliasingMethod 4` comes from the renderer settings in `Config/DefaultEngine.ini` in every run kind (from memory, unverified until the host reads it back). The stream codec stays VP8 (`stream.codec`). Stream ladder changes in `config.mjs` are recommendations for the user, never automatic.

A candidate is feasible when the night-rain landscape lap has FrameTime p95 at most 30.0 ms and GPUTime p95 at most 28.0 ms (a 10% margin; the gate stays 33.3 ms), and the hitch, VRAM and readback gates pass. Look is the mean SSIM over 20 images (10 views, two skies, landscape). A candidate gets a Look only when its look run exits cleanly and every one of the 20 stills has a score. Otherwise it gets an error and is never ranked, so a partial set cannot rank it on its easy views. Look values within max(0.005, 2 × (1 − mean repeatability SSIM)) tie, and a tie goes to the lower GPU p95. Millisecond differences below max(0.5 ms, 2 × |p95a − p95b|) of the two baseline runs tie.

The search is a greedy climb. Measure the baseline twice and the ceiling look twice; if the baseline is not feasible, search downward first, one step below per knob. Measure each single step up from the baseline once. Rank steps by Look gain divided by max(ms cost, 0.25) and drop steps with no visible gain. Combine steps in that order while the predicted GPU p95 stays at most 28 ms, measure, and drop the last step if infeasible. Then try each remaining step alone on top of the winner. Stop when nothing feasible and visibly better is left, after 24 measured candidates, or when the next candidate, at the planned candidate time, would not finish before the window end (`--until HH:MM`). `tune --reference <folder>` reuses a judged reference folder for the ceiling look, but only when its commit and package build equal the current ones, and when both ceiling runs at the tune size hold every still, exited cleanly and were not contended, as `render_ceiling` requires of a new ceiling run. Each candidate is scored on all 20 stills, so a partial ceiling would give every candidate an error.

Then confirm the baseline and the best ranked candidates, best first, with 3 measured laps each, both skies, landscape and portrait, until one passes every native gate. The stream runs follow by hand (notes-host step 8). If a stream row or the co-op guard drops the winner, `gauntlet_loop.py confirm <tune folder>` confirms the next ranked candidate. A confirmation that the window cut counts as not run, so `confirm` runs that candidate again, the baseline first, and replaces its result. When the baseline is itself the candidate, it runs once. A contended confirmation run runs once more, as in the search. After a second contended run the confirmation stops there, and that result also counts as not run. `report` treats both as missing evidence. While a ranked candidate has only missing evidence, the `confirmed` gate and the round are unverified, not failed, also after a better ranked candidate failed. A cut result that also holds a failed run counts as failed. The co-op guard skips a two-viewer row that a foreign GPU process contended. `confirm` writes no profile.

The durations are estimates from the offline model: `loop.json` plans 310 s per lap and 60 s to start and quit, and the search raises its plan to the longest candidate it has measured. A candidate is a perf run and a look run, about 20 minutes (1,190 s), so 24 take about 8 hours. Without `--reference`, the first candidate renders the ceiling look first, so the search plans it at 1,690 s. One confirmation run (1 warm and 3 measured laps) is about 22 minutes (1,310 s), and a candidate's confirmation is 4 such runs. So the baseline and the winner take about 3 hours more. The search keeps no time for the confirmation. A confirmation run starts only when its planned time ends before `--until`. The plan starts at `gauntlet_tune.run_estimate_s`, made longer by as much as the search found the host slower than its own plan (`tune.json` `planned_candidate_s` against the first candidate's plan). It rises to the longest confirmation run measured so far in that `tune` or `confirm` call, as the search does with its candidates. The confirmation stops at the first candidate that the window cut. The agreed window must therefore cover the search plus about 3 hours. It runs in an agreed GPU window with the live demo stopped.

## Phone profile
`Config/DefaultDeviceProfiles.ini` gets `+DeviceProfileNameAndTypes=NordPhoneStream,Linux` and a `[NordPhoneStream DeviceProfile]` section with `DeviceType=Linux`, `BaseProfileName=Linux` and `+CVars=` lines, `sg.*` first. The first version equals `QUALITY`, so switching changes nothing. `browser_streaming.py` gains `NORD_STREAM_QUALITY=current|phone|file:<path>`. `current` is the default and unchanged. `phone` passes `-DeviceProfile=NordPhoneStream`. `file:` takes a candidate ExecCmds file under `Saved/Gauntlet`. If the host shows that `-DeviceProfile=` does not apply in the Browser package, the launcher expands the same section into `-ExecCmds`, which is today's proven path. The CVar readback on every run catches a saved `GameUserSettings.ini` that overrides the profile. The ini syntax and the command-line override are written from memory and are unverified until the host reads them back. `gauntlet_loop.py write-profile --approved` rewrites only the section and writes an approval record beside the ini, `Config/NordPhoneStream.approval.json`, with the candidate, the tune folder, the time and the CVars. The parse test accepts a section that equals `QUALITY` or the approved record, so a hand edit after approval fails. That UE packaging ignores a `.json` file in `Config/` is from memory and unverified.

## Pieces

- C# (host): `Build/Scripts/NordGauntlet.Automation.csproj` and `Build/Scripts/NordMobileLoop.cs` (`NordMobileLoop : UnrealTestNode<UnrealTestConfiguration>`; params `mode`, `size`, `sky`, `laps`, `run`, `execcmds`; client role on `/Game/Maps/LaFontaine` with `-RenderOffscreen -ForceRes -unattended -nosound -csvGpuStats`; it runs the Browser package). `MaxDuration` covers the game's own limits: the load, the delay, the ready grace, every warm and measured lap at the lap limit, each hold (a look hold at its stills timeout) and the wait for the CSV file. `gauntlet_loop.py run_timeout` computes it from `loop.json` and passes it as `maxduration`.

## Sources
AGENTS.md asks for the source of each technique. These are the sources adapted, and what was taken from each.
- Epic, "Gauntlet Automation Framework Overview", "Running Gauntlet Tests" and "Gauntlet Controller" (dev.epicgames.com): the C# test node, `RunUnreal` with `-test=Node(params)`, the controller lifecycle and `EndTest`. Every public example is Win64, so Linux is a host check.
- HORU, "Gauntlet Automated Testing and Performance Metrics in UE4" (horugame.com): start the CSV capture from `OnPostMapChange` after the map loads, wait for `EndCapture`, then call `EndTest`.
- Epic, AutomatedPerfTesting plugin API (`UAutomatedPerfTestControllerBase` and the Sequence test) and its forum threads: the idea of a fixed flythrough per map with CSV and screenshots. It is adapted as a driven route. The plugin itself is not used, because it is experimental and needs BuildGraph and Level Sequence assets.
- Intel, "Unreal Engine Optimization Guide: Profiling Fundamentals": the CSV profiler flags and the 33.33 ms budget for 30 FPS.
- Epic, "Scalability Reference" and the Device Profiles documentation: the `sg.*` groups and the profile section format.
- Epic, "Lumen Performance Guide", "Virtual Shadow Maps" and "Temporal Super Resolution": the cost of each knob step, the TSR 50% floor, and why the TSR flicker cap must match the frame cap (it stays at 60 here).
- W3C, "Identifiers for WebRTC's Statistics API": the freeze definition, jitter buffer per frame and round-trip time.
- Epic, PixelStreamingInfrastructure UE5.8 frontend (`LatencyCalculator`, the installed library in `Browser/node_modules`): the `latencyCalculated` delay fields.
- Wang, Bovik, Sheikh and Simoncelli, "Image Quality Assessment: From Error Visibility to Structural Similarity" (IEEE TIP, 2004): SSIM and its constants. Andersson et al., "FLIP: A Difference Evaluator for Alternating Images" (NVIDIA, 2020): the optional report-only map.
- "LUT-Opt: Real-Time Rendering Parameter Optimization" (arXiv 2604.25178): pick the best SSIM against a maximum-quality render within a time budget, with additive costs.
- NVIDIA's Optimal Playable Settings, as described by TechRadar: scripted runs through the heaviest areas against a target frame rate.
- In-repo: `docs/gauntlet/fullswing-performance.md` (evidence format, "unverified, not pass"), the police road follower in `QuebecPolice.cpp`, `Scripts/profile_package.py` (CSV parse, warm-up discard, percentile), `Browser/stream-sample.mjs` and `Scripts/test_race_drive.py` (input disabled before driving).

## Host checks to settle first
- Do RunUnreal and the Gauntlet plugin work on Linux? If not, use `gauntlet_loop.py native --direct`.
- Does UBT build `QuebecGauntletTest` from `Quebec.uproject` in Development, and does Shipping build without it and without the Gauntlet plugin?
- Does `-DeviceProfile=` take effect in the Development Browser package, and does `GameUserSettings.ini` override it?
- Do the CSV columns include GPUTime with `-csvGpuStats`?
